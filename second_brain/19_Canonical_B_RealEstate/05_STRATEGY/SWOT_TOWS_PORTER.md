# SWOT, TOWS, and Five Forces

## SWOT

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Existing Dproperty operating context; integrated workflow vision; modular commercial offers; detailed technical baseline; three scenario models; founder domain insight | No verified PMF/retention; broad scope; founder dependency; conflicting legacy assumptions; unproven neutral trust; limited signed evidence; complex legal footprint |
| Opportunities | Threats |
| Independent agencies need operational discipline; developer distribution is fragmented; configurable SaaS can cross borders; training can lower activation cost; transaction graph can support network products | Horizontal CRM/ERP incumbents; local brokers/portals; vendor/platform dependence; privacy/AML/franchise exposure; marketplace leakage; support-heavy economics; weak capital markets; brand confusion |

## TOWS actions

- **S–O:** use Dproperty as a controlled test tenant; productize only repeated workflows; turn manuals into versioned templates; use Building Blocks to reduce implementation hours.
- **S–T:** legally separate platform/brokerage; publish source-of-truth boundaries; design adapters; make franchise funds restricted; instrument support and attribution.
- **W–O:** recruit external non-Dproperty design partners; price founding cohorts; collect actual onboarding and retention data; narrow one developer corridor.
- **W–T:** stage capital; freeze expansion when gates fail; no custom forks; no regulated VAULTED activity; preserve six-month pivot reserve.

## Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Intensity | Why | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | High | Many CRMs, broker tools, portals, spreadsheets and local systems | Own post-qualification transaction depth and audit, integrate rather than replace everything |
| Buyer power | High initially | Small agencies can churn, delay and request customization | ICP discipline, measurable ROI, annual terms after proof, standardized onboarding |
| Supplier power | Medium–high | Cloud, AI, GHL, LMS, e-signature and messaging providers | Abstraction, pass-through usage, export, multi-provider options for critical paths |
| New entrants | Medium | AI lowers interface/build cost; workflow/legal depth remains hard | Templates, transaction data, implementation learning, trust and partner distribution |
| Substitutes | High | Spreadsheets, shared drives, chat, assistants and manual coordinators | Prove fewer errors, faster cycles, visibility and auditable economics—not feature parity |

## Strategic conclusion

The defensible wedge is not “all-in-one real estate.” It is becoming the trusted system of record for complex transactions while integrating the systems customers already use. Neutrality, implementation economics and evidence—not breadth—determine whether the company earns platform status.
