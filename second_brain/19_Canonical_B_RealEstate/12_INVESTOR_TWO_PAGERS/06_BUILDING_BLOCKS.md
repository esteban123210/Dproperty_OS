# Building Blocks — Investor Two-Pager

## 1. The idea in plain English

Building Blocks is B_RealEstate's learning, onboarding, and certification product. It converts operating standards into role-based courses and records who was assigned, who completed, who passed, when certification expires, and whether a person is ready for a responsibility or workflow.

The product should run on an external LMS such as Open edX. BluePrint displays the relevant completion and certification status and can use it to gate activation or privileges. Building Blocks is not a daily document repository, a custom LMS, or an unaccredited promise of formal professional qualification.

## 2. Customer, problem, and value

**Customers:** franchise and partner teams, independent agencies, agents and coordinators, managers, developers, and selected external professionals.

**Problem:** real-estate training is often generic, disconnected from actual operations, difficult to verify, and quickly outdated. Managers cannot tell whether someone is ready to execute a transaction correctly.

**Value proposition:** faster onboarding, consistent execution, evidence of competence, current product/process training, and a direct link between learning and operational behavior.

The real test is not course completion. It is whether trained users activate faster, make fewer workflow errors, and produce better transaction outcomes.

## 3. Product architecture

| Layer | Offer | Commercial role |
|---|---|---|
| Core onboarding | Brand, BluePrint/CRM workflow, qualification, diligence, documents, compliance, close and commission | Included for defined franchise users |
| Specialist courses | Investment, project sales, compliance operations, leadership, advanced product | Paid enrollment |
| Certification | Assessment, evidence, expiry/renewal, role or inventory gate | Enrollment or renewal fee |
| Developer academy | Project-specific training, branded cohort, manager dashboard | Setup + program/license |
| Events/coaching | Live masterclasses and executive support | Separately priced |

The LMS owns curriculum and detailed learning activity. BluePrint owns organization/role context, required-course policy, activation gates, and synchronized certification evidence.

Free franchise onboarding is not counted as education revenue; its cost belongs in franchise delivery economics. Developer cohorts, certifications, and events require separate cost and pricing evidence. Content production must be amortized over realistic enrollment, not treated as free founder labor.

The product's strategic economics may matter more than direct course margin. If Building Blocks reduces implementation time, support tickets, compliance errors, and churn, it increases BluePrint and franchise lifetime value. Those effects must be measured rather than assumed.

## 6. Competitive landscape

Keller Williams University and RE/MAX University provide deep franchise-linked training libraries. Inman, coaches, associations, Coursera, and Udemy offer broad professional education. Developers and brokerages also run internal webinars and project briefings.

Building Blocks cannot win through content volume. Its wedge is operational specificity: courses tied to the exact B_ workflow, versioned standards, assessed competence, project eligibility, and measurable changes in BluePrint behavior.

## 7. Defensibility and risks

Course videos are easy to copy and quickly become stale. Defensibility is more plausible in the curriculum-to-workflow graph: role requirements, assessments, certification evidence, operating results, and a feedback loop from transaction errors to updated learning.

Main risks: low completion; weak willingness to pay; instructor dependence; high content-production cost; unrecognized certifications; stale legal content; and confusing training activity with business results.

## 8. What must be proven

- Six core modules and two paid specialist courses delivered without building a custom LMS.
- One paid external cohort with completion above 60%.
- Measurable improvement in activation time or workflow error rate.
- Positive gross contribution after content, instructor, platform, support, and acquisition costs.
- Repeat demand, renewals, or certification value.
- Clear naming decision: Building Blocks as the customer-facing brand; B_Academy retained only as a legacy alias if needed.

## Investor conclusion

**Fund as enablement infrastructure with disciplined monetization.** Building Blocks can improve adoption, standards, and retention across the ecosystem. It should not be valued as the core company unless external paid demand and repeatable education economics become material.
