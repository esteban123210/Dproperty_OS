## How to read this memorandum

This document is designed for an investor who assumes management is wrong until evidence proves otherwise. It uses four evidence labels:

- **[F] Fact:** supported by a primary document, executed agreement, accounting record or reproducible operating data.
- **[A] Assumption:** a numerical or operating input used to build a model; it must have an owner and sensitivity range.
- **[H] Hypothesis:** a claim that requires a defined customer or market experiment.
- **[T] Target:** an outcome management intends to achieve; it is not a forecast or current fact.

Unless explicitly labeled **[F]**, forward-looking customer counts, revenue, GMV, margins and valuations are management scenarios. No investor should rely on them without the underlying model, contracts, bank records, cohort exports and legal diligence.

## Innovation method: Borrowing Brilliance without imitation

David Kord Murray's six-step process is used as an operating discipline: define the problem, borrow from analogous solutions, combine the useful mechanisms, allow the combination to incubate through real use, judge strengths and weaknesses, and enhance the solution by removing weak elements. The six steps are summarized by [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/video/borrowing-brilliance-the-six-steps-to-business-innovation-by-building-on-the-ideas-of-others/) and the book's publication details are documented by [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Borrowing_Brilliance.html?id=SO68PAAACAAJ).

Austin Kleon's *Steal Like an Artist* reinforces the behavioral rule: study multiple influences, understand why they work, transform them through B_'s own context, share the work and subtract aggressively. The company is not authorized to copy proprietary code, confidential processes, brand expressions, protected content or contract language. Kleon's official description and ten principles are available on the [author's site](https://austinkleon.com/steal/).

### Step 1 — Define

The problem is not “real estate needs another CRM.” The problem is:

> Independent real-estate firms cannot reliably coordinate the operating, documentary, compliance and financial truth of a transaction across people and organizations without expensive manual management.

This definition excludes lead-generation software, public portals, accounting ledgers and generic productivity tools. It gives B_ a specific job, buyer and measurable outcome.

### Step 2 — Borrow

The company studies mechanisms—not appearances—from businesses that solved analogous problems.

| Reference model | Mechanism worth borrowing | What B_ must not copy or assume |
|---|---|---|
| AppFolio | Vertical system of record plus workflow and value-added transaction services | Property-management product scope, US economics or mature scale |
| Toast | Land with mission-critical vertical software; monetize transaction activity after workflow adoption | Payments economics, restaurant GTM or regulated capabilities B_ does not possess |
| ServiceTitan | Deep vertical workflow, operational benchmarking and an expanding platform around a fragmented trade | Enterprise scope before B_ earns PMF |
| Salesforce ecosystem | Clear core record, permissions, APIs and partner extensions | Horizontal breadth and administrative complexity |
| Franchise systems | Controlled deployment, operating standards, training and local ownership | Treating franchise sales as proof of software PMF |
| Private capital markets | Qualification, controlled disclosure, permissions and attributable introductions | Securities activity, custody, advice or regulatory status |
| Incident/quality systems | Convert a failure into root cause, corrective action and recurrence monitoring | Burdensome compliance theatre that users bypass |
| Professional certification | Competence evidence that unlocks economic access | Pay-to-play badges without practical assessment |

AppFolio is the strongest public strategic analogue, not a direct comparable. Its 2025 filing describes a unified system of record, action and growth and reports that value-added services materially exceed subscription revenue; this validates a pattern, not B_'s projections. See [AppFolio's 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1433195/000143319526000011/appf-20251231.htm).

### Step 3 — Combine

B_'s proposed combination is:

> vertical transaction operating system + controlled franchise proving ground + certification/implementation network + permissioned private opportunity exchange.

The combination matters only if the components share one canonical identity, permissions model, transaction record, event taxonomy and economic ledger. A bundle of disconnected subscriptions is not innovation and creates no moat.

### Step 4 — Incubate

Incubation happens inside real Dproperty transactions and external design partners—not in presentations. During the first 90 days, management must keep a dated learning log: observation, borrowed mechanism, adaptation, result, decision and new test. No borrowed feature enters the roadmap without a customer problem and acceptance metric.

### Step 5 — Judge

Every quarter, the board scores each borrowed mechanism against five questions:

1. Does it improve a customer outcome?
2. Does it increase recurring usage or retention?
3. Does it improve distribution or network liquidity?
4. Does it scale without proportional headcount?
5. Does it strengthen trusted proprietary data or switching value?

Anything scoring below 15/25 for two quarters is removed, redesigned or returned to manual operation.

### Step 6 — Enhance

Enhancement means subtraction first. B_ removes copied complexity, localizes the remaining mechanism to the selected corridor, adds explicit trust/data controls and instruments the result. The enhancement loop is complete only when a cohort metric improves.

### Originality and intellectual-property rule

B_ will maintain an **influence register** recording the public source, mechanism studied, internal adaptation and IP/legal review. Employees and vendors warrant that deliverables are original or properly licensed. Competitive research may use public products, filings, pricing, documentation and customer interviews; it may not use stolen credentials, confidential material, deceptive access or copied source code.

## 1. Executive decision

### The company

**B_RealEstate is the operating and transaction infrastructure for independent real-estate businesses, beginning with investment-focused agencies in Latin America and the cross-border corridors that serve them.**

It is not a collection of five startups. It is one platform with three layers:

1. **Operate:** BluePrint is the management system of record for verified financial health, operating controls, policies, process intelligence and management interventions.
2. **Standardize and distribute:** Dproperty/B_Franchising, BlankCRM and Academy create the first controlled deployments, customer acquisition, consistent behavior and proof.
3. **Connect and transact:** VAULTED adds verified private opportunities, matching, attribution and transaction economics once the network has sufficient supply and demand.

### The sharp initial wedge

The first ideal customer is an owner-led, investment/new-development real-estate agency with roughly 5–50 people, active transaction volume, fragmented back-office operations and a CRM that does not provide reliable operational or financial truth.

The urgent job is not “get more leads.” It is:

> Know what is happening across every transaction, document, compliance step, commission and operating failure without making the founder the human reconciliation layer.

## 2. The problem

Many real-estate agencies are founded by effective sellers. Their front office develops faster than their operating infrastructure. Leads, messages and follow-ups may live in a CRM, while the rest of the company remains fragmented across WhatsApp, drives, spreadsheets, accounting software, individual memory and manual reconciliation.

The resulting failures are expensive:

- the owner cannot see one reliable version of transaction or cash reality;
- documents, approvals and compliance evidence are incomplete or outdated;
- commission rules and expected payments are unclear;
- tasks move through people rather than an auditable workflow;
- operating mistakes recur because they are treated as isolated incidents;
- staff spend time compiling reports instead of improving outcomes;
- growth requires management hires before the business can comfortably afford them;
- cross-border referrals and private inventory depend on relationships without reliable attribution.

Generic CRMs optimize selling activity. Generic project tools record tasks. Accounting tools record booked financial events. They do not jointly own the qualified-opportunity-to-commission operating spine for a multi-party real-estate transaction.

### The economic pain hypothesis

BluePrint is valuable when it reduces or prevents measurable costs:

- founder/manager reconciliation hours;
- delayed or lost commission collection;
- transaction fall-through attributable to process failures;
- compliance/document exceptions;
- duplicate work and reporting effort;
- recurring operational incidents;
- premature back-office hiring;
- leakage or disputes in partner-originated transactions.

Every pilot must establish a baseline for these items. “Users like the interface” is not proof of value.

## 3. Solution architecture

### 3.1 One universe, clear boundaries

| Layer | Owns | Does not own |
|---|---|---|
| BluePrint | Evidence-backed financial health, expected vs actual cash, expenses, budgets and variance; KPI and CRM-usage oversight; process/Glitch monitoring; policies, management interventions, audit and AI executive support | Lead generation, mass marketing automation, full accounting ledger, payroll, escrow or autonomous legal decisions |
| BlankCRM | Full commercial execution from lead capture through legal, approvals, contracts, payment milestones, closing, commissions and post-sale; communications, automation and commercial dashboards through GoHighLevel | BluePrint management verification, company budgets/expenses and governance; proprietary CRM engineering |
| Academy | Curricula, assignments, completion, certification, readiness and continuing standards | Building a new LMS; becoming a generic course marketplace |
| VAULTED | Verified opportunity, access policy, NDA, interest, introductions, client attribution and marketplace transaction history | Public MLS/portal, custody, escrow, investment advice or indiscriminate listing volume |
| B_Franchising | Brand license, territory/market relationship, operating playbook, launch support, QA and full-stack adoption | Being the only route to use B_ software |
| Dproperty | Flagship brokerage/franchise brand, live operating laboratory, developer relationships, transaction proof and initial liquidity | Privileged access to independent customers' private data |

### 3.2 Two independent, connected workflows

BlankCRM executes lead capture → qualification → legal/document review → commercial approval → contract/signature → payment milestones → closing → commission calculation/approval → post-sale. Signature, settlement and accounting providers retain their source authority. This works without BluePrint.

BluePrint reads authorized CRM lifecycle evidence plus accounting/bank actuals → verifies provenance → compares expected vs actual cash → classifies expenses and budget variance → detects CRM-usage or process Glitches → proposes management intervention → records human review → produces a management report. Commercial remediation is performed in the CRM, not by BluePrint.

The management model supports BlankCRM, other CRMs and permissioned manual imports without requiring a sales-process migration.

### 3.3 BluePrint's initial modules

**Required for the first paid product**

- organization, roles, entitlements, tenant isolation and audit;
- evidence intake, external source IDs, timestamps and verification state;
- expected cash, actual receipts, expenses, budgets, forecasts and variance;
- KPI definitions and CRM-usage oversight;
- processes, Glitches, policies and management interventions;
- management reports, period close and immutable provenance;
- read-only GHL connector plus manual/CSV fallback and reconciliation exceptions;
- permission-aware CEO/CFO/COO/CMO support with citations [T].

**Explicitly postponed**

- native mobile applications;
- full accounting or money movement;
- proprietary CRM;
- public property portal;
- broad MLS aggregation;
- end-user no-code workflow builder;
- many CRM/accounting/bank integrations;
- autonomous compliance, signatures, payments or investment recommendations;
- country-specific legal content without counsel.

### 3.4 Copilot doctrine

Copilot is an interface and productivity layer, not the moat. It can search, explain, summarize, draft and propose limited reversible actions. It cannot approve compliance, sign, pay, alter commission entitlements or provide unsupervised regulated advice.

All answers must obey the user's underlying permissions, cite the records used, distinguish missing data from negative facts and create an audit event for material actions.

## 4. The customer and value proposition

### 4.1 Beachhead ICP

**Investment/new-development agency principal**

- 5–50 team members;
- 10+ active transactions or material monthly commission pipeline;
- uses WhatsApp plus CRM but reconciles critical work manually;
- operates in one or more currencies or markets;
- sells developer inventory or coordinates cross-border buyers;
- personally intervenes in reporting, documents or commission disputes;

- sufficient pain and volume to implement, but not an enterprise procurement cycle.

### 4.2 Buyer/user map

|---|---|---|---|
| Agency owner | Reliable company truth; lower control burden | Fears implementation and data exposure | 30-day proof, transparent data isolation, ROI report |
| Operations lead | One queue; fewer follow-ups and exceptions | Tool may add data entry | CRM intake, templates, automation and clear ownership |
| Transaction coordinator | Complete files and predictable next actions | Rigid workflow | Explain blockers; controlled exceptions |

### 4.3 Product promise

> BluePrint gives a fragmented agency evidence-backed financial health, management governance and process intelligence without replacing its CRM or executing its sales process.

### 4.4 Measurable customer outcomes

The first six months must quantify:

- owner/manager hours saved per week;
- percentage of active transactions with complete next-step ownership;
- document/compliance exception rate;
- reporting preparation time;
- commission discrepancies discovered or avoided;
- stage-cycle time and stalled transactions;
- repeated operating failures;
- weekly active operational users;
- time to first transaction completed through the full workflow.

### 4.5 Customer discovery evidence protocol

Management may not convert interview enthusiasm into revenue probability. Each interview record must contain respondent role, organization profile, existing stack, frequency and monetary consequence of the problem, current workaround, procurement authority, implementation concerns, reaction to price and permission to follow up. A valid willingness-to-pay signal is one of: payment, signed order, approved budget, procurement step or a clearly documented rejection at a specific price.

The initial discovery set must include:

- at least 20 external agency principals across Panama and one adjacent/cross-border market;
- at least 10 operations/transaction coordinators;
- at least five developers or inventory providers;
- at least 10 broker/agency demand-side participants for VAULTED;
- explicit interviews with agencies that view Dproperty as a competitor.

Selection must include negative cases. Referrals from friendly partners alone create unusable evidence.

## 5. Brand and corporate architecture

### 5.1 Recommended structure

**B_RealEstate** is the parent and neutral technology/network company. **Dproperty** is a distinct flagship customer, brokerage and franchise brand. Independent agencies contract with B_RealEstate, not with Dproperty, for software and network services.

This separation must be substantive:

- separate contracts, privacy notices and product terms;
- no Dproperty access to another tenant's data;
- independent governance over aggregated/benchmark products;
- role- and tenant-enforced technical separation;
- a clear conflict policy for opportunities involving Dproperty;
- separate product support identities where appropriate;
- published explanation of what data is private, aggregated, optional and never used competitively.

### 5.2 B_Franchising

B_Franchising is the commercial and operating system through which Dproperty markets and launches franchises. It can later host other concepts only after Dproperty's model is repeatable. It is not the software parent and should not imply that every B_ customer is a franchise prospect.

The full franchise offer may include:

- Dproperty brand and market rights;
- launch/onboarding program;
- operating manuals and QA;
- BluePrint and BlankCRM entitlements;
- Academy assignments/certification;
- developer and Dproperty Select/VAULTED access subject to policy;
- reporting, advisory and operating support;
- network buying power and referrals.

The franchise premium must come from the brand, territory, commercial network, hands-on support and proven operating model—not from locking basic software behind the franchise.

### 5.3 Franchise economics retained for planning

Current policy assumptions:

- launch fee: **$30,000**, planned increase to **$40,000** after the first five franchises;
- royalty: **6% of gross commission income (GCI)**;
- network fee: **1.5% of GCI**;
- minimum monthly floor from month 7: **$750**;
- developer program royalty: **10% of the developer team commission**;
- Private Collection indicative payout: **2.5% branded / 2.0% white-label**, subject to precise deal definitions.

These terms require legal review by jurisdiction and an economic test against franchisee contribution margin. Software value included in franchise fees must still be assigned an internal transfer price so SaaS economics are not obscured.

## 7. VAULTED marketplace design

### 7.1 First wedge

**Hypothesis:** verified Panama new-development, last-unit and selected assignment opportunities distributed to a curated network of investment-focused agencies/brokers serving LATAM and European buyers.

This wedge uses Dproperty's reachable supply and existing cross-border ambition. It is narrow enough to operate manually and valuable enough to justify careful attribution.

### 7.2 Minimum transaction loop

1. Developer or approved seller submits an opportunity and current commercial terms.
2. B_ verifies identity, authority, availability, key documents, terms and access policy.
3. Eligible agencies accept terms/NDA and register a client or request an introduction.
4. VAULTED timestamps access, interest and attribution.
5. A qualified deal becomes a BlankCRM/chosen-CRM transaction workspace.
6. Milestones and evidence establish whether a transaction is attributable.
7. Closing and commission evidence confirm GMV and economics.
8. Performance history updates verified dimensions—not a simplistic public star score.

### 7.3 Cold-start rules

- Invitation-only initially.
- One corridor, one asset class and one primary transaction pattern.
- Optimize for completed attributable GMV, not listing count.
- Manually approve supply and resolve disputes before automating policy.
- Do not charge both sides indiscriminately.
- Do not promise exclusivity without enforceable inventory and terms.
- Kill or redesign the wedge if 50 qualified introductions fail to produce sufficient progressing transactions.

### 7.4 Network north star

**Quarterly completed attributable GMV**, accompanied by:

- monthly transacting organizations;
- qualified introduction-to-close rate;
- median time to first qualified interest;
- repeat transacting rate;
- leakage/dispute rate;
- gross revenue and contribution margin per completed transaction.

No Dproperty commercial team receives independent-agency data. Support access is reasoned, time-bound and audited. Customer exports and deletion/retention processes are product requirements, not future legal housekeeping.

### 9.3 The moat

The credible moat is a combination of:

- embedded transaction and operating workflows;
- clean, permissioned data on how transactions progress and fail;
- verified commission and closing outcomes;
- developer supply and agency demand relationships;
- trusted attribution across organizations;
- certification tied to real operating capability;
- accumulated configuration and benchmark insight by market;
- switching costs created by value and history, not data hostage-taking.

Software features alone are copyable. Proprietary data alone is not defensible if customers do not trust its use. The moat forms only when workflow adoption, trusted governance and network transactions reinforce one another.

## 10. Business model

### 10.0 Market sizing that an investor can audit

The current plan does **not** claim a final TAM because the verified count of target agencies by country and validated ACV do not yet exist. Presenting a consulting-report headline would be false precision. Management must produce a bottom-up market workbook before circulation to investors.

#### Software market formula

For each country and ICP tier:

> legally operating target organizations × reachable percentage × validated annual contract value.

The organization count must cite a regulator, registry, association or reproducible database; duplicates, inactive firms and solo brokers outside the ICP must be removed. ACV must come from paid contracts or documented price tests.

#### VAULTED market formula

For each approved corridor and transaction type:

> annual eligible transaction value × realistically attributable share × realized net take rate.

“Global real-estate value” is not TAM. Inventory B_ cannot verify, demand B_ cannot reach, transactions B_ cannot attribute and economics B_ cannot legally earn are excluded.

#### Franchise market formula

The model must constrain openings by onboarding capacity, franchisee capital, operator quality, territory cannibalization and local franchise law.

## 13. Roadmap and gates

### Gate 1 — Problem proof (days 0–30)

Pass only if:

- 15+ agency-owner interviews confirm the pain and current workaround;
- five provide real workflow/data samples;
- three sign design-partner commitments;
- quantified baseline exists for at least two customer outcomes.

### Gate 2 — Solution proof (days 31–90)

Pass only if:

- real users process real transactions through the prototype/concierge workflow;
- at least three external pilots pay or sign enforceable paid conversion terms;

### Gate 3 — Retention proof (months 4–9)

- 10+ paying organizations, including five non-Dproperty;

Pass only if:

- 40+ paying organizations;
- non-Dproperty customers represent at least half of new SaaS ARR;

- no material tenant/privacy incident;
- VAULTED demonstrates attributable completed transactions.

### Gate 5 — Scale (months 19–36)

Pass only if:

- 150–500 paying organizations depending on ACV;
- net revenue retention above 110%;
- software gross margin trending above 75%;
- second market grows through a repeatable channel;
- marketplace repeat transaction rate and contribution margin improve;
- leadership is no longer founder-bottlenecked.

## 14. Red-team scrutiny

### Flag 1 — “This is five startups.”

**Valid.** Resolved through the hierarchy: build BluePrint; use third parties for CRM/LMS; operate VAULTED manually; scale franchises selectively. Any roadmap with simultaneous full builds fails investment review.

### Flag 2 — “A franchise company cannot produce venture-scale software economics.”

**Valid if franchises remain the ceiling.** The company passes only if external agency SaaS adoption becomes material. By Gate 4, at least half of new SaaS ARR must come from outside Dproperty.

### Flag 3 — “Competitors will not trust software owned by a brokerage.”

**Critical.** Corporate, contractual and technical neutrality are mandatory. Five paying non-Dproperty customers and explicit trust interviews are required before claiming a broad market.

### Flag 4 — “BluePrint is an ERP with endless scope.”

**Critical.** The qualified-opportunity-to-commission spine is the boundary. No full accounting, CRM, property management, portal or no-code platform in MVP.

### Flag 5 — “The AI layer is generic.”

**True.** Copilot improves UX and productivity; it is not defensibility. Defensibility comes from workflow adoption, governed data, verified outcomes and network access.

### Flag 6 — “VAULTED will leak transactions off-platform.”

**Likely.** Attribution, controlled access, registered interest, workflow evidence and economic value must reduce—not merely prohibit—leakage. Track disputes and effective take rate. Do not scale if enforcement destroys participation.

### Flag 7 — “The marketplace has no liquidity.”

**Unproven.** Begin with one reachable supply/demand corridor and concierge execution. Listings are vanity; completed attributable GMV is proof.

### Flag 8 — “Professional services will consume the margins.”

**Likely in early cohorts.** Track implementation hours and contribution by customer. Standardize configuration packs; charge for migration; refuse custom code unless it benefits the platform.

### Flag 9 — “LATAM expansion is treated as one market.”

**Invalid assumption to avoid.** Country-by-country franchise, data, brokerage, consumer, tax, e-signature and marketplace rules require separate gates. Expand via a corridor thesis, not flags on a map.

### Flag 10 — “The company has brand confusion.”

**Current risk.** B_ is the neutral infrastructure. Dproperty is the flagship branded operator. BluePrint is the core product. Other names describe clear jobs. No new product names until the portfolio earns them.

### Flag 11 — “The market-size story is inflated.”

**Current risk.** Bottom-up TAM must be built from countable target organizations × validated annual contract value plus distinct transaction economics. Do not sum global real-estate GMV, franchise fees and SaaS markets.

### Flag 12 — “Founder ambition and financing strategy may conflict.”

**Decision required.** A venture-scale outcome may require dilution, professional executives, years of reinvestment and reduced early dividends. If control/dividends dominate, pursue the profitable base case proudly and do not pretend to run a venture strategy.

### Flag 13 — “Data moat claims create legal and trust risk.”

**Critical.** Customer data use, aggregation, AI improvement, benchmark validity and cross-border transfers need product-level consent/governance plus counsel. Trust is part of the moat.

### Flag 14 — “Franchise support capacity can break the company.”

**Likely.** Franchise sales must be throttled by onboarding and QA capacity. Poor franchisees damage Dproperty and create bad data, cases and support load.

### Flag 15 — “The economics rely on assumptions rather than cohorts.”

**True.** The scenarios are decision models. Replace every assumption with a cohort actual as pilots mature; maintain a source/owner/date for each input.

## 15. Kill criteria

The plan needs explicit reasons to stop or pivot.

### Pivot BluePrint's ICP/value proposition if, after 20 qualified interviews and five real pilots:

- owners do not rank the problem among their top three priorities;
- willingness to pay remains below the cost to implement/support;

- measurable value depends mainly on bespoke consulting.

- external agencies will not trust the platform;
- non-Dproperty SaaS adoption is immaterial;
- onboarding cannot be standardized;
- retention or gross margin cannot approach viable vertical SaaS economics.

### Pause/kill VAULTED's initial wedge if:

- verified supply lacks meaningful differentiation;
- 50 qualified introductions produce no viable progressing cohort;
- attribution disputes/leakage make take rate uneconomic;
- regulatory requirements exceed the value available at current scale.

Killing a weak wedge protects the company. It does not invalidate the entire mission.

## Appendix B — Evidence and benchmark note

This plan deliberately avoids using top-down market-report numbers as if they were revenue. The most relevant public benchmark is that vertical real-estate operating platforms can become large when they combine a system of record with workflow and value-added transactions. AppFolio described this architecture in its 2025 Form 10-K and reported 22,096 property-management customers, 9.4 million units under management and $950.8 million revenue, of which $721.5 million came from value-added services. This is not a direct comparable—AppFolio serves property management primarily in the United States—but it supports the strategic principle that transaction/value-added layers can become larger than base subscriptions once the core operating system earns adoption and trust.

Source: [AppFolio 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1433195/000143319526000011/appf-20251231.htm).

The correct B_ market model is bottom-up:

> reachable target organizations × validated ACV + completed attributable GMV × realized net take rate + separately modeled franchise and service revenue.

It must be rebuilt quarterly from actual cohorts.
