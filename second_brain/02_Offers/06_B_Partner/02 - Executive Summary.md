# Own-Brand / White-Label Partner — Executive Summary

## Offer

The own-brand operating partner keeps its existing agency identity while adopting the B_ operating system, selected playbooks, BluePrint, training, governance, and eligible network inventory. White-label means configurable brand, domain, emails, documents, terminology, and feature visibility on one shared codebase—not a forked application.

The value must be stronger than “software plus a royalty.” The partner should receive measurable operating improvement: shorter cycle time, fewer missing documents, better manager visibility, consistent onboarding, transaction/commission control, and access to opportunities or capabilities it could not efficiently build alone.

## Ideal customer

An existing boutique agency with credible leadership, current transaction flow, willingness to standardize, clean data rights, a defined market niche, and enough capital to implement. Poor fit: a prospect seeking a cheap CRM, a custom software fork, a territory monopoly, or a guarantee of developer inventory.

## Contract and operating boundaries

- Partner owns its customer relationship and brand subject to agreed data/processing roles.
- B_ owns platform IP, global schemas, standard templates, and aggregated benchmarks only where permitted.
- Partner data is tenant-isolated. Any anonymized/aggregated use requires policy and contractual authority.
- Configuration does not grant source code, dedicated infrastructure, or indefinite custom support.
- Termination covers data export, retention/deletion, de-branding, outstanding fees, and ongoing transactions.

## Strategic risk

The own-brand offer has a weaker brand rationale than Dproperty. Its fee and royalty are investable only if B_ proves operating ROI and distribution value. If pilots view it as ordinary SaaS, convert the offer to software/implementation pricing rather than defending a franchise label.
