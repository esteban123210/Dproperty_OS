# White-Label / Own-Brand Partner — Investor Two-Pager

## 1. The idea in plain English

The white-label partner keeps its existing agency name and customer relationships while adopting the B_RealEstate operating system behind the scenes. It receives configurable BluePrint workflows, selected playbooks, training, governance, reporting, integration support, and eligible network supply without becoming Dproperty.

“White-label” means configurable branding, documents, domain, terminology, and feature visibility on a shared codebase. It does not mean a custom software fork, transfer of source code, dedicated infrastructure by default, or unlimited custom support.

## 2. Customer, problem, and value

**Buyer:** an established boutique agency with current transaction volume, a credible local brand, clean data rights, and leadership willing to standardize.

**Problem:** the agency wants stronger operations and technology but does not want to surrender its brand or spend years building an internal back office.

**Value proposition:** preserve local identity while gaining a structured operating layer, measurable workflows, training, transaction and commission control, and access to selected network capabilities.

The partner should buy because the model improves profit, risk control, and management visibility—not because it is described as a “franchise.”

## 3. Offer boundaries

The partner receives implementation, shared platform configuration, standard operating playbooks, defined training, governance, data isolation, reporting, and support. The partner owns its customer relationship and local brand; B_ owns the platform IP, global schemas, and standard content. Cross-tenant access is prohibited by default, and benchmark use requires contractual authority and aggregation safeguards.

Termination must specify export, retention/deletion, de-branding, open-transaction handling, outstanding fees, and access revocation.

## 6. Competitive landscape

The closest strategic reference is Side, which powers real-estate companies behind their own brands with technology, brokerage operations, transaction support, and services. Software-only alternatives include combinations of CRM, transaction management, accounting, consultants, and outsourced transaction coordinators. The cheapest competitor is the agency's existing stack of spreadsheets, WhatsApp, and trusted staff.

B_'s potential distinction is a lighter, LATAM/cross-border-oriented operating model connected to BluePrint, developer supply, training, and transaction evidence. However, if partners only value the software, a royalty becomes difficult to defend.

## 7. Defensibility and risks

Defensibility could come from repeatable operating outcomes, integration/configuration packs, benchmark data, workflow templates, and cross-network supply. The partner's own brand reduces B_'s consumer-brand leverage, so operational ROI must carry the offer.

Main risks: model confusion; franchise-law exposure; weak willingness to pay a royalty; heavy customization; data-neutrality concerns; support cost; partner churn; and partners copying processes after implementation.

## 8. What must be proven

- Three paid partners with different starting stacks but the same core implementation.
- Implementation below 40 hours initially and trending below 20.
- Quantified reduction in cycle time, errors, or management labor.
- Partner gross profit improvement greater than all fees.
- Renewal and software usage after the free period.
- A/B commercial test: royalty model versus setup + SaaS model.

## Investor conclusion

**Invest as an experiment, not as settled franchising.** This model can widen distribution faster than Dproperty because it preserves local brands. If operational ROI is not strong enough to support 4% of GCI, convert it to transparent implementation and SaaS pricing rather than forcing the franchise label.
