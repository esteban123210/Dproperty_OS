# Dproperty Franchise — Investor Two-Pager

## 1. The idea in plain English

Dproperty Franchise lets a qualified local operator open a premium real-estate brokerage under the Dproperty brand while using B_RealEstate's operating system. The franchisee receives the brand, launch playbook, BlankCRM/chosen-CRM transaction workflows, optional BlankCRM, mandatory Building Blocks onboarding, approved templates, reporting, operating standards, and eligible curated inventory/network access.

The offer is not a passive investment, a guaranteed territory forever, a lead guarantee, or a promise of returns. The local operator remains responsible for licenses, staff and agents, client acquisition, premises, local marketing, taxes, compliance, and service quality.

## 2. Customer, problem, and value

**Buyer:** an owner-operator or established small brokerage with local credibility, sales ability, and enough capital to operate through ramp-up.

**Problem:** many independent agencies are founder-dependent, run transactions through WhatsApp and spreadsheets, lack consistent diligence and reporting, and have limited access to trusted cross-border or developer inventory.

**Value proposition:** launch faster with a differentiated investment-property brand and a complete operating model instead of assembling brand, process, software, training, templates, and supply relationships separately.

The promise must be measured as better economics and control: faster launch, more qualified opportunities, fewer missing documents, shorter cycle time, improved conversion, accurate commissions, and better owner visibility.

## 3. What the franchise includes—and does not

Included core: Dproperty brand license; launch and activation checklist; BluePrint Core for 12 months within defined limits; transaction, compliance, document, closing, and commission workflows executed in BlankCRM/chosen CRM;  essential Building Blocks onboarding; reporting standards; test transaction; approved templates; and defined HQ support.

Separately charged or locally funded: office build-out, employees and agents, brokerage licenses, local legal/tax work, local advertising, travel, complex migration, custom development, premium courses, excess AI/messaging use, extra offices, and optional BlankCRM.

At the current mature-office scenario—$360,000 collected annual GCI, 36 closes, $198,000 agent payouts, and $72,000 local fixed cost/owner salary—the franchisee is modeled to earn **$58,224 annual operating profit, or 16.2% of GCI**. Modeled fee-plus-setup cash payback is **37 months**. The office needs approximately **$92,632 additional annual GCI**, or **9.3 additional modeled closings**, versus remaining independent to cover incremental franchise costs.

For HQ, a mature office at $360,000 GCI produces approximately **$21,600 annual royalty** plus approximately **$3,600 annual BluePrint revenue after the free year**, before direct support and platform costs. The 1% fund is restricted, not operating profit. Entry-fee margin cannot be claimed until all launch labor, travel, sales commission, legal, and support costs are measured.

## 6. Competitive landscape

RE/MAX and Keller Williams offer mature global brands, systems, training, technology, and referral networks. Premium networks and strong local brokerages compete on reputation, luxury positioning, relationships, and inventory. Remaining independent competes on low fees and maximum autonomy.

Dproperty cannot win on brand scale today. Its plausible wedge is narrower: curated investment/new-development transactions, cross-border client care, BluePrint-powered operational control, and a high-acceptance standard for supply and operators. That positioning is only defensible if it produces better office economics and client outcomes.

## 7. Defensibility and risks

Potential defensibility comes from a repeatable launch system, transaction data, country-specific operating packs, curated developer relationships, verified performance benchmarks, and a trusted investment-oriented brand. The logo alone is not a moat.

Main risks: weak brand premium; poor operator selection; support overload; inconsistent client service; regulatory variance; territory conflict; dependence on entry fees; and franchisee resistance if fees exceed measurable incremental value.

## 8. What must be proven

- Two profitable reference offices with 12 months of clean cohort data.
- Franchisee payback acceptable under downside, not only base case.
- Launch delivered within budget and target implementation hours.
- Royalty collection, software use, and standards compliance without founder intervention.
- Trademark, territory, franchise, brokerage, labor, privacy, tax, and consumer-law clearance per country.
- Positive HQ contribution after fully loaded support and product costs.

## Investor conclusion

**Conditional invest.** Dproperty can become a credible, cash-generating distribution channel, but it is not yet proven enough to underwrite as a scalable franchise platform. Invest in controlled pilots and evidence, not a rapid country-count story.
