> **Boundary in one line:** BluePrint owns the management verification record, policies and oversight; the CRM owns deal execution and commercial records.

# BluePrint Product Constitution

## Product promise

BluePrint gives a sales-led real-estate agency **management leverage**: a founder should be able to operate a professional back office with a small administrative team instead of building an expensive internal management structure.

The product promise is not “more software.” It is:

> **Run the agency with institutional discipline without requiring institutional headcount.**

## Core customer

Small and growing real-estate agencies, initially about **5–50 people**, where founders/principals are strong sellers but lack the time, appetite or management expertise to run finance oversight, process control, reporting, documentation and internal follow-up at a professional standard.

## Primary operator

A capable administrator/secretary/coordinator. The interface must let this person execute sophisticated back-office work without being an ERP specialist, accountant, process engineer or COO.

## Executive user

Owner/CEO/principal. BluePrint must answer: what is actually happening, what is verified, what is off-plan, what needs attention, what decision is required, and why.

## What BluePrint owns

**Management evidence and commercial oversight:**
- sourced observations of CRM lifecycle events, approvals and commercial outcomes;
- independent verification status, provenance and reconciliation exceptions;
- expected vs actual cash, expenses, budgets, variance and commission-liability oversight;
- policies, process/Glitch monitoring, KPI and CRM-usage oversight;
- management recommendations, decisions, audit and period close.

Commercial execution remains in BlankCRM/chosen CRM, including legal work, approvals, contracts, payment milestones, closing, commissions and post-sale. BluePrint may hold evidence references and management interpretations, never a second writable commercial master.

## What BluePrint coordinates but does not replace

- CRM/front office;
- accounting/general ledger;
- bank/payment platforms;
- document storage/legal archive;
- e-signature;
- LMS;
- VAULTED marketplace.

## Explicit non-goals

Do not build native:
- CRM, lead nurturing, funnels or marketing campaigns;
- property/project/unit **inventory**, listing management or MLS;
- the full commercial lifecycle;
- escrow/custody, money movement or FX;
- full accounting ledger, tax, payroll or statutory filing;
- generic ERP;
- property management;
- sales-agent task manager;
- marketplace inventory;
- legal advice or autonomous regulated decisions.

## Boundary principle

BluePrint owns management verification and oversight. The CRM owns commercial execution. The transaction record exists because it is the atom of management truth for a brokerage — commissions, compliance and reporting all resolve to it. Inventory, listings and MLS belong to CRM, portal, developer systems or VAULTED.

## Core product principle

**Human language in; structured, validated, auditable company records out.**

Chat is the interface, never the database.

## Trust principle

BluePrint never treats an agent-entered CRM field as equivalent to verified corporate truth. Every important datum carries source, time, verification state and evidence/approval metadata.

## Management principle

BluePrint should automate information processing, coordination, control and reporting work traditionally performed by management. It does not claim to replace executive judgment, legal accountability, hiring/firing, negotiation or strategic leadership.

## Standardization principle

BluePrint is opinionated. Customers should not need a months-long ERP implementation. The product ships with a real-estate-agency operating model, standard management rhythms, baseline KPIs, incident taxonomy, approval patterns and process templates that can be configured without rebuilding the system.
