# BluePrint

## Product definition

> **BluePrint is an agentic back office. It gives a small, sales-led company a CEO, CFO, COO and CMO in one place — without the cost of hiring an actual C-suite.**

Chat-first. Everything happens in the background; the front end is a conversation.

## The problem — stated precisely

A CRM does plenty well. It standardizes pipelines, computes commission splits, attaches documents and tells the sales team what the due process is.

**But a CRM's output is only as good as the discipline of the people feeding it — and those are the same people paid on what it reports.**

So a deal gets marked Won early. Or misclassified — wrong type, wrong stage, wrong amount. And then:

- CRM revenue does not reconcile with what accounting actually processed;
- neither reconciles with real accounts receivable;
- nobody can say which number is true;
- management runs on instinct.

**Good CRM usage is itself the CRM's main dependency.** That is the gap BluePrint exists to close.

## The two rules that define the product

1. **Nothing is recorded without evidence.** A sale, purchase or payment registers only when the valid attachment exists — receipt, invoice, contract, bank proof. No attachment, no record.
2. **Agents manage, not just report.** They do not render a dashboard and wait. They detect, explain, suggest and escalate.

## The agentic C-suite

| Agent | What it does |
|---|---|
| **CFO** | Registers only evidence-backed entries. Send it an invoice and say what it is: it classifies into P&L and budget line, applies the correct accounting treatment, generates the record, and can push to the accounting system. Tracks cash, receivables and budget variance. Produces CFO-grade reporting. |
| **COO** | Runs the **Glitch Report**. Detects that *the same process step always fails*, or that *one person fails more often than the rest*. Suggests process changes, team changes, retraining. Produces operating KPIs. Consultant **and** manager. |
| **CEO** | The company view: what needs attention, what is off plan, what decision is pending, and why — on the verified position, not the reported one. |
| **CMO** | **Reads the CRM** and reports whether campaigns, funnel and sales execution match management's goals. The audit of the front office. |
| **Auditor / Consultant** | Continuous audit trail, period close, and a business that is **due-diligence ready** at any moment. |

## The Glitch Report

Adopted from the **Four Seasons** hotel glitch report: **any glitch, however small, gets reported and recorded.** The purpose is not blame — it is a measurable record of process efficiency and efficacy.

This produces a dataset no CRM has: **where the company actually breaks.** It is what lets the COO agent recommend rather than merely display, and it is why **the product becomes more valuable the longer it runs.**

Reporting a glitch must be as easy as sending a message. If it is harder than that, the dataset never accumulates and the product thesis fails.

## Manuals: what the CRM does with them vs what BluePrint does

| | CRM | BluePrint |
|---|---|---|
| Role of the manual | **A guideline** — tells the team the due process | **An instrumented process** |
| What you learn | Whether someone opened it | **Where it fails, how often, and who** |
| Output | Compliance, hopefully | Glitch → diagnosis → suggested improvement → measured outcome |

Both can hold the same manuals. Only BluePrint tells you whether they work.

## What BluePrint owns

**Verified financial truth** — evidence-backed records, classification to P&L and budget, cash, receivables, commission liability, variance, period close.

**Process intelligence** — the process and SOP registry, the Glitch Report, root causes, recurrence, corrective actions, process health.

**Management layer** — management actions, approvals, decisions, KPI definitions, management reporting, the long-term audit trail.

**Company knowledge** — governed manuals, policies, templates, contacts, institutional memory.

**The CRM audit** — reads the front office and reports alignment to management goals.

## What BluePrint does NOT own

- Leads, marketing automation and the **pre-qualification pipeline** → **BlankCRM**
- Property, project and unit **inventory**, listings, MLS
- **The general ledger of record**, tax filing, payroll → **accounting platform**
- Property management
- Course delivery → **Building Blocks**
- Marketplace listings and matching → **VAULTED**
- Escrow, custody, money movement, FX
- The sales-agent prospecting workspace

**CRM-agnostic by design.** It must work with GoHighLevel, HubSpot, Salesforce or manual intake.

## On the transaction and commission record

BluePrint holds it — but as a **CFO function, not a pipeline function.** It does not own the deal *workflow*; it owns the **evidence-backed record of what actually happened**, which is the thing the CRM cannot guarantee.

Verification hierarchy: **Reported → Operationally verified → Financially verified → Closed-period/final.**

## Who it is for

**A single operator or small company that runs mostly on its sales team, has some back-office operations, and has neither the need nor the means to hire a C-suite** — but that by using BluePrint can operate at the organizational standard of a multinational without complicating its work.

**The floor is two people, not thirty.** Size is not the qualifier. *Sales-led with no C-suite* is the qualifier.

**Persona:** a franchisee, a team of two, excellent at selling apartments. BlankCRM makes their selling easier. **BluePrint makes their back office meet the highest standard.**

## It makes the business sellable

If the operator ever wants to sell the business or the franchise, BluePrint means they hand over an **ongoing managed operation** — current financial reporting, clean accounting, documented processes and manuals, audit trail, management history.

A two-person agency with that is worth materially more than one whose records live in the founder's head and a spreadsheet.

**BluePrint is an exit-value argument, not only an efficiency argument.** It is also the strongest argument in a franchise sale: the franchisee is buying a business that will be *worth something* when they leave it.

## Commercial hypothesis

**Open question this repositioning raises:** the ICP floor moved to two people, but pricing was modelled on a ~30-person agency. Whether a two-person team supports $399/month is `[R]` unvalidated. See [[03 - Offer and Pricing]].

## Honest status

The agentic C-suite is the **product thesis, not built software.** Every capability described above is `[T]` target. What exists today is the specification.

**The adoption risk is the Glitch Report.** People do not enjoy logging their own failures, and it is the dataset everything else depends on. If glitch capture does not become habitual, the COO agent has nothing to reason over.
