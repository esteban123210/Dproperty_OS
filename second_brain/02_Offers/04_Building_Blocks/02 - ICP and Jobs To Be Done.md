# Building Blocks — ICP and Jobs To Be Done

## Buyer vs learner

These are different people, and the pricing and messaging differ accordingly.

- **Buyer** decides and pays: franchise principal, agency owner, developer sales director, or the individual professional buying their own course.
- **Learner** consumes: new agent, coordinator, administrator, manager.

The buyer does not experience the product. The **learner's completion rate** is what determines whether the buyer renews. That gap is the central design risk.

## Segment 1 — Franchise / B_ Partner team *(primary, validated demand)*

**Profile:** a newly signed franchise or own-brand partner with 3–15 people, none of whom have run this operating model before.

**Job:** *"Get my team to launch readiness in weeks, consistently, without me personally training everyone."*

**Today's alternative:** shadowing a senior person, a PDF manual, and hoping. Quality varies per office — which is precisely the franchise consistency problem.

**Proof needed:** time-to-readiness, consistency across offices, and that the principal's own hours drop.

**Commercial role:** bundled into the package at allocated standalone value.

## Segment 2 — Existing BluePrint/BlankCRM customer *(attach, plausible)*

**Profile:** an agency already using B_ software, hiring or promoting people.

**Job:** *"Someone new joined, or someone is underperforming — get them to standard without me building a course."*

**Trigger that makes this work:** BluePrint surfaces a process failure or capability gap and points at the remediation. **This is the highest-intent moment in the entire ecosystem** and the reason Building Blocks attaches rather than being an afterthought.

**Proof needed:** enrollments actually triggered by BluePrint signals, not just at onboarding.

**Commercial role:** paid expansion — the main growth lever, and the one that makes Building Blocks scale with customer headcount rather than customer count.

## Segment 3 — Developer sales organization *(enterprise cohort, plausible)*

**Profile:** a developer with an internal or broker sales force selling a specific project.

**Job:** *"Get 40 brokers selling my project correctly, fast, and let me see who is certified."*

**Distinct need:** project-specific, time-boxed, branded, with a manager dashboard — closer to campaign enablement than to an ongoing academy.

**Proof needed:** whether developers will pay for training separately from the sales-partnership fee, or expect it included. **This is a real risk:** it may simply get absorbed into the Developer Partnerships retainer.

## Segment 4 — Independent professional *(standalone, unvalidated)*

**Profile:** an agent or small-agency owner with no other B_ product, buying a course for themselves.

**Job:** *"Learn how professional investment real estate is actually transacted."*

**Honest assessment — the weakest segment.** Competing against abundant free content and established local training bodies. B_ has no brand recognition with this buyer and no content-marketing engine. Acquisition cost could easily exceed a $199 enrollment.

**Do not build for this segment first.** If standalone revenue is wanted, the credible route is Segment 3 (cohorts) or reputation earned through Segments 1–2 — not direct-to-individual course sales.

## Jobs summary

| Segment | Core job | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Franchise / partner team | Reach launch readiness consistently | `[F]` real demand |
| Existing software customer | Close a capability gap on trigger | `[A]` plausible, high-intent |
| Developer organization | Certify a broker force on a project | `[A]` may be absorbed into retainer |
| Independent professional | Learn the craft | `[A]` weak — do not lead here |

## Anti-personas

- Anyone seeking an **accredited licence or formal qualification** — Building Blocks does not issue these and must never imply it.
- Organizations wanting a **generic corporate LMS** — Open edX alone is cheaper without us.
- Buyers wanting **content only**, with no interest in certification, gating or the BluePrint loop — they will not value the price.

## The experience principle

Learners arrive because something specific needs to change — a launch, a new role, a failure. The product should open with **what you must complete and why**, not with a course catalogue.
