# Developer “Franchise” / Sales Program — Investor Two-Pager

## 1. The idea in plain English

The Developer Sales Program gives a property developer one governed way to manage project and unit information, authorize agencies, distribute approved inventory, capture qualified interest, control reservations and approvals, coordinate transactions, validate commissions, and see channel performance.

It combines a developer workspace, selected VAULTED distribution, and BluePrint execution. It is not construction management, a generic advertising agency, an ERP replacement, or a promise that B_ will sell the inventory.

Management currently uses “developer franchise” as a convenient label. For investors and contracts, **Developer Sales Program** is more accurate until the offer is standardized, transferable, repeatable, and legally determined to be a franchise or another form of commercial license.

## 2. Customer, problem, and value

**Buyer:** developer owner, commercial director, or project director. Users include inventory administrators, internal sales, approved agencies, transaction coordinators, finance, and commission reviewers.

**Problem:** developers often distribute price lists and availability through PDFs, Excel, email, and WhatsApp. Information becomes stale, agencies duplicate leads, approvals are slow, documents vary, attribution is disputed, and management cannot see which channels truly convert.

**Value proposition:** a controlled source of project/unit truth connected to agency eligibility, demand qualification, transaction execution, and performance reporting.

## 3. Product and workflow

1. Developer and project due diligence.
2. Structured project, tower, unit, price, availability, incentive, document, and owner records.
3. Versioned inventory feed with freshness SLA and named owner.
4. Agency authorization and distribution windows.
5. Qualified interest with source, consent, and attribution evidence.
6. Inventory validation, allocation, or reservation.
7. BluePrint transaction workspace for documents, approvals, milestones, closing, and commission.
8. Developer report covering response, reservations, close rate, cycle time, channel contribution, and disputes.

## 4. Revenue model and unit economics

There is deliberately no published universal price yet. The commercial hypothesis combines:

- one-time project setup and data normalization;
- monthly platform fee based on active projects, users, or unit volume;
- agency/distribution workspace fees where appropriate;
- separately scoped integration and implementation;
- success economics only where legally permitted and contractually attributable.

The unit must be a **project or developer account**, not property GMV. The investor model should use:

| Metric | Formula / gate |
|---|---|
| First-year contract value | Setup + 12 months SaaS + legally valid collected success fees |
| Recurring ACV | Monthly platform fee × 12 + contracted recurring modules |
| Gross contribution | Collected revenue − implementation labor − support − cloud/API − partner shares − direct compliance cost |
| Implementation payback | Acquisition + onboarding cost ÷ monthly gross-margin contribution |
| Success-fee revenue | Completed attributable GMV × contracted net take rate, after reversals and partner shares |
| Minimum price | Direct delivery cost ÷ (1 − target gross margin) |

No success fee enters a forecast until the contract defines payer, trigger, attribution window, taxes, cancellations, refunds, partner shares, and collection timing. The first paid pilot must establish actual onboarding hours, support load, willingness to pay, sales cycle, and gross margin.

## 6. Competitive landscape

Sell.Do offers real-estate-native CRM, inventory, channel-partner, booking, post-sales, and developer workflows. Salesforce and Propertybase offer extensible CRM and real-estate lifecycle capabilities. Large developers can build or customize ERP/CRM systems, while smaller developers continue with spreadsheets, portals, and WhatsApp.

B_'s potential wedge is not feature breadth. It is a focused corridor connecting governed developer inventory to independent agencies, qualified investors, and a complete BluePrint transaction record. The disadvantage is that incumbents have deeper CRM/ERP breadth and more implementation resources.

## 7. Defensibility and risks

Potential defensibility: direct supply rights, structured project/unit history, agency authorization graph, attributable outcome data, country configuration, and switching costs created by trusted operating workflow.

Main risks: stale developer data; long enterprise sales cycles; custom integration creep; disputed lead attribution; off-platform leakage; legal restrictions on success fees; developers demanding exclusivity; and excessive dependence on a few projects.

## 8. What must be proven

- One paid design partner and one production project.
- Signed responsibility matrix and inventory-freshness SLA.
- At least one transaction completed through the entire governed workflow.
- Clear attribution accepted by developer and agency.
- Standard implementation that can be repeated without a custom rebuild.
- Positive gross contribution after all direct labor.
- Legal opinion on brokerage, referral, franchise, AML, privacy, consumer, tax, and success-fee structure.

## Investor conclusion

**Promising enterprise wedge; too early to call a franchise.** Fund a paid design-partner pilot with strict scope. The program becomes valuable when it proves reliable inventory and attributable transactions, not when it signs non-binding developer logos.
