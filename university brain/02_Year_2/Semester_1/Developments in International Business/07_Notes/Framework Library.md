### Ashraf et al 2026 - Localization Matters More Than Ever

**Session:** 2  
**Source:** [Ashraf et al 2026 - Localization Matters More Than Ever](../03_Readings/Session_02/Ashraf%20et%20al%202026%20-%20Localization%20Matters%20More%20Than%20Ever.md)

**Core rule:** Localization now concerns the operating model, not just marketing: a multipolar company builds regionally rooted businesses coordinated by central strategy.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Three capabilities: build regional production, technology, data and supplier capacity; shape local rules and ecosystems; share AI-enhanced intelligence rapidly across the global network. Regional autonomy and global learning must coexist. The article describes proprietary research on 34,866 MNEs, supplier data on 3,029 companies, 1,000 executives and company examples.

**Decision use and boundary:** Duplicating functions sacrifices some scale efficiency to gain resilience and institutional fit. The proprietary evidence is not a universal causal test. Apply to IKEA sourcing and services; compare CAGE diagnosis with the organizational response in Santos and Williamson.

### Ghemawat 2001 - Distance Still Matters

**Session:** 2  
**Source:** [Ghemawat 2001 - Distance Still Matters](../03_Readings/Session_02/Ghemawat%202001%20-%20Distance%20Still%20Matters.md)

**Core rule:** Market size alone overstates opportunity: assess the bilateral distance between home and host countries and weight it by the industry.

**Mechanism and evidence:** CAGE separates cultural distance (language, norms, identity), administrative distance (political ties, trade arrangements, regulation), geographic distance (physical distance, access, transport, communication) and economic distance (income, infrastructure, resources and costs). STAR TV illustrates language and programming mismatch; the Tricon example shows that distance adjustments can reorder a market ranking.

**Decision use and boundary:** Distance is relational and industry-specific; a country score is not a sufficient investment decision. Cultural averages conceal within-country variation. Compare with OLI location advantages and with Beugelsdijk et al. on observed cultural-distance effects. The PDF cover attributes it to Randy Collins, but the article itself identifies Pankaj Ghemawat; use the latter.

### Dunning 2000 - The Eclectic Paradigm

**Session:** 2  
**Source:** [Dunning 2000 - The Eclectic Paradigm](../03_Readings/Session_02/Dunning%202000%20-%20The%20Eclectic%20Paradigm.md)

**Core rule:** OLI is an envelope joining theories of ownership advantages, location attractions and internalization benefits to explain foreign value-adding activity.

**Mechanism and evidence:** O asks what firm-specific advantage supports international activity. L asks where immobile resources and institutions complement that advantage. I asks whether coordinating transactions inside the firm offers net benefits over contracts or markets. The paper extends OLI dynamically to asset-augmenting FDI, alliances and a knowledge-intensive economy rather than only exploiting an existing asset abroad.

**Decision use and boundary:** OLI is a contextual explanatory framework, not a mechanical three-box forecast or a claim that every foreign sale requires ownership. Compare asset exploitation with Luo and Tung asset seeking; use transaction-cost logic to justify I and CAGE to examine L.

### Luo and Tung 2018 - A General Theory of Springboard MNEs

**Session:** 2  
**Source:** [Luo and Tung 2018 - A General Theory of Springboard MNEs](../03_Readings/Session_02/Luo%20and%20Tung%202018%20-%20A%20General%20Theory%20of%20Springboard%20MNEs.md)

**Core rule:** Some MNEs expand abroad to acquire strategic resources, remedy capability weaknesses and overcome home-country constraints, then use the acquired assets to strengthen global competitiveness.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Springboard and post-springboard activity form an upward spiral: acquire assets abroad, combine them with home strengths, upgrade the home base and compete more widely. Amalgamation, ambidexterity and adaptation describe distinctive strengths. Home institutions may both support expansion and create vulnerability; acquisition integration and cross-cultural management determine whether asset purchases become capabilities.

**Decision use and boundary:** Emerging-market origin does not make every firm a springboard MNE; the mechanism is strategic asset seeking and capability recombination. Fast acquisition can outpace integration. Compare dynamic OLI and Uppsala experiential learning.

### Bamford et al 2020 - Joint Ventures and Partnerships in a Downturn

**Session:** 3  
**Source:** [Bamford et al 2020 - Joint Ventures and Partnerships in a Downturn](../03_Readings/Session_03/Bamford%20et%20al%202020%20-%20Joint%20Ventures%20and%20Partnerships%20in%20a%20Downturn.md)

**Core rule:** A downturn makes partnerships tools for sharing capital needs, restructuring existing collaborations and preparing for recovery.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Review the existing portfolio for capital allocation, operational performance, governance and strategic relevance; consider restructuring ownership, combining assets or exiting before automatically terminating partnerships. New collaborations can provide capabilities and market access while spreading risk. The authors examine JV formation and termination across 35 years and present industry comparisons of returns.

**Decision use and boundary:** Partnerships require aligned objectives, decision rights, incentives and exit arrangements. Historical JV returns do not prove that a JV is always superior to full ownership. Use in Popeyes to assess partner fit and governance, alongside Meyer et al. on access to embedded local resources.

### Santos and Williamson 2015 - The New Mission for Multinationals

**Session:** 3  
**Source:** [Santos and Williamson 2015 - The New Mission for Multinationals](../03_Readings/Session_03/Santos%20and%20Williamson%202015%20-%20The%20New%20Mission%20for%20Multinationals.md)

**Core rule:** To compete with strong local firms, multinationals should integrate locally and adapt globally.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Local integration means embedding products, marketing and distribution in communities; co-investing and innovating with local suppliers; engaging governments and regulators. Global adaptation means reshaping the multinational network to use diverse local strengths instead of forcing a uniform home-country model on each subsidiary. Emerging-market examples challenge the assumption that scale and global brands automatically beat local competitors.

**Decision use and boundary:** Local embedding consumes resources and may complicate global coordination. Preserve transferable assets while adapting the system around them. Compare Ashraf et al. multipolar operations and the integration-responsiveness framework; apply to Popeyes partner, menu and supply-chain choices.

### Beugelsdijk et al 2018 - Cultural Distance and Firm Internationalization

**Session:** 3  
**Source:** [Beugelsdijk et al 2018 - Cultural Distance and Firm Internationalization](../03_Readings/Session_03/Beugelsdijk%20et%20al%202018%20-%20Cultural%20Distance%20and%20Firm%20Internationalization.md)

**Core rule:** Cultural distance affects different internationalization decisions and outcomes differently; it is not a universal predictor of lower ownership or lower firm performance.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The meta-analysis spans pre-investment location, ownership and establishment choices and post-investment practice transfer and performance. Firms are less likely to enter culturally distant locations; conditional on entry they prefer greenfields and transfer more management practices. The aggregate evidence does not show a general effect on investment amount or JV versus full ownership. Subsidiary performance is negatively affected, but whole-MNE performance is not in the aggregate.

**Decision use and boundary:** Results depend on cultural measurement and home-country context. Distinguish subsidiary from corporate performance and greenfield/acquisition from ownership share. Compare the practical CAGE screening tool with this empirical evidence rather than treating country culture as destiny.

### Meyer et al 2009 - Institutions Resources and Entry Strategies

**Session:** 3  
**Source:** [Meyer et al 2009 - Institutions Resources and Entry Strategies](../03_Readings/Session_03/Meyer%20et%20al%202009%20-%20Institutions%20Resources%20and%20Entry%20Strategies.md)

**Core rule:** Entry mode depends jointly on host institutions and the local resources the investor needs.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Weak market-supporting institutions increase the value of JVs for obtaining local resources. As institutions strengthen, acquisitions become more viable for accessing intangible, organizationally embedded resources. Greenfield entry, acquisition and JV solve different resource-access and market-inefficiency problems. Survey and archival evidence covers India, Vietnam, South Africa and Egypt.

**Decision use and boundary:** A country label alone cannot choose entry mode; specify the needed resource and whether it can be bought separately, accessed through a partner or obtained by buying an organization. Compare transaction-cost internalization with institutional and resource-based explanations; apply to Popeyes local operator selection.

### Henisz and Zelner 2010 - The Hidden Risks in Emerging Markets

**Session:** 4  
**Source:** [Henisz and Zelner 2010 - The Hidden Risks in Emerging Markets](../03_Readings/Session_04/Henisz%20and%20Zelner%202010%20-%20The%20Hidden%20Risks%20in%20Emerging%20Markets.md)

**Core rule:** Political risk can divert expected profits through regulation and selective enforcement even without formal expropriation.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Traditional contracts and insurance cannot remove all policy exposure. Map the actors, their interests and influence, build political capital, communicate the local benefits of investment and understand decision-makers incentives. The article uses corporate examples and analytical tools for stakeholder networks, communication and bargaining. Operational efficiency must be evaluated alongside political acceptability.

**Decision use and boundary:** This is the article, not the AES-Telasi teaching case. Its historical claim about reduced direct seizure is not a guarantee for every period or country. Compare Rice and Zegart organizational risk capabilities and Henisz et al. evidence linking stakeholder cooperation to valuation.

### Rice and Zegart 2018 - Managing 21st Century Political Risk

**Session:** 4  
**Source:** [Rice and Zegart 2018 - Managing 21st Century Political Risk](../03_Readings/Session_04/Rice%20and%20Zegart%202018%20-%20Managing%2021st%20Century%20Political%20Risk.md)

**Core rule:** Political action affecting a business can come from governments, activists, local officials, individuals and other actors connected by technology and supply chains.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Four capabilities: understand exposure and risk appetite; analyze information, scenarios and consequences; mitigate through relationships, diversification and preparation; respond with effective crisis action and learning. Long supply chains and viral communication transmit local events rapidly. The framework asks executives to identify organizational gaps rather than relying on a country-risk list.

**Decision use and boundary:** A low-probability event can still have a large cumulative or portfolio effect. The framework organizes judgment; it does not supply reliable probabilities for unprecedented geopolitical shocks. Apply to AES headquarters governance and contrast national political risk with bilateral Nvidia exposure.

### Henisz et al 2014 - Spinning Gold

**Session:** 4  
**Source:** [Henisz et al 2014 - Spinning Gold](../03_Readings/Session_04/Henisz%20et%20al%202014%20-%20Spinning%20Gold.md)

**Core rule:** Stakeholder cooperation helps firms realize the value of physical assets by reducing the risk of disruption and hold-up.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The study analyzes 26 gold mines owned by 19 listed firms during 1993–2008, coding over 50,000 stakeholder events. It relates cooperation and conflict to market capitalization while accounting for physical asset value. Including stakeholder relationships reduces the unexplained discount on physical asset NPV from 72% to 37–13%; this is a valuation-model result, not a guaranteed return on engagement spending. Session 7 assigns Introduction, The valuation of stakeholder management, and Discussion.

**Decision use and boundary:** Evidence comes from gold mining; transfer the mechanism carefully to platforms and other sectors. Non-contractual stakeholders may determine whether contractual rights produce cash flow. One canonical reading serves both sessions. Compare institutional legitimacy and transaction-cost hold-up; apply to AES and Shopee stakeholders.

### Ramamurti 2001 - The Obsolescing Bargaining Model

**Session:** 4  
**Source:** [Ramamurti 2001 - The Obsolescing Bargaining Model](../03_Readings/Session_04/Ramamurti%202001%20-%20The%20Obsolescing%20Bargaining%20Model.md)

**Core rule:** The traditional two-party MNE–host-government model is insufficient; FDI bargaining is a dynamic two-tier and multi-party process.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Tier 1 consists of bargaining among home and host governments and multilateral institutions, producing macro rules on FDI. Tier 2 consists of firm-level negotiations constrained by those rules. Classical obsolescing bargaining predicts that an investor loses leverage after sunk investment, but changing macro institutions and competing parties can alter that trajectory.

**Decision use and boundary:** Do not attribute the simple classical model as the papers final conclusion: its contribution is to revise it. Treaty constraints and institutional credibility vary by country pair and industry. Compare Session 5 weakening supranational constraints with the institutional protection of micro bargains.

### Chipman 2016 - Why Your Company Needs a Foreign Policy

**Session:** 5  
**Source:** [Chipman 2016 - Why Your Company Needs a Foreign Policy](../03_Readings/Session_05/Chipman%202016%20-%20Why%20Your%20Company%20Needs%20a%20Foreign%20Policy.md)

**Core rule:** Geopolitical expertise, corporate diplomacy and geopolitical due diligence should be core corporate capabilities.

**Mechanism and evidence:** A corporate foreign policy gives the firm a coherent approach to political relationships and exposure instead of assuming the international status quo will persist. Analyze conflict, changing state alignments and strategic dependencies; build relationships and internal expertise; connect geopolitical judgments to investment and operational decisions. The article draws on political developments and company practice in its 2016 context.

**Decision use and boundary:** Corporate diplomacy cannot override binding state restrictions or erase asymmetric dependency. Evaluate which distinctive assets provide bargaining room. Compare Rice and Zegart risk process with a geopolitical strategic posture; apply to Nvidia competing government demands.

### Mol et al 2023 - Should I Stay or Should I Go

**Session:** 5  
**Source:** [Mol et al 2023 - Should I Stay or Should I Go](../03_Readings/Session_05/Mol%20et%20al%202023%20-%20Should%20I%20Stay%20or%20Should%20I%20Go.md)

**Core rule:** A geopolitical shift forces MNEs to weigh operational exposure against reputational and societal pressures when deciding whether to stay or leave.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The article develops an operational-versus-reputational framework using Danish MNE responses in Russia following the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Decisions depend on sunk assets, local employees, supply chains, values, home-country pressures and exit feasibility. Geopolitical change can reverse prior assumptions about profitable operations and expose tensions between stakeholder obligations.

**Decision use and boundary:** Public announcements, operational withdrawal and legal transfer of assets are different events. Staying may harm reputation; leaving may harm employees or create openings for rivals. Do not assume all firms face the same feasible choice. Compare Chipman corporate foreign policy and sanctions-related institutional pressure in Meyer and Thein.

### Estrin and Hancke 2026 - Geopolitics Geoeconomics and Organizational Models

**Session:** 5  
**Source:** [Estrin and Hancke 2026 - Geopolitics Geoeconomics and Organizational Models](../03_Readings/Session_05/Estrin%20and%20Hancke%202026%20-%20Geopolitics%20Geoeconomics%20and%20Organizational%20Models.md)

**Core rule:** Geopolitical blocs and renewed vertical integration can reshape international production without eliminating global trade or value chains.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The commentary constructs scenarios around two inward-looking political-economic groupings, weakly aligned countries and blocs such as the EU, and firms vertically reintegrating under technological or political pressure. Home location changes firms strategic options; policy and security considerations may displace narrow comparative advantage in sourcing and location decisions.

**Decision use and boundary:** This is scenario-based conceptual analysis, not a prediction that a single two-bloc future must occur. Distinguish changes in the composition of flows from a collapse in their aggregate volume. Compare Altman et al. resilient flows and the Session 5 shift from global efficiency toward security.

### Meyer and Thein 2014 - International Sanctions Against Myanmar

**Session:** 5  
**Source:** [Meyer and Thein 2014 - International Sanctions Against Myanmar](../03_Readings/Session_05/Meyer%20and%20Thein%202014%20-%20International%20Sanctions%20Against%20Myanmar.md)

**Core rule:** Home-country sanctions and civil-society pressure can restrict firms abroad even when host-country operations remain possible.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The article examines Myanmar under adverse home-country institutions, exploring how formal rules and informal social pressure change international business decisions. MNE responses depend on exposure to sanctioning home institutions, business activity and stakeholders; regulatory compliance is only part of the institutional problem.

### Anwar 2026 - The Algorithmic Boardroom

**Session:** 6  
**Source:** [Anwar 2026 - The Algorithmic Boardroom](../03_Readings/Session_06/Anwar%202026%20-%20The%20Algorithmic%20Boardroom.md)

**Core rule:** AI should augment board judgment within explicit governance and accountability arrangements.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Four pillars: policy and principle; people and competency; process and due diligence; proof and accountability. Four operational stages: technical vetting before deliberation; strategic simulation at the board meeting; ethical and qualitative calibration with humans; accountability logging. ### Moses et al 2025 - Data Accuracy in West African Markets

**Session:** 6  
**Source:** [Moses et al 2025 - Data Accuracy in West African Markets](../03_Readings/Session_06/Moses%20et%20al%202025%20-%20Data%20Accuracy%20in%20West%20African%20Markets.md)

**Core rule:** Investment decisions are only as reliable as the market data and local definitions on which they depend.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Challenges include unmeasured informal activity and misunderstood terminology, infrastructure deficits and missing data, and regional or regulatory variation. ### Hennart 2019 - Digitalized Service Multinationals

**Session:** 6  
**Source:** [Hennart 2019 - Digitalized Service Multinationals](../03_Readings/Session_06/Hennart%202019%20-%20Digitalized%20Service%20Multinationals.md)

**Core rule:** Digital platforms and network externalities do not, by themselves, require abandoning existing IB governance theory.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Hennart critiques claims that network advantages are an entirely new FSA and networks a third governance mode. Network effects resemble familiar scale mechanisms; examine actual business models and contracts to determine governance. Distinguish foreign sales from the need for a physical foreign footprint and whether network effects cross borders or remain local.

**Decision use and boundary:** Do not infer global network advantage from user counts alone. Local drivers, sellers, hosts, regulations and complementary assets can make a digital service location-bound. ### Lindner et al 2025 - AI and Augmented Decision Making

**Session:** 6  
**Source:** [Lindner et al 2025 - AI and Augmented Decision Making](../03_Readings/Session_06/Lindner%20et%20al%202025%20-%20AI%20and%20Augmented%20Decision%20Making.md)

**Core rule:** AI changes the behavioral assumptions of IB theory by easing some human rationality constraints while introducing machine and human–AI interaction biases.

**Mechanism and evidence:** Humans face incomplete information and cognitive limitations, use heuristics and display judgment and present-state biases. AI adds information and processing capability, but biased training data and objectives can distort outputs; benefits and trust in human–AI interaction can also be uneven. Reassess the boundary conditions of theories such as Uppsala and internalization when decision processes are augmented.

**Decision use and boundary:** Augmentation is not perfect information or replacement of experiential learning. Local definitions, objectives, incentives and oversight still matter. Compare Anwar implementation governance and Moses data accuracy; directly useful for mock-exam Question 2.

### Kim 2020 - Whats the Best Pace of Expansion

**Session:** 7  
**Source:** [Kim 2020 - Whats the Best Pace of Expansion](../03_Readings/Session_07/Kim%202020%20-%20Whats%20the%20Best%20Pace%20of%20Expansion.md)

**Core rule:** The preferred expansion pace depends on whether firms enter familiar home-region markets or more distant regions.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The HBR research brief summarizes 767 listed Chinese manufacturing MNEs that opened subsidiaries during 2002–2014. Faster intra-regional expansion is associated with better financial performance; faster inter-regional expansion with worse performance. Knowledge overlap supports reuse of technological and marketing FSAs; distant expansion brings time-compression diseconomies and managerial complexity.

**Decision use and boundary:** The short brief and Kim et al. academic article report the same study, not independent evidence. Chinese manufacturing results do not automatically establish the best pace for a Singaporean platform. The PDF includes unrelated briefs; only the expansion brief is extracted as course reading content. Compare pace with Lu and Beamish extent.

### Kim et al 2020 - Fast Internationalization Across Regions

**Session:** 7  
**Source:** [Kim et al 2020 - Fast Internationalization Across Regions](../03_Readings/Session_07/Kim%20et%20al%202020%20-%20Fast%20Internationalization%20Across%20Regions.md)

**Core rule:** Internationalization speed and geographic region interact in their effects on financial performance.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The framework combines speed and home regionalization. Data cover 767 publicly listed Chinese manufacturing MNEs over 2002–2014; intra-regional speed benefits performance while inter-regional speed is harmful. Technological and marketing resources are valuable for fast home-region expansion but can be vulnerable in distant regions. Time-compression diseconomies and constrained adaptation explain why advantages travel imperfectly.

**Decision use and boundary:** Separate speed, scope and location; effects are not a global causal rule for all industries. Country differences exist within regions. Link to the HBR brief and the uploaded condensed Kim study aid, and use the study to test a Shopee regional-cluster interpretation.

### Lu and Beamish 2004 - The S Curve Hypothesis

**Session:** 7  
**Source:** [Lu and Beamish 2004 - The S Curve Hypothesis](../03_Readings/Session_07/Lu%20and%20Beamish%202004%20-%20The%20S%20Curve%20Hypothesis.md)

**Core rule:** International diversification and performance can follow a horizontal S-curve as early foreignness costs, later benefits and eventual coordination costs change their balance.

**Mechanism and evidence:** The study analyzes 1,489 Japanese firms over 12 years. At low multinationality, liabilities and learning costs can reduce performance; at intermediate levels, experience, scale and asset exploitation improve it; at high levels, complexity and coordination burdens can dominate. Technology and advertising investment moderate gains from FDI expansion.

**Decision use and boundary:** This is a relationship with extent of international diversification, not a guaranteed chronological path or a fixed optimal country count. Historical Japanese firm evidence requires contextual application. Compare Kim on speed and region, and assess which costs or capabilities would explain a firms position on the curve.

### Foundational theories connecting the sessions

**Liability of foreignness:** Additional costs relative to local competitors arise from spatial coordination, unfamiliarity, host legitimacy or discrimination, and home-country constraints. Transferable FSAs and appropriate local practices may offset them; ordinary competitive weakness is not automatically LOF.

**Transaction costs and internalization:** Compare search, contracting, monitoring, enforcement and opportunism costs in markets with internal coordination costs. Asset specificity, uncertainty and incomplete contracts can support internalizing activities. Ownership does not remove all institutional or stakeholder risk.

**Uppsala learning and commitment:** Experiential knowledge reduces uncertainty and supports increasing commitment: no regular exports, independent representatives, a sales subsidiary, then foreign production. The stage picture is a teaching simplification rather than a mandatory path for every firm. Born globals, asset-seeking MNEs and AI augmentation provide useful comparisons.

**Integration and responsiveness:** Decide which assets and processes gain from global coordination and which require local adaptation. Product uniformity, ownership, resource commitment and organizational coordination are distinct choices. Local embedding can generate resources for the global network rather than only copying home-country capabilities.

**Network effects:** Direct effects connect users of the same type; indirect effects connect different sides of a platform. Determine whether effects remain within a country or cross borders. A high global user count does not guarantee local drivers, sellers, hosts, data quality or legitimacy.

**Risk and Knightian uncertainty:** Measurable probabilities support conventional hedging; unprecedented political shifts may require scenarios, relationships, reversible commitments and active judgment. Do not treat unknown political outcomes as reliably insurable simply because they are called risk.
