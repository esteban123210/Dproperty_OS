## How the sources fit together

The Session 1 sources answer one overarching question from different levels of analysis:

1. **Ghemawat:** borders and distance still strongly constrain cross-border flows; managers systematically overestimate globalization.
2. **Altman et al.:** newer evidence does not show broad deglobalization either; international flows remain resilient while geography and geopolitics still structure them.
3. **Zaheer:** those cross-border frictions become firm-level costs through the **liability of foreignness**.
4. **Slides:** connect these ideas to MNEs, FDI, firm-specific advantages, transaction costs, vertical integration, internalization, and OLI.
5. **Bean of Africa:** makes the abstractions concrete through payment, logistics, customs, information, documentation, and intermediary frictions.

## Session 1 conceptual chain

**The world is semi-globalized**  
→ borders and distance still matter  
→ cross-border transactions generate extra costs and uncertainty  
→ foreign firms face a **liability of foreignness (LOF)**  
→ firms need **firm-specific advantages (FSAs)** strong enough to offset LOF  
→ external markets themselves generate transaction costs  
→ firms compare **market vs hierarchy**  
→ if internal coordination is superior, they **internalize**  
→ when **Ownership + Location + Internalization** advantages coexist, FDI/MNE organization can be attractive.
