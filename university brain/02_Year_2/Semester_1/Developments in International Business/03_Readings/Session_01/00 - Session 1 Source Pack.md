## How the sources fit together

The five sources answer a single overarching question from different levels of analysis:

1. **Ghemawat:** borders and distance still strongly constrain cross-border flows; managers systematically overestimate globalization.
2. **Altman et al.:** newer evidence does not show broad deglobalization either; the world remains internationalized but far from frictionless, and flow patterns vary greatly by country and geopolitical relationship.
3. **Zaheer:** those cross-border frictions become firm-level costs through the **liability of foreignness**.
4. **Slides:** connect these ideas to the foundations of IB: MNEs, FDI, firm-specific advantages, transaction costs, vertical integration, and market-vs-hierarchy choices.
5. **Bean of Africa:** makes the abstractions concrete by showing the transaction costs and intermediary problems involved in moving a simple product across borders.

## Session 1 conceptual chain

**Borders and distance matter**  
→ international markets are not frictionless  
→ cross-border transactions generate additional costs and uncertainty  
→ foreign firms face a liability of foreignness  
→ firms need firm-specific advantages strong enough to offset that liability  
→ when market transactions are costly or unreliable, internalization / hierarchy may outperform arm's-length exchange  
→ this helps explain why multinational enterprises and FDI can exist.
