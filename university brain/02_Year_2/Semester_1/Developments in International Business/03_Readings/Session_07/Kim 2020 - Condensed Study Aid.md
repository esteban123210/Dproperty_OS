WHAT'S THE BEST PACE OF EXPANSION

Condensed Reading Session 7 Article 1

Kim, H. (2020). What's the Best Pace of Expansion? Harvard Business Review, 98(3), 29.

## PART I WHAT IS THIS ARTICLE REALLY ABOUT

The article answers a practical question that is often framed too simply: should a company expand abroad quickly or slowly? Kim's answer is that there is no universally best speed. The right pace depends on where the company is expanding and how much of its existing knowledge and competitive advantage can travel to the new markets.

Fast expansion can work well inside a firm's home region. Nearby markets usually share more customer habits, institutions, business networks and operating conditions with the home country. This overlap allows the firm to reuse what it already knows. Its technology, brand and marketing routines can be deployed across several countries before competitors occupy the opportunity.

The logic changes when a company enters a distant region. Managers must learn unfamiliar regulations, customer expectations, partners and competitive structures while also coordinating a rapidly growing international organization. If many distant markets are entered at once, learning is compressed into too little time. The firm may spend heavily, repeat avoidable mistakes and discover that capabilities which were valuable at home do not transfer automatically.

The managerial conclusion is therefore a two-speed approach: move relatively fast where knowledge and capabilities are reusable, but expand more slowly and experimentally where the firm faces greater distance. Speed should follow evidence of transferability and learning rather than management ambition alone.

THE ARTICLE IN ONE SENTENCE Do not choose one global expansion speed: move faster where your existing advantages travel well, and more slowly where distance requires new learning and adaptation.

## PART II THE CENTRAL ARGUMENT

The article begins with two competing views. One view says that rapid expansion helps a company capture customers, scale and first-mover advantages before rivals. The other says that moderate growth gives the organization time to learn and absorb complexity. The research summarized by Kim reconciles the two positions by adding a spatial condition: the financial effect of speed depends on whether expansion stays within the home region or crosses into a more distant region.

Dimension | Intra-regional expansion | Inter-regional expansion

Typical context | Countries within or near the firm's home region | More distant regions with larger institutional and market differences

Knowledge overlap | Higher. Existing market knowledge and routines are more reusable. | Lower. The firm must learn unfamiliar customers, institutions and operating systems.

Firm-specific advantages | Technology, brand and marketing capabilities are easier to exploit quickly. | The same advantages may require adaptation and can become less valuable or even constraining.

Risk of moving fast | Usually lower because coordination and learning demands are more familiar. | Higher because learning, adaptation and management capacity cannot be compressed without cost.

Recommended pace | Faster, provided operational capacity and economics remain sound. | Slower and staged, with commitment increasing after local learning is demonstrated.

## PART III WHY SPEED WORKS DIFFERENTLY ACROSS REGIONS

## Knowledge overlap reduces the cost of speed

Intra-regional countries are not identical, but they often provide more familiar institutions, customer expectations and commercial networks than markets in another region. Because less has to be relearned, the company can add subsidiaries quickly without multiplying uncertainty at the same rate. Faster expansion can therefore be a form of exploitation: the firm reuses capabilities it already possesses.

## Firm-specific advantages are often region-bound

Firm-specific advantages, or FSAs, are capabilities that help a company outperform competitors, such as technology, brands, data, logistics routines or marketing knowledge. The article warns against assuming that these advantages are globally transferable. A marketing formula designed for one region may not fit another region's consumers, channels or institutions. Technology can also require local infrastructure, partners, standards or complementary skills before it creates value.

## Time compression diseconomies make rushed learning expensive

Some capabilities take time to build. Local knowledge, relationships, legitimacy and management routines cannot always be created simply by spending more money or assigning more people. When learning is forced into a short period, its quality can fall while costs rise. This is the meaning of time compression diseconomies: attempting to do the same learning much faster can produce disproportionately high costs and weaker results.

## Organizational complexity can outrun management capacity

Each additional market creates coordination demands across product, marketing, regulation, data, logistics, partners and talent. With rapid inter-regional expansion, these demands are also less standardized. Managers may have to solve several unfamiliar problems simultaneously, and resources are spread across markets before any one operating model is proven. The problem is therefore not speed alone, but speed combined with distance and limited organizational capacity.

## PART IV EVIDENCE AND BOUNDARIES

The HBR article summarizes research on 767 publicly listed Chinese manufacturing multinationals that established foreign subsidiaries between 2002 and 2014. The researchers compared expansion within the home region of East, South and Southeast Asia with expansion into more distant regions and examined return on assets and other financial measures.

The main pattern was clear: faster intra-regional expansion was associated with better financial performance, while faster inter-regional expansion was associated with worse performance. The underlying academic study reports that a one-standard-deviation increase in intra-regional speed was associated with ROA improvements of about 5.9% to 10.2%, depending on the type of host country. Comparable increases in inter-regional speed were associated with ROA reductions of about 6.5% to 7.6%. Technological and marketing resources strengthened the benefits of fast intra-regional expansion but did not provide the same protection in distant regions.

These findings should be applied as a framework, not as a universal law. The sample consists of Chinese manufacturing firms, whereas Shopee is a Singaporean digital platform. The data are historical and regional categories can conceal important country differences. The evidence identifies systematic associations after statistical controls, but it cannot prove that speed alone caused every performance outcome. A distant market can still succeed, and a nearby market can still fail. The practical question is whether the firm has accumulated enough market-specific knowledge and whether its capabilities genuinely travel.

## PART V THE MANAGERIAL RULE

Kim's argument does not recommend permanent regional confinement. It recommends sequencing commitment. Firms can use faster expansion to exploit proven capabilities in familiar markets, while treating distant-market entry as a staged learning process. Management should increase commitment only after it has evidence that the local model works and that the organization can support the additional complexity.

Separate expansion speed by market type rather than announcing one worldwide growth target.

Use pilots, partnerships and limited commitments to learn in distant regions before scaling.

Test whether technology, brand and marketing routines create value locally without unsustainable subsidies.

Measure organizational capacity, regulatory readiness and local-team depth before opening several markets simultaneously.

Treat withdrawal as disciplined portfolio management when evidence fails, but capture the learning before exiting.

## PART VI LINKAGE TO THE SHOPEE CASE AND ASSIGNMENT

The assignment asks whether Shopee's international expansion created sustainable value and what management should prioritize over the next three to five years. Kim provides the central analytical lens: assess pace and geographic distance together, then test whether Shopee's capabilities travel and whether the resulting growth improves economics rather than only scale.

Shopee evidence | Reading lens | What management should test

Southeast Asia and Taiwan became Shopee's strong base. | Home-region speed can exploit familiar mobile, seller and consumer capabilities. | Country contribution, repeat demand, seller density, logistics performance and whether growth becomes less subsidy-dependent.

Brazil was entered in 2019 and became a major success before the 2021 expansion burst. | Distance does not make success impossible. Time, localization, gaming links and market conditions can build a new regional capability base. | Whether Brazil's economics and routines are transferable to a Latin American cluster rather than to every country globally.

In 2021 Shopee entered several Latin American and European countries, then India, in rapid succession. | Speed and geographic distance increased together, raising learning and coordination demands. | Management bandwidth, local-team depth, compliance readiness, localization cost and time to reach stable unit economics.

France closed roughly five months after entry; India closed roughly four months after launch despite rapid user growth. | Market traction alone is not evidence of sustainable value. Fast entry can reveal demand while still failing institutionally or economically. | Contribution margin after promotions and logistics, customer retention, regulatory legitimacy, political exposure and cost of exit.

## How the reading supports the case analysis

Shopee's strongest evidence supports a regional or cluster-based model. Its Southeast Asian position shows that rapid scaling can create value when the company understands mobile-first customers, local sellers, logistics and digital payments. Brazil shows that a distant market can become successful, but its sequence matters: Shopee entered in 2019, localized its offer, connected gamification with Garena's popularity and benefited from a major shift toward e-commerce. Brazil is therefore evidence that inter-regional expansion can work after learning, not proof that Shopee should enter many distant markets simultaneously.

The 2021 expansion wave is more consistent with Kim's warning. Shopee entered multiple Latin American markets, Poland, Spain, France and India within a short period. France was closed in March 2022 after only a few months. India achieved more than 21 million monthly active users and reportedly processed ten million daily orders, yet the business still closed in March 2022 amid regulatory, political, competitive and legitimacy pressures. These exits show why downloads, orders and gross merchandise value are incomplete indicators of sustainable internationalization performance.

## Evidence management should use

Financial value Country contribution margin after promotions, logistics and local overhead; customer-acquisition payback; cash burn; and the path to profitability.

Demand quality Repeat purchasing, retention, order frequency, seller retention and organic rather than subsidized growth.

Capability transfer Whether Shopee's technology, gamification, seller tools and marketing advantages work locally without excessive redesign.

Institutional fit Regulatory compliance, political legitimacy, data rules, relationships with local sellers and exposure to policy reversal.

Organizational capacity Strength of local management, logistics partners, customer service and headquarters' ability to coordinate several markets at once.

Portfolio learning Time needed to stabilize a market, evidence transferred from previous entries and whether failed experiments improve later decisions.

## Assignment focused conclusion

Shopee's expansion strengthened its competitive position in Southeast Asia and created a promising base in Brazil, but the rapid inter-regional expansion of 2021 did not yet demonstrate sustainable value. Over the next three to five years, management should consolidate profitable regional clusters, improve the economics of existing operations and treat new distant markets as staged experiments. Acceleration should occur only when unit economics, retention, regulatory legitimacy and organizational capacity show that Shopee's capabilities are genuinely transferable.

Core thesis The best pace of internationalization depends on geographic space.

Intra-regional logic Greater knowledge overlap makes rapid exploitation of FSAs more feasible.

Inter-regional logic Distance, adaptation and coordination costs make rapid expansion more dangerous.

Key mechanism Time compression diseconomies mean that learning cannot always be accelerated efficiently.

Evidence 767 Chinese manufacturing MNEs over 2002-2014; faster regional expansion improved performance, while faster distant expansion reduced it.
