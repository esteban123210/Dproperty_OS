Risk vs. Uncertainty
Knight (1921); LeRoy & Singell (1987) 3
Knight’s definitions of risk and uncertainty were based on his threefold classification of 
unknown outcomes (pp. 224-25 ff.):
(1) a priori probabilities, which are derived deductively, as in rolling dice;
(2) statistical probabilities, which are generated by empirical evaluation of relative 
frequencies, as in life insurance; and
(3) estimates, in which “there is no valid basis of any kind for classifying instances” 
(1921, p. 225). 
(1) and (2) are considered risk. (3) is considered uncertainty.
This differentiation matters substantially from a risk management perspective!

## Page 4

Risk vs. Uncertainty
Knight (1921); LeRoy & Singell (1987) 4
In business, we mostly encounter “true” risk in well-developed, well-functioning markets: 
exchange rates, interest rates, and commodity prices.
• We can manage this risk through contracts in a straightforward way.
• Well-functioning markets offer various derivatives for risk management.
Knight [(1921, p. 232)] explain[s] profit as the reward for bearing uncertainty: “It is this 
true uncertainty which . . . gives the characteristic form of ‘enterprise’ to economic 
organization as a whole and accounts for the peculiar income of the entrepreneur”. 
• “True” uncertainty requires active management through strategy.
The financial and political “risk” we discussed last time are mostly uncertainty. Therefore, 
political risk is a management problem rather than a hedging problem.

• National industrial policy: A set of government strategies designed to promote and regulate the development 
of specific industries within a country and to address various challenges and opportunities within specific 
sectors of the economy
• The primary objective of national industry policy is to foster economic growth, enhance competitiveness, 
create jobs, and achieve sustainable development
• Key components of national industry policy may include government actions like:
• Strategic Planning: Identify key industries that are crucial for economic development
• Incentives and Subsidies: Provide financial incentives, tax breaks, or subsidies
• Infrastructure Development: Invest in infrastructure development to support targeted industries
• Research and Innovation: Fund research institutions, collaborate with industry players, and create 
favorable conditions for innovation and technology adoption.
• Trade Policies: Implement policies to protect and promote domestic industries

## Page 28

What Does This Mean for MNEs?
• What Is Corporate Foreign Policy? To navigate the geopolitical complexities of the 
modern world, companies have to, in effect, “privatize” foreign policy—that is, they must
internalize many of the elements traditionally employed in statecraft.
• Develop your own foreign policy stance. The first principle of corporate diplomacy is 
that companies must develop their own approach to foreign governments, rather than 
manipulate or be manipulated by the policies of their home country.
• Diversify your political relationships. Companies must engage all actors rather than 
attempt to mitigate geopolitical risk just through good government contacts and good 
social practices.
Chipman (2016)

• Strategic non-compliance
• Nvidia’s leverage derives precisely from its 
indispensability to both sides.
• The moment it fully aligns with one, it loses the threat 
point it holds with the other.

more room to maneuver than a firm that one government
