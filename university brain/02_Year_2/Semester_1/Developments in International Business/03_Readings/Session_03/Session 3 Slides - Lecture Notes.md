## Lecture synthesis

Choose entry modes according to control, commitment, resources and institutional fit. Non-equity modes include exporting and contractual arrangements such as licensing/franchising, turnkey projects, R&D contracts and co-marketing. Equity modes include JVs and wholly owned operations established by acquisition or greenfield investment. Separate ownership share from establishment mode. Partnerships trade control for resource access and risk sharing; local responsiveness must be coordinated with global integration.

## Page 51

Entry modes Advantages Disadvantages
1. Non-equity modes: exports
Direct exports ▪ Scale economies concentrated in home country
▪ Better control over distribution
▪ High transportation costs for bulky products
▪ Marketing distance from customers
Indirect exports ▪ Concentration of resources on production ▪ Less control over distribution
▪ Inability to learn how to operate overseas
2. Non-equity modes: contractual agreements
Licensing/ franchising ▪ Low development costs
▪ Low risks in overseas expansion
▪ Inability to engage in global coordination
R&D contracts ▪ Ability to tap into the best locations for certain 
innovations at low costs
▪ Difficult to negotiate and enforce contracts
▪ May lose core innovation capabilities
Co-marketing ▪ Ability to reach more customers ▪ Limited coordination
3. Equity modes: Joint ventures
JV ▪ Sharing costs and risks
▪ Access to partner’s knowledge/ assets
▪ Politically acceptable
▪ Divergent goals and interests of partners
▪ Limited equity and operational control
▪ Difficult to coordinate globally 
4. Equity modes: Wholly owned subsidiaries
Greenfield ▪ Complete equity and operational control
▪ Protection of know-how 
▪ Ability to coordinate globally
▪ Higher development costs and risks
▪ Slow entry speed
Acquisitions ▪ Same as greenfield, but fast entry speed ▪ Same as greenfield, but post-acquisition problems
Pros and cons of different entry modes
Source: Peng (2009)
