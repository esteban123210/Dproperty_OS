## Page 14

Different Types and Levels of Risk
Levels of risk
• Macro risks: Risks that affect all firms in a host country
• Micro risks: Risks that are specific to an industry, firm, or project within a country
This categorization affects how risks can be diversified (micro within country, macro 
across countries)
Types of risk
• Political risk: The risk that the host government unexpectedly changes “the rules of the 
game“
• Financial risk: The risk of unexpected events in a country‘s financial, economic, or 
business life

## Page 15

Managing 21st Century Political Risk
Rice & Zegart (2018) Date 15
Until recently, political risk was relatively easy to understand. More often than not, it 
involved dictators who suddenly seized foreign assets for their own domestic agendas, like 
Venezuela’s Hugo Chávez. Today expropriating leaders are far less common than they used 
to be. And although national governments are still the main arbiters of the business 
environment, a great deal of the political risk within and across countries now comes 
from other players: individuals wielding cell phones, local officials issuing city 
ordinances, terrorists detonating truck bombs, UN officials administering sanctions, and 
many more. Events in far-flung places affect businesses around the world at dizzying 
speed.

Insurance Against Political Risk (1)
• Legal contracts are useful only if they are 
enforced, and shifting laws and regulations can 
render them void.
• Even when contracts can be legally enforced, 
inventive politicians can circumvent them 
through means other than changing laws.
• A firm’s exposure is determined by its own 
ability to manage the policy-making process.
• Financial hedges have limited value for similar 
reasons.
• Some instruments are based on the average risk 
premium associated with existing companies in 
a given country, but they give false comfort. 
Henisz & Zelner (2010)

## Page 19

Insurance Against Political Risk (2)
Slide 
19
The simplest way of managing risk is to avoid it
If that is not possible, insurance might be a way forward
There are some indicators when a risk may be insurable:
• The potential loss is identifiable in time, place, cause, and amount (e.g., repatriation of profits)
• Many firms (not necessarily within a country) are exposed to the risk (e.g., tariff increase)
• The occurrence of losses is independently distributed across potential firms (not necessarily within a 
country) incurring losses (e.g., war, revolution)
• The loss is outside the influence of the insured (e.g., expropriation)
If insurance is not available, firms need to manage the risk…

## Page 20

Managing Political Risk
• Understand the organization’s risk preferences 
and make these preferences shared.
• Analyze available information about the risk 
and ensure that the organization has access to 
relevant and accurate data.
• Mitigate the risk as it emerges and ensure a 
system that allows and facilitates early action.
• Learn from past events and disseminate risk-management knowledge throughout the 
organization.
Rice & Zegart (2018)

## Page 38

World Bank
• The World Bank is an international financial institution that provides 
financial and technical assistance to developing countries for development 
projects and programs. It was established in 1944 (during the Bretton 
Woods Conference).
• Aim: Reduce poverty and support sustainable development by providing 
financial resources, technical expertise, and policy advice to developing 
countries
• Structure: Two main institutions: International Bank for Reconstruction and 
Development (IBRD) & International Development Association (IDA)
• Activities:
• Financial Assistance: Provides loans, grants, and guarantees to support a 
wide range of development projects, including infrastructure, education, 
healthcare, and environmental sustainability
• Technical Assistance: Offers technical assistance and knowledge sharing 
to help countries design and implement effective development strategies

## Page 49

What Would It Take for AES to Succeed?
• Invest in goodwill: A selective focus on efficiency gains can create short-term 
financial gains but may result in long-term financial costs. Firms must be aware of the 
costs their business practices impose.
• Framing the debate: Firms need to master the art of political spin. Presenting a 
venture as “fair,” “equitable,” or “growth-enhancing” is often a simpler, more powerful 
way to secure political support.
• Finding political pressure points: The network of relationships in a society greatly 
influences policy outcomes. To turn these networks to their advantage, international 
investors must identify and engage local politicians’ power bases. 

## Page 50

Example: Invest in Goodwill
A smarter approach was used by Italian state-owned oil company Eni. After the 1998 
devaluation of the real, when many companies put their investment plans on hold or even 
exited Brazil, Eni’s then-CEO, Franco Bernabe, visited Rio de Janeiro to announce a $500 
million investment. He proclaimed: “Now is the time to show that Petrobras [the state-owned oil company] has long-term friends.” Eni and Petrobras have collaborated closely 
ever since .
Henisz & Zelner (2010)

LG Telecom—the third entrant, behind the much larger SK Telecom and Korea Telecom—

## Page 52

Example: Finding Political Pressure Points
Through its subsidiary Agip KCO, Eni has adopted a business model that responds to the 
former Soviet republic’s economic and social needs. The company favors Kazakh over non-Kazakh suppliers, and it conducts knowledge-transfer, training, and development seminars 
for them. At least 60% of local employees are Kazakh citizens. The company also funds the 
construction of various public works, including the national library, the prime minister’s 
residence, schools, computer labs, and multifamily housing units for the poor. As a result, 
many Kazakh officials now have a stake in Eni’s success.
Henisz & Zelner (2010)

Key Take-Aways from the Case (1)
Policy matters
• Extremely salient in international business management.
• Why? 
• The main things that change when you cross borders are political and social institutions: 
• Political regimes, regulations, social norms, etc.
• MNCs are large firms with sizable investments = high visibility
• Strong presence in infrastructure and extractive industries, which are very political regardless of 
ownership
• Firms have to manage political risk. 
• They can’t outsource it; they can’t insure/hedge against it.

## Page 54

Key Take-Aways from the Case (2)
Do not take on too much risk up front

• Generally, high return always means high risk.
