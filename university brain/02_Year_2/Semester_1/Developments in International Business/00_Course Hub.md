## Course at a glance
Developments in International Business focuses on how multinational enterprises (MNEs) navigate cross-border complexity. The course moves from the basic question of why MNEs exist, through where and how firms internationalize, to political risk, geopolitics, AI, and the performance consequences of international expansion.

The central skill is not memorizing isolated cases. It is learning to diagnose an international business problem systematically, select the right framework, and turn that analysis into a firm-level recommendation.

## Assessment dashboard
| Component | Weight | Format | Key implication |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Mini case assignments | 20% | Team | Weekly one-page decision analysis for non-presenting teams |
| Case discussion/debate | 20% | Team | Prepared argument, rebuttals, Q&A |
| Final exam | 60% | Individual, open book | Slides + readings; apply and compare frameworks |

**Pass condition:** final exam grade >= 5.5 **and** weighted course average >= 5.5.

## Weekly operating rhythm
1. Read the required readings before class.
2. Read the discussion case and identify the decision to be made.
3. Use the session framework(s) to diagnose the case.
4. If non-presenting: produce the one-page mini case.
5. If presenting: build an argument, anticipate rebuttals, and prepare evidence.
6. After class: update the session note and Framework Library with the generalizable insight.
7. Tag concepts that should go into the open-book exam index.

## Course north star
For every topic, be able to answer:
- **What cross-border challenge is the firm facing?**
- **Which international-business framework best explains it?**
- **What trade-offs does the framework reveal?**
- **What should management do, and why?**
- **When would that recommendation change?**
