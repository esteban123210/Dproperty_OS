## Course objectives
By the end of the course, students should be able to:
1. Understand key challenges associated with doing business across borders that arise from firms' context, including cultural differences and government policies.
2. Assess contextual issues systematically in terms of their impact on firms' geographic configuration around the world.
3. Draw strategic, firm-level implications from contextual issues, including implications for foreign-market entry, entry-mode options, and organizational design.
4. Extract generally applicable insights from individual cases and apply them to new cases.
5. Work effectively in teams and contribute to class learning through presentation, discussion, and interaction.

## Course content
The course examines the complexity and continual change of the global business environment, with a focus on multinational enterprises (MNEs). It develops frameworks for understanding the managerial processes that allow firms to operate across multiple geographies, business systems, and cultures.

A core question is what distinguishes successful MNEs from failures. Successful firms may be better able to navigate cross-country differences in politics and culture, adapt where required, and exploit differences where possible. Other firms may underestimate these obstacles or overestimate their ability to negotiate them.

The course therefore emphasizes frameworks for discussing contemporary international-management challenges and tools for producing strategy recommendations for multinational companies. Current topics in international management are linked to international business practice and applied through cases.

## Assessment
| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Mini case assignments (team) | 20% |
| Case discussion/debate (team) | 20% |
| Final individual examination (open book) | 60% |

### Mini case assignments
Each week, all teams consider the week's case. All non-presenting teams submit a short **one-page written assignment excluding references**. The assignment should prioritize the team's concerns about the business decision(s) to be made in the case, interpreted through the assigned readings. Team members' names should appear in the first-page header. Case questions are provided on Canvas.

### Case discussions/debates
From Week 2 onward, teams present different perspectives on the case and debate the merits of their proposed strategies.

Format:
- Prepared argument: 10 minutes
- Two debate rounds of rebuttals
- Question-and-answer session

Non-presenting students act as the Board of Directors / Steering Committee or other relevant decision-makers. The class questions both teams and ultimately determines the action that should be taken. Non-presenting students also give feedback on presentation form and content.

### Final exam
The final exam is open-book and uses short-answer and/or essay questions. It draws on the **slides and readings**. Material from the in-class/debated cases is **not examined directly**.

The exam assesses the ability to:
- demonstrate comprehension of course material;
- apply frameworks and theory to business challenges; and
- compare and contrast different International Business strategies and perspectives.

### Passing and resit rules
- Final exam grade must be **5.5 or higher**.
- Weighted course average must be **5.5 or higher**.
- In a resit, only the final exam can be retaken.
- Other component grades remain valid for the resit.
- If the course is not passed in the current study year, the whole course must be retaken the following year, including lectures, group/individual assignments, and final exam.

## Session overview

- Puhr, H. (2026). *The Bean of Africa: Cross-border trade, intermediaries, and the true cost of a morning cup*.

**Required**
- Ghemawat, P. (2012). *Actually the world isn't flat*. TEDGlobal 2012.
