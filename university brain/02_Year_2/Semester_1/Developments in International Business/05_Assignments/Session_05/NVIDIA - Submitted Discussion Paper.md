Developments in International Business | NVIDIA Case: Between Two Giants | PT Group 3 
Laura Barrera Vera | Nathalia Cayres Cipelli | Una Fetahagić | Andrei Naidenov | Esteban Oliviero Lopera 
I. THE STRATEGIC QUESTION & RESPONSE. NVIDIA cannot be fully neutral between the US and China. By 2025, it had 
become clear that actions taken to satisfy one government could trigger a negative response from the other. The most 
realistic strategy is therefore not neutrality, but boundary -led engagement: comply with binding laws while avoiding 
voluntary commitments that make NVIDIA a political instrument of either government. This approach is consistent with 
Chipman’s (20 16) argument that global companies need their own “foreign policy” when operating across competing 
political environments. 
II. WHAT ARE THE FACTORS THAT ENABLE A MIDDLE PATH? 
• NVIDIA is valuable to both sides. Its advanced chips and CUDA software ecosystem are difficult to replace for both 
governments. Although China’s share of NVIDIA’s revenue has fallen significantly and Huawei is becoming a stronger 
competitor, Chinese customers still have an interest in NVID IA technology. This gives NVIDIA some bargaining power 
and supports Chipman’s (2016) view that companies with unique capabilities can develop a more independent position. 
• NVIDIA depends on a global value chain. NVIDIA designs mainly in the US, relies on TSMC in Taiwan, ASML technology 
from the Netherlands, and suppliers such as SK Hynix and Samsung. China also plays an important role in critical 
materials and base minerals. This interdependence makes a complete separation costly for everyone and gives NVIDIA 
a strong reason to maintain a global rather than purely American identity. 
• There is a difference between following the law and becoming an enforcement tool. NVIDIA should fully comply 
with US export controls and customer due diligence requirements. However, where the law leaves discretion, it should 
avoid taking on broader monitoring or enforcement responsibilities. This distinction is important to the middle -path 
strategy (Chipman, 2016; Jamison and Puhr, 2026). 
• NVIDIA should continue diversifying markets and political relationships. Growing business in Europe, India, the 
Middle East and other markets will reduce dependence on either China or the US. Broad relationships with governments, 
customers, suppliers and investors can also reduce political vulnerability (Chipman, 2016). 
 
III. WHAT MAKES THE MIDDLE PATH DIFFICULT? The biggest challenge is that the US sees NVIDIA and semiconductors 
as part of its geopolitical strategy. As Mol et al. (2023) explain, multinational companies can become instruments of their 
home governments during geopolitical conflicts. At the same time, NVIDIA’s immediate financial dependence on China has 
declined significantly. This makes alignment with Washington less costly in the short term. However, leaving China could 
strengthen competitors such as Huawei and weaken NVIDIA’s long-term position world-wide, deteriorating its competitive 
advantage. Mol et al. (2023) show that withdrawing from a market can create opportunities for local competitors. NVIDIA’s 
previous experience also shows that legal compliance alone does not guarantee political neutrality. The A800, H800 and 
H20 were designed to comply with US restrictions, but this did not prevent China from putting pressure on NVIDIA and its 
customers (Jamison and Puhr, 2026). 
 
IV. RECOMMENDATION AND CONCLUSION. NVIDIA should pursue a middle path based on clear boundaries. It should: 
• Fully comply with binding laws but avoid unnecessary political monitoring or enforcement roles. 
• Diversify markets and its supply chain to reduce dependence on either country. 
• Keep decisions reversible, avoiding commitments that permanently tie NVIDIA to one side. 
• Set clear trigger points for changing strategy if US restrictions increase, China blocks NVIDIA products, Huawei 
becomes a strong substitute, or key supply chains are disrupted. 
In short, NVIDIA cannot remain politically neutral, but it can remain strategically independent. The middle path is strongest 
as a long-term competitive strategy; it protects NVIDIA’s global ecosystem, limits political dependence and keeps options 
open. As Chipman (2016) and Mol et al. (2023) suggest, in a geopolitical conflict, multinational companies need to 
understand not only market risks but also when to compl y, where to set boundaries, and how to preserve their strategic 
freedom.
