I. INDIA AS GROWTH MARKET FOR IKEA 
- Market and demand. India's furniture market was valued at US$24 billion in 2024 and is forecast to reach US$37.18 billion by 2030, 
representing a CAGR of 7.63%. Urbanization, rising disposable income, home refurbishment, and a young middle class support long -term 
demand. Rapid e-commerce growth also allows IKEA to reach customers beyond cities with large stores. 
- Consumer behavior and distance. Indian customers are highly diverse by region and income, remain price -sensitive, often prefer 
customized or traditional furniture, and commonly expect delivery and assembly rather than DIY . This creates distance from IKEA's traditional 
operating model. IKEA must localize products, price points, and services without losing its distinctive design and customer experience. 
- Product and pricing fit. IKEA has already adapted through locally relevant kitchenware, pressure cookers, lower price points, and assembly 
services. Its broad and coordinated whole-home range is a strength, but affordability must be considered in terms of the full customer cost, 
including delivery and assembly. 
- Competition and range. IKEA's advantage is not necessarily the largest assortment. Pepperfry offers a much broader online marketplace 
range of more than 125,000 products, while other competitors are more specialized: Nilkamal in moulded -plastic and value furniture, 
Featherlite in office furniture, and Forte in affordable ready -to-assemble products. Local players also benefit from deeper dealer networks 
and market knowledge. IKEA instead differentiates through an integrated range across furniture, storage, kitchens and accessories, presented 
as coordinated room and whole-home solutions. 
- Sourcing and operating model. The local-sourcing requirement creates complexity but can become a location advantage by lowering costs, 
improving local fit and developing India as a supply base. IKEA must build supplier quality, logistics and last-mile capabilities while controlling 
its brand and experience. 
- Channels and format economics. Large stores require substantial capital, accessible sites, and costly urban real estate. However, they also 
allow IKEA to demonstrate its main competitive advantage: an immersive customer experience in which complete rooms and home solutions 
make the IKEA lifestyle tangible. Large experience centers can therefore build brand awareness, trust, and customer acceptance in priority 
metropolitan areas. Small stores improve access but may not carry enough range or generate sufficient traffic, as demonstrated by the 
Mumbai closure. They should not simply reproduce a reduced version of the large -store format. Online, delivery and assembly can extend 
reach, while redesigned smaller locations can later support planning, ordering, collection and customer service once demand and 
acceptance have been established.

III. CONCLUSION 
− India should be treated as a strategically important growth market, but market growth alone does not justify rapid nationwide store expansion. 
− IKEA should maintain the overall pace of expansion while changing the mix and sequence of investment. This is not a passive s tatus-quo 
strategy: IKEA should accelerate digital reach, delivery and assembly, local sourcing and product localization, while kee ping the physical-
store rollout selective and phased. 
− IKEA should follow an "anchor -and-expand" model. Large experience centers should be opened only in priority metropolitan areas where 
demand, accessibility and site economics are attractive. Their role is to present IKEA's coordinated whole -home solutions and build 
acceptance of the IKEA lifestyle. Digital channels and services can then extend this experience across the wider metropolitan cluster. 
− Smaller locations should be introduced only after customer demand has been demonstrated. Rather than replicating the full sto re format 
with a limited range, they should focus on planning, ordering, collection, and customer service. The planned expansion into North India 
should therefore serve as a controlled test of this cluster model. Further rollout should depend on its results and losses re maining within a 
pre-agreed threshold; if that threshold is exceeded, IKEA should slow further physical expansion or even divest.
