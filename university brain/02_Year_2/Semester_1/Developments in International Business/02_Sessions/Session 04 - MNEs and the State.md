# Session 04 - MNEs and the State

**Instructor:** Harald Puhr

## Learning objective
Understand the government-related risks and uncertainties companies face regarding FDI and how these factors influence short- and long-term strategy.

## Discussion case
- Henisz, W. J., & Zelner, B. A. (2010). *AES-Telasi: Power trip or power play?*

## Required readings
- Henisz, W. J., & Zelner, B. A. (2010). The hidden risks in emerging markets. *Harvard Business Review, 88*(4), 88-95.
- Rice, C., & Zegart, A. (2018). Managing 21st century political risk. *Harvard Business Review, 96*(3), 130-138.

## Supplementary readings
- Henisz, W. J., Dorobantu, S., & Nartey, L. J. (2014). Spinning gold: The financial returns to stakeholder engagement. *Strategic Management Journal, 35*(12), 1727-1748.
- Ramamurti, R. (2001). The obsolescing 'bargaining model'? MNC-host developing country relations revisited. *Journal of International Business Studies, 32*(1), 23-39.
- Lindner & Puck (2025), Chapters 3.1, 3.3, 3.6, 5.

## Concepts to master
- Political risk and government-related uncertainty.
- FDI exposure.
- Stakeholder mapping and nonmarket strategy.
- Bargaining power between MNE and host state.
- Obsolescing bargaining logic.
- Value of stakeholder engagement.

## Pre-class questions
- Which political risks are visible and which are hidden?
- Which stakeholders can block or enable the firm's strategy?
- How does bargaining power change after an investment becomes sunk?
- Which relationships should be built before a crisis occurs?
- How should political risk change the structure or timing of FDI?
