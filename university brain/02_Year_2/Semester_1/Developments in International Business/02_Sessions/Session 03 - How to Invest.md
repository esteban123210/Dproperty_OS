# Session 03 - How to Invest

**Instructor:** Arad Ahi

## Learning objective
Evaluate how firms choose foreign-market entry modes by assessing strategic objectives, cultural and institutional distance, organizational capabilities, resource commitments, desired control, partnership opportunities, and the balance between global integration and local responsiveness.

## Discussion case
- Wang, L. L., & Liu, Z. (2024). *Popeyes in China: Making fried chicken fly in a foreign market*.

## Required readings
- Bamford, J., Baynham, G., & Ernst, D. (2020). Joint ventures and partnerships in a downturn. *Harvard Business Review, 98*(5), 116-124.
- Santos, J. F., & Williamson, P. J. (2015). The new mission for multinationals. *MIT Sloan Management Review, 56*(4), 45-54.

## Supplementary readings
- Beugelsdijk, S., Kostova, T., Kunst, V., Spadafora, E., & Van Essen, M. (2018). Cultural distance and firm internationalization: A meta-analytical review and theoretical implications. *Journal of Management, 44*(1), 89-130.
- Meyer, K., Estrin, S., Bhaumik, S., & Peng, M. (2009). Institutions, resources, and entry strategies in emerging economies. *Strategic Management Journal, 30*(1), 61-80.
- Lindner & Puck (2025), Chapters 7.4, 8.1-8.6.

## Concepts to master
- Entry-mode choice.
- Exporting, licensing/franchising, alliances/JVs, acquisitions, greenfield investment.
- Control versus commitment.
- Resource and capability fit.
- Cultural and institutional distance.
- Partner complementarity and governance.
- Global integration versus local responsiveness.

## Pre-class questions
- What does the firm need to control directly?
- What resources or capabilities does a local partner provide?
- Which risks rise with greater resource commitment?
- When does a JV outperform full ownership?
- Which activities should be globally integrated and which localized?
