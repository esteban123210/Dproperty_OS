# Session 01 - What is International Business and Why MNEs Exist

**Instructors:** Arad Ahi & Harald Puhr

## Learning objective
Set the context for the course and develop understanding of the challenges associated with cross-border activities.

## Discussion case
- Puhr, H. (2026). *The Bean of Africa: Cross-border trade, intermediaries, and the true cost of a morning cup*.

## Required reading
- Ghemawat, P. (2012). *Actually the world isn't flat*. TEDGlobal 2012.

## Supplementary reading
- Altman, S. A., Bastian, C. R., & Fattedad, D. (2024). Challenging the deglobalization narrative: Global flows have remained resilient through successive shocks. *Journal of International Business Policy, 7*(4), 416-439.
- Zaheer, S. (1995). Overcoming the liability of foreignness. *Academy of Management Journal, 38*(2), 341-363.
- Lindner, T., & Puck, J. (2025). *International Business: Mastering Strategic Challenges in the 21st Century*. Chapters 2, 7.1, 7.2.

## Concepts to master
- What makes international business meaningfully different from domestic business?
- Why do firms cross borders?
- Semi-globalization versus the idea of a flat world.
- Liability of foreignness.
- Cross-border frictions, intermediaries, and hidden transaction costs.

## Pre-class questions
- What evidence challenges the claim that the world is economically 'flat'?
- Where do borders still matter most for firms?
- When can foreignness become a disadvantage?
- What would justify internalizing an activity across borders instead of relying on market intermediaries?
