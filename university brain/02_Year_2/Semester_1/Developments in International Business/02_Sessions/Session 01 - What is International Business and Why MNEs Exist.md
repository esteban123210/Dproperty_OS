## Core Session 1 question
> If borders create real costs and markets are imperfect, why and when does it make sense for firms to organize economic activity across countries rather than simply transact through markets?

## Conceptual chain

### 1. International Business = cross-border complexity
International Business concerns firms engaging in cross-border economic activity. Crossing borders adds cultural, institutional, political, geographic, informational, and coordination complexity.

### 2. The world is not flat
Ghemawat shows that people systematically overestimate the proportion of activity occurring across borders. The world is internationalized, but far from borderless.

**Key idea:** semi-globalization.

### 3. The world is not broadly deglobalizing either
Altman et al. update the evidence and show that international flows remain resilient. The correct picture is not 'everything is global' or 'globalization is ending,' but a world where international integration remains important while geography, institutions, geopolitics, and country differences still structure flows.

### 4. Borders become firm-level costs
Zaheer's **liability of foreignness (LOF)** explains why foreign firms can face additional costs that local firms avoid: unfamiliarity, distance, legitimacy problems, and home/host-country constraints.

### 5. Foreign firms therefore need an advantage
The slides connect LOF to **firm-specific advantages (FSAs)**: technology, brand, know-how, organizational capabilities, distribution skills, access to capital/resources, etc.

A useful simplified condition is:

> **International expansion is sustainable when transferable firm-specific advantages can outweigh the additional costs of operating abroad.**

### 6. Markets themselves create transaction costs
The Bean of Africa case makes the problem concrete: payments, logistics, intermediaries, customs, documentation, communication, and uncertainty all make a seemingly simple transaction expensive and fragile.

### 7. Why MNEs can exist: markets vs hierarchy
The slides introduce Coase/Williamson logic. Every transaction has costs beyond the price. Firms compare organizing the activity through an external market ('buy') versus internal coordination ('make').

When market contracting is sufficiently costly or unreliable, internalization can be more efficient. In an international setting, this can help explain why a firm establishes foreign subsidiaries, integrates parts of its value chain, or uses FDI rather than relying only on arm's-length exporting.

## Essential frameworks and definitions

### International Business
Business activity crossing national borders; managerial challenge = added complexity and uncertainty.

### Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)
Investment that creates a lasting interest and an effective managerial voice/control in an enterprise outside the investor's home economy.

### Liability of Foreignness (LOF)
Additional costs/disadvantages a foreign firm faces relative to local firms.

### Firm-Specific Advantage (FSA)
A transferable capability/resource that can offset the disadvantage of being foreign.

### Transaction costs
Costs of using the market beyond the purchase price: search, negotiation, monitoring, enforcement, information, coordination, payment, logistics, compliance, etc.

### Internalization
Bringing an activity inside the firm's hierarchy when internal coordination is more efficient than external market contracting.

### Vertical integration
Ownership/control of additional upstream or downstream activities in the value chain.

## Evidence worth remembering
### Ghemawat (2012)
- Cross-border voice calls: about 2% (roughly 6-7% including internet telephony in the talk's data).
- First-generation immigrants: about 3% of world population.
- FDI: less than 10% of real investment in 2010.
- Corrected export/GDP estimate in the talk: likely below 20% rather than 30%+.
- Foreign Facebook friendships: roughly 10-15%.

**Meaning:** cross-border activity matters, but people greatly overestimate how close the world is to a no-border benchmark.

### Altman et al. (2024)
- Aggregate international activity remained at/near record levels after the pandemic recovery.
- No robust general shift from global to regional activity.
- U.S.-China partial decoupling and Russia's reorientation are real but do not yet imply universal bloc fragmentation.
- Country-level globalization patterns differ substantially.

### Zaheer (1995)
- Foreign trading rooms showed evidence of lower performance than comparable local operations.
- When advantage is embedded in organizational capabilities, importing strong parent-company routines can outperform blindly imitating local practices.
- Adaptation should therefore be selective rather than automatic.

## Bean of Africa decision lens
Do not ask only: **Is Ugandan coffee cheaper / attractive?**

Ask:
1. What are the total transaction costs?
2. Which intermediaries solve which frictions?
3. Which frictions are recurring rather than one-off?
4. Which activities should remain outsourced?
5. Which capabilities would need to be internalized at scale?
6. What FSA could a European importer build that makes it better than an individual buyer?

## Generalizable takeaway
> International strategy starts from the fact that borders are neither irrelevant nor absolute barriers. Firms internationalize successfully when they possess advantages worth transferring across borders and choose governance structures that manage the resulting transaction costs and foreignness disadvantages better than available alternatives.

## Exam-ready summary
**Core claim:** International business exists in a semi-globalized world where borders create real frictions. MNEs need FSAs to overcome liability of foreignness and may internalize cross-border activities when market transaction costs are high.  
**Frameworks:** semi-globalization; LOF/FSA; transaction-cost economics; markets vs hierarchy; FDI/internalization.  
**Use when:** explaining why a firm internationalizes, why a foreign unit underperforms, or why a firm should export/contract versus own/control foreign activities.  
**Compare with:** Session 2 will move from 'why internationalize?' to 'where to invest?'.
