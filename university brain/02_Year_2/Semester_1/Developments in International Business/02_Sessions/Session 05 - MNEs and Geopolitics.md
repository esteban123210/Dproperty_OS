# Session 05 - MNEs and Geopolitics

**Instructor:** Harald Puhr

## Learning objective
Understand how the re-emergence of geopolitical tensions changes the nature of political risk and how this affects MNE strategies.

## Discussion case
- Jamison, A., & Puhr, H. (2026). *Between two giants: Nvidia and the bilateral political risk of semiconductors*.

## Required readings
- Chipman, J. (2016). Why your company needs a foreign policy. *Harvard Business Review, 94*(9), 36-43.
- Mol, M. J., Rabbiosi, L., & Santangelo, G. D. (2023). Should I stay or should I go? How Danish MNEs in Russia respond to a geopolitical shift. *AIB Insights, 23*(1).

## Supplementary readings
- Estrin, S., & Hancke, B. (2026). How geopolitics, geoeconomics, and new organizational models are changing the world of business. *Journal of International Business Policy, 9*(2), 254-266.
- Meyer, K. E., & Thein, H. H. (2014). The case of international sanctions against Myanmar. *Journal of World Business, 49*(1), 156-171.
- Lindner & Puck (2025), Chapters 3.1, 3.3, 3.6, 5, 12.3.

## Concepts to master
- Geopolitical risk versus traditional country political risk.
- Bilateral and networked political exposure.
- Geoeconomics.
- Sanctions and export controls.
- Corporate foreign policy.
- Stay / scale down / suspend / exit decisions.
- Strategic dependence and supply-chain exposure.

## Pre-class questions
- How is geopolitical risk different from a host-country political-risk problem?
- Which relationships between states create risk for the firm?
- Where is the company strategically dependent on a country, technology, supplier, or market?
- What are the costs of staying versus exiting?
- What would a coherent corporate foreign policy look like?
