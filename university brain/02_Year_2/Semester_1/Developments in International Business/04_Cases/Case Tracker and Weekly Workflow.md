## Weekly case workflow

### 1. Define the decision
Write the decision as a management question that requires action.

**Bad:** What problems does IKEA face in India?  
**Better:** Should IKEA accelerate expansion in India using its existing format, deepen localization first, or alter its geographic / format strategy?

### 2. Separate facts from interpretation
Create three buckets:
- **Case facts** - directly supported by the case.
- **Framework implications** - what the readings suggest the facts mean.
- **Assumptions** - things we need to believe for the recommendation to work.

### 3. Prioritize 2-4 decisive issues
The one-page assignment is too short for exhaustive analysis. Rank issues based on:
- impact on the decision;
- uncertainty;
- irreversibility / commitment;
- fit with the week's readings.

### 4. Apply frameworks as decision tools
Do not describe the framework and then describe the case separately. Use the framework to compare alternatives.

### 5. Build alternatives
For each realistic strategic option, capture:
| Option | Upside | Downside | Assumptions | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

### 6. Recommend and specify conditions
A strong recommendation includes:
- what to do;
- why this option dominates;
- what must be true;
- what to monitor;
- what would trigger a change in strategy.

### 7. Prepare the counterargument
Before submission/debate, write the strongest argument against your own recommendation.

## After-class extraction
The cases themselves are not directly examined, but the **generalizable lessons are valuable**. After every case, record:
- Which framework proved most useful?
- What did the losing argument underestimate?
- Which fact changed the recommendation most?
- What principle would transfer to another industry or country?
- What comparison could appear in an exam essay?
