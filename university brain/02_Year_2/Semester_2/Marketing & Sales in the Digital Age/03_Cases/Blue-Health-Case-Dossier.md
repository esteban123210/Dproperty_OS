# Blue Health Case Dossier

## Case identity

The uploaded Phase 1 diagnostic identifies **Blue Health Intelligence B.V.**, a preventive-health clinic in Amsterdam, with consumer-facing brand **Blue**. The project materials do not describe the U.S. Blue Health Intelligence analytics company owned by Blue Cross Blue Shield plans. Keep the two organizations separate.

The facts below summarize the uploaded company introduction and Phase 1 diagnostic; they are not independently verified in this note.

## Offer and experience

- A self-paid 360-degree preventive health check priced at €299; the company intro rounds this to €300.
- About one hour at the Amsterdam clinic.
- Ten tests, including ECG, carotid ultrasound, blood and urine analysis, and a 32-camera 3D body scan, according to the supplied materials.
- Results discussed with a BIG-registered doctor; an app-based result and action plan are stated to follow within 24 hours.
- Referrals or next-step advice are provided if follow-up care is needed; the clinic is not presented as a treatment provider.
- The diagnostic describes the differentiator primarily as service design, convenience, customer experience, and price—not a unique clinical technology.

## Customer and market hypotheses

The documents do not agree on one primary segment:

- Sep 17/24 deck: health-conscious Amsterdam expats aged 25-45.
- First-pass canvas: health-conscious Amsterdam professionals around 28-45, with gym members and expats as possible indicators.
- Phase 1 diagnostic: affluent urban consumers aged 35-55, entrepreneurs/zzp'ers, expats, and gift-buyers; employers as a possible second channel.

Treat these as hypotheses, not a settled segment. The founder interview should establish who actually books, pays, uses, repeats, and refers.

## Current journey described in the files

Awareness via public relations, founder content, social/search, and some partnerships; online booking; an in-person one-hour visit; doctor discussion; results/action plan in the app; possible annual re-test or referral. The current experience appears stronger through the visit than after it. The uploaded materials do not establish how many customers use the app between visits or return the following year.

## Central strategic tension

The brand and project framing promise preventive insight and ongoing health awareness. The present product is described as a single €299 check, and annual repeat behaviour is not evidenced. The first-pass canvas calls the gap between transfer/delivery and lifetime value the main disconnect. This is a strong candidate for the final diagnosis, pending founder data.

## Evidence gaps that matter most

1. Customer age, gender, income, location, segment and payer mix.
2. Bookings per week, capacity utilization, no-shows, conversion, and wait time.
3. Acquisition channel mix, cost per booking, referral and gift-card contribution.
4. Price tests, package choices, willingness to pay, and employer/insurer discussions.
5. Repeat intent, actual repeat rate, app engagement, and action-plan completion.
6. Referral acceptance and follow-through with GPs/specialists.
7. Current AI use, data access, consent, clinical review, and desired future use.
8. Unit economics: revenue, variable cost, clinician capacity, CAC, and contribution margin.

## Proposed analytical stance

The company may be selling peace of mind and convenient interpretation as much as a clinical service. Test the value and trust claims carefully, distinguish prevention from diagnosis/treatment, and avoid implying proven health outcomes unless the evidence supports them.
