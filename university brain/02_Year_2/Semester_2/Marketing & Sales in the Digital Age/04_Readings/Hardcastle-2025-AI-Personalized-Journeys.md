# Understanding Customer Responses to AI-Driven Personalized Journeys

**Authors:** Hardcastle, Vorster, and Brown (2025)  
**Journal:** *Journal of Advertising*, 54(2)  
**Source in this workspace:** User-provided condensed reading; consult the original article for formal citation and direct quotations.

## Main argument

AI personalization can make discovery easier by improving relevance, speed, or variety. It can also repeat ads, narrow choice, misread a person, or make the customer feel watched. The balance between value and control changes across the customer journey. People may accept data use when exploring, then become more sensitive to pressure, unavailable offers, or data misuse around purchase, loyalty, and advocacy.

The study uses journey maps and interviews with 20 consumers across discovery, consideration, conversion, loyalty, and advocacy. It offers qualitative insight into mechanisms and tensions, not a statistical estimate that applies to all customers.

## Relevance to Blue

- Personalization should help a customer understand an offered check, book, or follow an agreed action plan rather than pressure them into unnecessary tests.
- Use clear, limited follow-up that a customer can pause or opt out of.
- Avoid sensitive-data retargeting or recommendations that imply a diagnosis.
- Treat the brand as responsible for the whole experience when an algorithm makes a poor recommendation.

## Use in the assignment

Apply the article at specific journey stages. Identify the customer value, the data used, the possible loss of autonomy, and the control offered. Combine automation with honest messaging and human support; personalization does not replace brand reputation, peer recommendations, helpful employees, or good service.
