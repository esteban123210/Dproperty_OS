# Dynamic Pricing

**Authors:** Kopalle, Pauwels, Akella, and Gangwar (2023)  
**Journal:** *Journal of Retailing*, 99  
**Source in this workspace:** User-provided condensed reading; consult the original article and figures for full bibliographic details.

## Main argument

Dynamic pricing is broader than real-time automated price changes. Prices can respond to demand differences across the “4 Ps”:

1. **People** - different customers have different willingness to pay.
2. **Products** - configurations or bundles create different value.
3. **Periods** - demand and urgency change over time.
4. **Places** - demand differs by location or channel.

The conceptual review explains how data and technology can support finer demand estimates while creating risks around fairness, privacy, algorithmic behaviour, complexity, and long-term trust. It combines prior research, cases, and simplified illustrations rather than testing one new pricing system.

## Relevance to Blue

The useful assignment question is not simply “Can Blue use AI to charge each person a different price?” A better question is whether transparent tests of **offer design** (for example, self-pay, employer-paid, or follow-up options) can reveal where value and willingness to pay differ. Segment-level experiments are easier to explain and govern than prices inferred from personal health data.

Consider fairness and trust alongside conversion, contribution margin, and repeat purchase. Keep customer eligibility and price logic transparent, and do not infer sensitive willingness to pay from health or vulnerability data.

## Use in the assignment

Use the 4 Ps to organize pricing hypotheses, then identify the evidence needed for each one. Distinguish a conceptual framework from proof that Blue should deploy dynamic pricing.
