## How to Use These Profiles

### PHASE 1: Team Assignment (This Week)

1. **Each person reads all 5 profiles**
2. **Ask yourself:** "Which character feels natural to me? Where do I actually think?"
3. **Answer honestly** (not based on what the story needs, but based on genuine fit)
4. **Assign roles** using the Casting Checklist in the Overview document

**Goal:** Each person should say "I could be myself in this role." If not, reassign.

---

### PHASE 2: Individual Preparation (Before the Trip)

**If you're assigned the Narrator:**
- [ ] Read Character Profile 01
- [ ] Practice speaking the opening/closing lines (not memorizing, but familiarizing)
- [ ] Understand your role: you've already integrated the lesson
- [ ] Prepare 4–5 short voice-overs you'll record post-trip

**If you're assigned the Main Character:**
- [ ] Read Character Profile 02
- [ ] Choose/prepare your notebook (beautiful, high-quality)
- [ ] Practice being vulnerable on camera
- [ ] Understand your 5-day evolution (confused → integrated)
- [ ] You're on camera the most; be ready for this level of presence

**If you're assigned the Serious Character:**
- [ ] Read Character Profile 03
- [ ] Prepare concept explanations (not scripts, but talking points)
  - Deep Tech definition and why it matters
  - Station F as designed serendipity
  - Structure as enabling (not limiting)
- [ ] Understand your growth: structure is necessary, not sufficient
- [ ] Be ready to debate Messy Character respectfully

**If you're assigned the Messy Character:**
- [ ] Read Character Profile 04
- [ ] Understand your domain: informal moments, trust, timing
- [ ] Prepare to bring authentic social energy
- [ ] Be ready to debate Serious Character with respect
- [ ] Friday (free time + dinner) is YOUR showcase day
- [ ] Energy is your superpower—bring it

**If you're assigned the Seasoned Character:**
- [ ] Read Character Profile 05
- [ ] Understand: You're an observer for 4 days, then deliver the climax
- [ ] Learn the final café dialogue word-for-word (but don't memorize—internalize)
- [ ] Be ready for the most important scene in the video
- [ ] Your calm presence matters as much as your words

---

### PHASE 3: During the Trip

**Each morning:** Check your character's focus for that day (see Shooting Schedule)

**Example (Tuesday):**
- Serious Character is the primary focus
- Main Character is secondary
- Messy Character brings social energy at lunch
- Seasoned Character is observing quietly

**Stay in character but stay authentic.** You're not acting. You're being yourself in a role that fits you.

---

### PHASE 4: Filming Specific Scenes

**For Concept Explanations (Serious Character):**
- Find a quiet space
- Camera steady (tripod)
- External mic recording clean audio
- Speak clearly, not rushed
- Let pauses breathe
- Explain as to a smart friend, not a classroom
- Multiple takes; choose the best

**For Social Moments (Messy Character):**
- Be yourself
- Bring genuine energy
- Connect people naturally
- Don't perform—be present
- Camera should follow your movement
- Capture authentic conversations

**For the Final Café Scene (Seasoned + Main):**
- This is NON-NEGOTIABLE
- Two cameras, external mic, tripod
- Record full scene multiple times
- Let pauses land
- Every word matters; follow the script
- Main Character writes final line: "Entrepreneurship = connection"
- This is your climax; protect it

---

### PHASE 5: Post-Trip Assembly

**Narrator:**
- Record voice-overs (opening, bridges, closing)
- Provide clean dialogue audio for editing

**Main Character:**
- Ensure notebook journey is visually clear (first page → final page)
- Provide best takes of processing moments
- Help editor see your arc

**Serious Character:**
- Provide best takes of concept explanations
- Help editor cut them clearly
- These are Learning Integration moments (30% of grade)

**Messy Character:**
- Provide best takes of social/informal moments
- Help editor capture the informal ecosystem
- Show why relationships matter

**Seasoned Character:**
- Ensure final café scene is pristine
- Provide best audio of the dialogue
- This is the climax; treat it as such

## Filming Tips for Each Character

### Filming the Narrator
- Record voice-overs in quiet space
- Multiple takes of each line
- Conversational, not performed
- Let pauses breathe
- Use good microphone (audio quality matters)

### Filming the Main Character
- Lots of camera presence (they're on most)
- Close-ups of face (emotions matter)
- Frequent notebook shots (visual motif)
- Longer takes on processing
- Longer takes on final café
- Warm light, approachable framing

### Filming the Serious Character
- Direct camera when explaining
- Wide shots during formal visits
- Close-ups of notes (show structured thinking)
- Dialogue shots with others
- Natural light, thoughtful framing
- Multiple takes of concept explanations

### Filming the Messy Character
- Move with them (they're in motion)
- Capture their eyes (emotional intelligence)
- Group shots often (they're with people)
- Fast cuts, liveliness
- Natural light, warm tones
- Authentic conversations over staged moments

### Filming the Seasoned Character
- Background presence for 4 days (wide shots, quiet moments)
- Two cameras for the café scene
- Close-ups of listening
- Stability and tripod for the climax
- External mic (crystal clear audio)
- Multiple takes, let pauses breathe
