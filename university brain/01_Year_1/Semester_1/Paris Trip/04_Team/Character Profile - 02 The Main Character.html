Core Character Description

The Main Character is the emotional entry point. They arrive in Paris believing entrepreneurship can be defined neatly, then slowly discover that the answer is richer, messier, and more human than expected.

  • Curious
  • Observant
  • Vulnerable
  • Creative
  • Emotionally intelligent
  • Open to change

Worldview

Starting belief: entrepreneurship is probably about starting a company, having a great idea, raising money, and scaling.

Entrepreneurship isn't one thing. It's a system where structure, people, culture, and capital dance together.

Voice & Personality

  • Honest, direct, conversational, and unafraid to say “I don't know.”
  • Uses questions more than declarations: “So entrepreneurship is...?”
  • Shows visible confusion, curiosity, surprise, and integration.
  • Gets more confident as the week progresses.

Example Lines

I thought I knew what entrepreneurship was. Paris made me uncertain.
So... this is entrepreneurship? Long timelines, patient capital, science-based? That's very different from what I imagined.
I'm more confused now than I was on Monday. But... it's a good confused. Like I'm getting closer to something real.
Entrepreneurship = connection.

5-Day Evolution

Tuesday · Naive confidence: arrives with a definition and neat notes.
Wednesday · First crack: cultural innovation challenges the model.
Thursday · Complexity: diagrams, arrows, and “???” appear.
Friday · Paradox: structure and relationships both become valid.
Saturday · Integration: the final notebook page says “Entrepreneurship = connection.”

Interactions

Narrator

Witness and guide. The Narrator frames what the Main Character experiences.

Serious Academic

Student-teacher dynamic, but collaborative. The Main Character asks real questions.

Messy Networking

Mirror and dialogue partner. The Messy Character validates uncertainty and intuition.

Seasoned

Student and wisdom-giver. The final café scene validates the journey.

Filming Guidance

  • Capture processing moments with notebook, coffee, and quiet thinking.
  • Use close-ups of face and notebook; the emotion matters.
  • Follow the eye line: what is the character noticing?
  • Use warm, golden light to support openness and vulnerability.

The Notebook as Character

The notebook visualizes the transformation: neat definitions early, then crossed-out notes and diagrams, then final synthesis. It should appear throughout the film as the visible record of learning.

Summary

The Main Character succeeds if viewers say: “I related to them.” Their confusion must feel real, their growth must feel earned, and the final realization must land authentically.
