## Assignment 1: Individual Analytical Brief

### 📌 Basics

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Format** | 1,000-word analytical brief |
| **Length** | Main brief + optional appendix (max 7 pages total) |
| **Deadline** | July 19, 2026 |
| **Weight** | 60% of final grade |
| **Submission** | [Digital format TBD] |

### 📖 Core Task

Write a **compact, analytical** document comparing the French and Dutch startup ecosystems.

**This is NOT:**
- A travel diary
- A description of the trip
- A list of what you visited
- A tourism review

**This IS:**
- An argument based on observations and evidence
- A comparison of ecosystems
- A strategic analysis of startup support systems
- Recommendations for ecosystem builders

### 🎯 Required Components

#### 1. Question & Relevance (~150 words)
- **A clear guiding question** — What is your central question?
- **Why it matters** — For founders? Investors? Policymakers? Ecosystem builders?

**Example questions:**
- What can Amsterdam learn from Paris about supporting deep tech startups?
- How do Paris and Amsterdam differ in their approach to startup infrastructure?
- Is Paris better positioned than Amsterdam for science-based entrepreneurship?
- How do cultural institutions contribute to entrepreneurial ecosystems?
- What role do universities play in turning research into startups?
- Are startup ecosystems built more through institutions or informal networks?

#### 2. Analysis & Evidence (~650 words)
- **The core argument** — This is the bulk of your brief
- **Evidence from:**
  - Site visits
  - Course sessions
  - Program observations
  - Ecosystem concepts
  - External data (if available)
  - Personal field reflections

**Possible analytical dimensions:**
- Funding and capital availability
- Deep tech support
- Startup hubs and infrastructure
- Incubators and accelerators
- University talent pipelines
- Research commercialization
- Cultural and creative innovation
- Government support systems
- International access
- Founder networks
- Market size
- City attractiveness
- Informal networking culture

#### 3. Recommendations (~200 words)
- **Specific and actionable recommendations**
- **For whom:** Amsterdam, Paris, founders, investors, universities, policymakers

**Example recommendations:**
- Amsterdam should strengthen bridges between universities and deep tech startups
- Paris should improve international accessibility for foreign founders
- Both ecosystems should formalize cross-border founder exchange programs
- Incubators should create more structured post-program alumni networks
- MBA programs should treat informal ecosystem observation as part of entrepreneurial learning
- Policymakers should see culture, lifestyle, and social spaces as part of startup infrastructure

#### 4. Self-Evaluation (1-2 paragraphs)
- **Honest reflection** on your work
- **Grade you believe it deserves** — and why
- **Strengths of your argument**
- **Limitations or gaps you recognize**

#### 5. AI Disclosure (1 paragraph)
- **How you used AI tools** (if at all)
- **What AI was used for:**
  - Brainstorming
  - Structure and outline
  - Script or text drafting
  - Research organization
  - Writing support
- **What was 100% your own thinking and observation**

**Example AI paragraph:**
"We used AI tools to support brainstorming, narrative development, script drafting, and the organization of field observations. AI was also used to help translate site visit insights into entrepreneurship concepts such as deep tech, social capital, ecosystem density, and knowledge transfer. All final interpretations, examples, footage, and conclusions were reviewed by the team and grounded in our own observations from the Paris trip."

### ✅ Evaluation Criteria

Your brief will be assessed on:

| Criterion | What it means |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Analytical Depth** | Do you understand the ecosystems? Are your comparisons nuanced? |
| **Actionable Recommendations** | Are your recommendations specific and implementable? |
| **Integration of Course Insights** | Have you used what you learned from visits and sessions? |
| **Structure & Clarity** | Is the argument clear? Is it well-organized? |
| **Originality** | Is this your own thinking, or generic observations? |
| **Honest Self-Evaluation** | Do you understand your own work's strengths and limitations? |

## Assignment 2: Group Video Journal

### 📌 Basics

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Format** | Video (MP4 or similar) |
| **Length** | 5–7 minutes |
| **Resolution** | 1080p, 16:9 (preferred) |
| **Subtitles** | Encouraged |
| **Deadline** | 72 hours after returning to Amsterdam |
| **Weight** | 40% of final grade |
| **Grading** | 40% peer votes + 60% faculty votes |

### 📖 Core Task

Create a **video documentary** of your learning journey in Paris.

**This is NOT:**
- A travel vlog
- Highlight reel of tourist spots
- Slideshow of photos
- Simple tour footage

**This IS:**
- A short film with narrative structure
- Storytelling about entrepreneurship
- Integration of site visits with analysis
- Team learning journey
- Creative cinematography

### 🎯 Must-Have Components

#### 1. Footage from 3+ Site Visits
**Minimum requirements:**
- Hello Tomorrow
- Station F
- WILCO

**Stronger version includes:**
- École des Mines
- Le19M / POUSH
- Seine visit or opening drinks
- Closing dinner

**For each site, capture:**
- Exterior and signage
- Team arriving
- Speaker or guide
- Room or space
- Q&A moments
- Notes being taken
- One reflection afterward

#### 2. Field Reflections Throughout
**Include reflections:**
- After Hello Tomorrow
- After Le19M / POUSH
- After Station F
- After WILCO
- During opening drinks
- During closing dinner
- At final breakfast

**Format:** Direct to camera, short lines, or voiceover

#### 3. Every Team Member Appears
**Requirements:**
- Each person appears on screen OR provides voice-over
- At least once during the 5–7 minute video
- Can be dialogue, narration, or appearance

#### 4. Clear Learning Narrative
**Your video should show:**
- A journey (beginning → middle → end)
- Evolution of understanding
- Real questions and real answers
- Transformation or "aha" moments
- Connection to entrepreneurship concepts

#### 5. Analytical Connection to Entrepreneurship
**Your video must show:**
- Understanding of ecosystems
- Use of course concepts:
  - Deep Tech
  - Knowledge Transfer
  - Cultural Innovation
  - Startup Hub
  - Accelerator
  - Social Capital
  - Ecosystem Thinking
- Evidence-based analysis, not just travel

#### 6. Professional Production Quality
**Minimums:**
- Clean audio (no loud background noise)
- Stable shots (tripod or steady hand)
- Good lighting (use natural light when possible)
- Coherent color palette (not wildly different)
- Subtitles (or very clear dialogue)
- Logical editing (cuts, transitions feel intentional)

### 📊 Faculty Scoring Rubric

Faculty votes are worth 60% of your grade.

| Criterion | Weight | What faculty looks for |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| **Learning Integration** | 30% | Do you understand the startup ecosystem? Do you use concepts correctly? |
| **Narrative & Structure** | 25% | Is there a clear story? Does it have a beginning, middle, end? |
| **Creativity & Impact** | 25% | Is it visually interesting? Does it engage the viewer? |
| **Production Quality** | 20% | Is the audio clear? Are the shots stable? Is editing clean? |

### 🎬 Suggested Narrative Approach

#### "What Is Entrepreneurship?"

A main character arrives in Paris with a question: **"What is entrepreneurship?"**

Different characters give different answers:

1. **Serious Academic Entrepreneur**
   - Shows the official ecosystem
   - Explains deep tech, infrastructure, capital
   - Likes frameworks and definitions

2. **Messy Networking Entrepreneur**
   - Shows informal moments
   - Emphasizes trust, relationships, serendipity
   - Likes stories and people

3. **Seasoned Entrepreneur**
   - Appears at the end
   - Synthesizes both perspectives
   - Final wisdom: entrepreneurship = structure + people

4. **Main Character**
   - Asks questions
   - Evolves understanding
   - Learns from both sides

5. **Narrator**
   - Ties the journey together
   - Reflects on what was learned
   - Speaks directly to camera occasionally

**Story Arc:**
- Open: Main character asks the question
- Formal Ecosystem: Serious character shows official infrastructure
- Informal Ecosystem: Messy character shows relationships and trust
- Synthesis: Seasoned entrepreneur explains why both matter
- Close: Main character realizes entrepreneurship = ecosystem

### 📹 Peer Review (40% of grade)

After upload, peer review begins within 48 hours.

**Peer reviewers will vote on:**
- Does the video show learning?
- Is it well-made?
- Did the team appear to work well together?
- Would you watch this again?

## Comparison: Amsterdam vs. Paris

**Both assignments should use the comparison:**

**Paris Strengths:**
- Strong visible startup infrastructure (Station F)
- Institutional depth (universities, deep tech support)
- Cultural and creative power
- Dense city networking
- Large domestic market

**Amsterdam Strengths:**
- International openness
- English-friendly environment
- Pragmatic experimentation
- Strong sustainability focus
- Agile business culture
- Access to European markets
- High quality of life
- Less hierarchical culture

**Thesis idea:**
Paris and Amsterdam are **complementary** ecosystems, not just competitors.

## Tips for Success

### For the Individual Brief
✅ Start with a strong question  
✅ Back every claim with evidence from the trip  
✅ Use specific examples (not generic statements)  
✅ Compare, don't just describe  
✅ Make recommendations actionable  
✅ Be honest about limitations  

### For the Video
✅ Plan your narrative before filming  
✅ Capture footage at every site (more than you think you need)  
✅ Record good audio separately if possible  
✅ Film reflections while observations are fresh  
✅ Include everyone in the frame at least once  
✅ Edit ruthlessly (every shot should serve the story)  
✅ Add subtitles for clarity
