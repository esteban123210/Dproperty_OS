# 🎬 Video Creative Direction: "What Is Entrepreneurship?"

**A short cinematic field journal about the Paris startup ecosystem**

---

## Executive Summary

**Working Title:** What Is Entrepreneurship?

**Core Concept:** Instead of a travel vlog, we create a short film where the Paris trip becomes a story about one central question: *What is entrepreneurship, really?*

**Duration:** 5–7 minutes  
**Team Size:** 5 members  
**Approach:** Cinematic, analytical, playful, reflective  
**Visual Style:** Wes-Anderson-inspired Paris aesthetic (symmetry, pastels, summer light)  

**The Thesis:** 
Entrepreneurship is not one thing. It's an ecosystem where structure and relationships, analysis and intuition, institutions and networks coexist and strengthen each other.

---

## 1. Core Narrative Idea

### The Central Question
A main character arrives in Paris trying to answer one question:
> "What is entrepreneurship, really?"

### The Journey
Throughout the trip, different characters give her different answers:
- **The Serious Academic Entrepreneur:** Entrepreneurship is structure, institutions, capital, deep tech, product-market fit.
- **The Messy Networking Entrepreneur:** Entrepreneurship is relationships, trust, conversations, timing, knowing who to call.
- **The Seasoned Entrepreneur:** Both are true, but incomplete. Entrepreneurship is the ecosystem that connects them.

### The Resolution
**Final Message:**
> "Entrepreneurship is money, networking, creativity, structure, risk, failure, timing, culture, and trust. Innovation thrives when all of these things coexist."

**Short version:**
> "Entrepreneurship is not one person, one place, or one method. It is an ecosystem."

- Occasional voice-over

## 4. Story Structure: Six Chapters

### CHAPTER 1: Arrival in Paris — The Question
**Duration:** ~1 minute  
**Theme:** Setup, team introduction, central question

**Opening:**
- Black screen
- Narrator: "Five students left Amsterdam with one question. It sounded simple. It was not."
- Title card appears: "What Is Entrepreneurship?"

**Team Formation Slide:**
- Show professor's team formation slide (5 members, hacker/hipster/hustler mix)
- Narrator: "The professor gave us one slide about team formation. Five members. Ideally, not all the same. One hacker. One hipster. One hustler. It looked like administration. It was actually the plot."
- Introduce each of the five team members

**The Question:**
- Main character arrives in Paris
- Main character: "What is entrepreneurship, really?"
- Serious character: "Start with structure."
- Messy character: "Start with coffee."

**Tension:** Sets up the central conflict — structure vs. relationships

---

### CHAPTER 2: The Official Ecosystem — Structure
**Duration:** ~2.5 minutes  
**Theme:** Formal visits, analysis, frameworks, institutions

**Guided by:** The Serious Academic Entrepreneur  
**Scenes:** Site visits with learning hooks

#### École des Mines (if included)
**Learning Hook:**
```
KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER
Universities turn talent and research
into entrepreneurial potential.
```
**Serious character line:** "Entrepreneurship begins before the company. It starts with talent."

#### Hello Tomorrow
**Learning Hook:**
```
DEEP TECH
Science-based innovation that needs time,
capital, and credibility.
```
**Serious character line:** "Some ideas do not need hype first. They need proof."

#### Le19M / POUSH
**Learning Hook:**
```
CULTURAL INNOVATION
Craft, identity, and creativity
as sources of economic value.
```
**Main character line:** "This does not look like startups."  
**Serious character response:** "It is still innovation."

#### Station F
**Learning Hook:**
```
STARTUP HUB
A place where founders, investors, mentors,
and support systems meet.
```
**Main character line:** "It feels like a city inside a building."  
**Serious character response:** "Exactly. Density creates access."

#### WILCO
**Learning Hook:**
```
ACCELERATOR
A structured support program that helps
startups test, refine, and grow faster.
```
**Serious character line:** "Startups do not grow alone. They need feedback, pressure, mentorship, and structure."

**Chapter Arc:** Main character processes formal ecosystem. Fills notebook with definitions. Begins to see entrepreneurship as systematic.

---

### CHAPTER 3: The Informal Ecosystem — People
**Duration:** ~1.5 minutes  
**Theme:** After-hours learning, networking, relationships, social capital

**Guided by:** The Messy Networking Entrepreneur  
**Setting:** Bar or restaurant

**The Transition Scene:**
Main character sits in a café with her notebook, trying to process everything she's learned.

She writes: "Entrepreneurship = structure?"

Messy character appears.

**Dialogue:**
- Main: "I have definitions. But none of them breathe."
- Messy: "That is because you wrote them too neatly."
- Main: "I was told entrepreneurship is structure."
- Messy: "It is. Until people get involved."
- Main: "And then?"
- Messy: "Then it becomes trust. Timing. Introductions. Bad pitches. Good conversations. Someone who knows someone."
- Messy: "The real ecosystem starts when the official meeting ends."

**Learning Hook:**
```
SOCIAL CAPITAL
The value created through relationships,
trust, and networks.
```

**Supporting Footage:**
- Networking in bars and restaurants
- Informal team conversations
- Laughter and playful moments
- Coffee and croissants
- People connecting

**Chapter Arc:** Main character realizes structure alone is not enough. Informal relationships are infrastructure too.

---

### CHAPTER 4: The Seine / Reflection — The Conflict
**Duration:** ~1 minute  
**Theme:** Processing and integrating opposing views

**Settings:**
- Opening drinks along the Seine
- Boat tour or Seine walk
- Summer evening light
- Team moments

**Visual Focus:**
- Water and bridges
- Paris skyline
- Summer light on stone
- Team in conversation
- Details (coffee cups, notebook, glasses)

**Short Reflections from Each Character:**
- Serious: "Without structure, ideas disappear."
- Messy: "Without people, ideas suffocate."
- Narrator: "Without story, nobody remembers them."
- Main: "So entrepreneurship is not one place."

**Text Card (Field Note):**
```
The ecosystem is formal by day.
Informal by night.
Alive in between.
```

**The Seasoned Entrepreneur appears** quietly in background, still largely silent. Observing.

**Chapter Arc:** The question evolves from "which answer is right?" to "how do they connect?"

---

### CHAPTER 5: Closing Dinner — The Debate
**Duration:** ~0.75 minutes  
**Theme:** Different perspectives coming together

**Setting:** Closing dinner (Friday evening)

**Short Dialogue Fragments:**
- Serious: "Entrepreneurship needs discipline."
- Messy: "Entrepreneurship needs people."
- Narrator: "Entrepreneurship needs editing."
- Main: "Maybe entrepreneurship needs contradiction."

**Narrator:** "By the last dinner, the question had changed. It was no longer: which answer is right? It was: how do they connect?"

**The Seasoned Entrepreneur still present** but not yet speaking.

**Atmosphere:**
- Team laughing together
- Wine glasses
- Plates and food
- Real conversation
- Energy and warmth

**Chapter Arc:** Realization that both perspectives are necessary and complementary.

---

### CHAPTER 6: Final Morning Café — The Answer
**Duration:** ~1.5 minutes  
**Theme:** Integration, final wisdom, resolution

**Setting:** Saturday morning café (before departure)

**The Scene:**
Main character sits alone with coffee and her notebook. The notebook now shows her journey:
- "What is entrepreneurship?"
- "Entrepreneurship = structure?"
- "Entrepreneurship = people?"
- "Entrepreneurship = culture?"
- Final page blank (ready to be filled)

**The Seasoned Entrepreneur approaches and sits down.**

**FULL DIALOGUE:**

**SE:** "May I sit?"

**MC:** "You were everywhere."

**SE:** "So were you."

*Pause. He sits.*

**SE:** "What did you learn?"

**MC:** "That entrepreneurship is structure."

*Pause.*

**MC:** "And people."

*Pause.*

**MC:** "And culture."

*Pause.*

**MC:** "And money."

*Pause.*

**MC:** "And maybe confusion."

**SE:** "Good."

**MC:** "Good?"

**SE:** "Yes. People who are too serious forget that entrepreneurship is human. People who are too chaotic forget that entrepreneurship needs discipline."

*Pause.*

**SE:** "Do not choose only one version."

*Pause.*

**SE:** "Entrepreneurship is money, networking, creativity, structure, risk, failure, timing, culture, and trust."

*Pause.*

**SE:** "Innovation thrives when all of them coexist."

**MC:** "So entrepreneurship is an ecosystem."

**SE:** "Exactly."

*He stands to leave.*

**SE (final line):** "The serious part builds the company. The human part keeps it alive."

**Main character closes the notebook.**

**She writes on the final page:** "Entrepreneurship = connection."

## 8. Visual Style Guide

### Color Palette (Wes-Anderson-Inspired)

**Primary Colors:**
- Butter yellow (#F4D03F)
- Cream white (#F9F7F0)
- Powder blue (#B4D4FF)
- Dusty pink (#D4A5A5)
- Soft orange (#F5B041)

**Secondary Colors:**
- Muted wine red (#8B3A3A)
- Olive green (#6B8E23)
- Beige linen (#D4C8B0)
- Paris stone grey (#A9A9A9)

**Usage:**
- Use these in wardrobe choices
- Choose locations with these natural color palettes
- Adjust color grading in post-production to emphasize these tones
- Keep color palette consistent throughout video

### Camera & Framing

**Style:**
- Static, intentional shots (minimize movement)
- Centered subjects (symmetrical framing)
- Rule of thirds for some shots
- Long takes (let scenes breathe)
- Close-ups of details (coffee, notebook, hands)
- Wide establishing shots of spaces

**Technique:**
- Use tripod for stable shots
- Avoid excessive pans and zooms
- Quick cuts between scenes (1–3 seconds average)
- Hold on emotional moments
- Subtle camera moves when intentional

### Typography & Title Cards

**Chapter cards:**
- Clean, readable fonts
- White or black text depending on background
- Centered placement
- 2–3 seconds on screen
- Examples:
  - "What Is Entrepreneurship?"
  - "CHAPTER 1: Arrival"
  - "DEEP TECH: Science-based innovation..."

**Notebook text (visible close-ups):**
- Handwritten or hand-written style font
- Small but legible
- Document the journey:
  - "What is entrepreneurship?"
  - "Entrepreneurship = structure?"
  - "Entrepreneurship = people?"
  - "Entrepreneurship = culture?"
  - "Entrepreneurship = connection"

### Recurring Visual Motif: The Notebook

**The notebook appears:**
- Opening scene (blank or with question)
- Main character writing at various locations
- Close-ups of handwriting
- Pages turning
- Different stages of filling in
- Final scene (last page: "Entrepreneurship = connection")

**Purpose:**
- Visual continuity
- Shows main character's journey
- Concrete representation of learning
- Beautiful cinematic element
