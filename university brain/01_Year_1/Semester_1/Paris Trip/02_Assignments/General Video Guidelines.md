## PART 1: CHOOSING YOUR NARRATIVE APPROACH

### Option A: Character-Driven Narrative
**Use if:** Your team wants a scripted, theatrical approach with distinct roles

- Pros: Clear structure, each person has defined role, creative and engaging
- Cons: More complex to execute, requires good acting authenticity, more editing work
- **See:** Character Framework documents for full details

### Option B: Journey-Based Narrative
**Use if:** You want to follow the actual trip chronologically

- Structure: Day 1 → Day 5, capturing what actually happened
- Focus: Show the progression of understanding as it unfolded
- Each member appears naturally, not in assigned "roles"
- More documentary-style, less scripted

**Approach:**
- Each day has a theme (see Daily Schedule)
- Capture footage of actual activities, real reflections
- Show team learning in real-time
- Let understanding evolve naturally through footage progression

### Option C: Concept-Based Narrative
**Use if:** You want to organize around entrepreneurship concepts rather than time

- Structure: Deep Tech → Cultural Innovation → Infrastructure → Acceleration → Synthesis
- Each concept is illustrated with footage from relevant sites
- Jump between days as needed to show different examples
- More analytical/educational than story-driven

**Approach:**
- Each chapter focuses on one concept
- Use footage from multiple days/sites to illustrate it
- Team members explain/reflect on the concept
- Progress from simple concepts to integration

### Option D: Hybrid/Organic Narrative
**Use if:** You want to blend elements without a strict framework

- Mix journey-based (real chronology) with character moments
- Include concept explanations when relevant
- Let the story guide structure more than a predetermined outline
- More flexible, requires strong editing instinct

## PART 4: CONCEPT INTEGRATION (Learning Integration = 30% of Grade)

**You MUST clearly demonstrate understanding of these concepts:**

### Core Concepts to Address
- [ ] **Deep Tech** — Science-based innovation, long timelines, patient capital
- [ ] **Startup Hub** — Concentration of founders, investors, mentors enabling access
- [ ] **Accelerator** — Structured support program for scaling startups
- [ ] **Knowledge Transfer** — How universities/research becomes entrepreneurial action
- [ ] **Cultural/Creative Innovation** — Design, craft, brand as entrepreneurship
- [ ] **Social Capital** — Trust, networks, relationships enabling opportunity
- [ ] **Ecosystem** — How all these pieces work together as a system

### How to Show Understanding

**Option 1: Direct Explanation**
- Someone explains each concept clearly on camera
- Use simple language
- Ground it in what you actually saw
- Example: "Deep tech is science-based innovation. What we saw at HelloTomorrow is..."

**Option 2: Concept Cards/Text**
- Show text on screen defining the concept
- Back it up with footage illustrating it
- Include someone's voice explaining it
- Example: Text says "STARTUP HUB," then show Station F footage

**Option 3: Narrative Integration**
- Show the concept in action through footage
- Someone reflects on seeing it in practice
- Don't lecture; show and let viewers understand
- Example: Show relationship-building at dinner, then someone says "That's social capital"

**Option 4: Comparison**
- Show how the same concept plays out differently in different places
- Example: "Deep Tech requires patience. Cultural innovation requires brand building. Both are entrepreneurship"

**Key principle:** Viewers should leave the video understanding what you learned about entrepreneurship. Make it explicit, not implicit.

## PART 5: PRODUCTION STANDARDS

### Audio (Most Important)

**Critical:**
- [ ] All dialogue is clear and understandable
- [ ] No loud background noise
- [ ] No wind noise (if filming outside)
- [ ] Balanced levels (not too quiet, not too loud)

**How to achieve this:**
- Record important dialogue with external microphone
- Record in quieter environments when possible
- Use subtitles (makes dialogue clear even with imperfect audio)
- Test audio levels before filming
- Have backup recording of key statements

**If you can only improve one thing: fix the audio.**

---

### Video Quality

**Standards:**
- [ ] Resolution: 1080p minimum (Full HD)
- [ ] Frame rate: 24fps (cinematic) or 30fps
- [ ] Stable camera (tripod when possible, steady hand otherwise)
- [ ] Good lighting (use natural light when available)
- [ ] Consistent white balance (colors look right)

**How to achieve this:**
- Use tripod for any static shots
- Test camera settings before the trip
- Prioritize natural light (outdoors, near windows)
- Use phone stabilizer if available
- Avoid shaky, handheld footage

---

### Editing

**Standards:**
- [ ] Clean cuts (transitions feel intentional, not random)
- [ ] Appropriate pacing (not too fast, not too slow)
- [ ] Subtitles on all dialogue (accessibility + clarity)
- [ ] Professional color grading (consistent, warm, summery tone)
- [ ] Sound design (background music, sound effects appropriate)

**Rough timeline:**
- **First week after return:** Organize footage, rough assembly
- **Second week:** Add titles, subtitles, color grading
- **Before deadline:** Sound design, music, final edit to 5–7 min exactly

## PART 6: NARRATIVE STRUCTURE (Works for Any Approach)

- What question are you asking? (central question)
- What are you hoping to learn?

- Team arriving, breakfast on Day 1
- Brief intro of each person
- Setting: Paris, the hotel, first site

**Tone:** Curious, open, excited

---

### Act 2a: Formal Ecosystem (1.5–2 minutes)
**Goal:** Show what you learned from official visits

**Should include:**
- Hello Tomorrow (Deep Tech)
- Station F or Le19M/POUSH (Infrastructure or Culture)
- WILCO (Acceleration)
- One concept explanation per visit

**Footage to use:**
- Site visit footage
- Presenter explanations
- Team reactions and processing
- At least one person explaining what they learned

**Tone:** Intellectual, engaged, sometimes surprised

---

### Act 2b: Informal Ecosystem (1–1.5 minutes)
**Goal:** Show what you learned from being together

**Should include:**
- Café moments, meals, conversations
- Relationship building, team bonding
- Informal learning (things learned outside formal sessions)
- Someone reflecting on the value of informal time

**Footage to use:**
- Dinner scenes, bars, cafés
- Natural conversation
- Team laughing together
- Seine or Paris exploration
- Evening moments

**Tone:** Warm, human, relational

---

### Act 3: Integration & Wisdom (1–1.5 minutes)
**Goal:** Show what it all means together

**Should include:**
- Synthesis: how formal + informal fit together
- Key learning moments
- Evolution of thinking
- Final reflection on what entrepreneurship actually is

**Footage to use:**
- Closing dinner reflections
- Final morning conversation
- Final montage of images from the week
- Someone's final thought or wisdom

**Tone:** Thoughtful, integrated, wise

**Final moment should land:** Viewers should feel that the central question has been answered (or deepened meaningfully).

## PART 12: KEY PRINCIPLES (Regardless of Approach)

**Remember these, no matter what narrative you choose:**

### 1. Show, Don't Tell
- Don't say "we learned about entrepreneurship"
- Show us learning, reacting, understanding

### 2. Make Concepts Visible
- When you explain Deep Tech, show the HelloTomorrow building/presenter
- When you explain hubs, show Station F density
- Connect concept to image

### 3. Include Everyone
- If you have 5 people, all 5 must appear
- Give each person a moment that matters
- Don't let anyone be invisible

### 4. Let Your Real Learning Show
- Genuine moments beat perfect performances
- Your actual confusion is more interesting than polished certainty
- Real team dynamics are more engaging than scripted scenes

### 5. Respect the Viewer's Time
- Every shot should serve the story
- Cut ruthlessly
- No footage just because you filmed it
- 5–7 minutes is short; make every second count

### 6. Protect the Audio
- Bad audio kills good video
- Good audio salvages bad video
- Spend time on audio; you won't regret it

### 7. Build to Something
- Start with a question or hook
- Build understanding
- End with something earned
- Viewers should feel satisfied, not confused
