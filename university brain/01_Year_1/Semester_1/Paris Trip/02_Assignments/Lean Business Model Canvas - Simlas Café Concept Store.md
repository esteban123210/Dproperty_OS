# Lean Business Model Canvas
## Simlas's Multi-Space Concept Store: Slow Living Hub

**Core Mission:** Help people slow down through intentional, artsy, bohemian spaces that foster wellbeing, creativity, and connection.

---

## 1. PROBLEM

**Who has the problem?**
- Urban professionals (25–50 years old) experiencing burnout, overstimulation, and disconnection
- Creative individuals seeking authentic community and meaningful spaces
- Mindfulness practitioners and wellness professionals lacking accessible teaching spaces
- Conscious consumers wanting curated products that align with their values
- People caught in fast-paced city life who crave rituals of slowing down

**What is the problem?**

In cities like Paris:
- **Overstimulation culture:** Constant rushing, digital overwhelm, productivity obsession
- **Lack of intentional spaces:** Most commercial spaces are transactional and sterile
- **Fragmented wellness:** Yoga studios, cafés, retail shops exist separately—no integrated experience
- **Shallow community:** People consume but don't connect; spaces exist but don't belong to anyone
- **Wellness access:** Yoga teachers, mindfulness coaches, therapists lack affordable teaching spaces
- **Disconnection paradox:** More "connected" digitally, but isolated and anxious

**The core insight:**
> **People don't know how to slow down because there's nowhere intentional to do it.** Modern city life offers abundance of everything except space for pause, reflection, and genuine connection.

**Why does it matter?**
- Mental health crisis in urban areas (anxiety, burnout, loneliness)
- Growing wellness movement seeking authentic alternatives to corporate gyms/studios
- Conscious consumers want to support businesses with purpose, not just profit
- Creative professionals need hubs where they can teach, share, collaborate
- Cities need cultural anchors that heal, not just entertain or sell
- **Entrepreneurship can solve this:** by building systems that serve human wellbeing, not just extraction

---

## 2. SOLUTION

**What are you building?**

A **café + concept store hybrid** that:
- Serves as a community gathering space (coffee, pastries, seating)
- Functions as a curated retail experience (designed products, local makers, branded merchandise)
- Operates with transparent business structure (sustainable model, scalable operations, clear metrics)
- Maintains artistic integrity while proving profitability (integration, not compromise)

**The unique blend:**
```
Café
(ritual, warmth, belonging)
+
Concept Store
(design, curation, discovery)
+
Business Discipline
(structure, sustainability, scalability)
=
Cultural Anchor + Profitable Venture
```

**Core offering:**
- Premium coffee and seasonal pastries
- Curated selection of local design products, books, and merchandise
- Seating designed for both quick service and lingering
- Events: artist talks, workshops, product launches
- Community: regular customers feel ownership

---

## 3. KEY METRICS (How you'll know it's working)

### Revenue Metrics
- **Café revenue:** Avg. order value, daily customer count, repeat customer rate
- **Retail revenue:** Avg. product sale value, conversion rate (browsers to buyers)
- **Events revenue:** Ticket sales, sponsorship deals, private bookings
- **Target:** 70% café / 30% retail split in Year 1, evolving based on market

### Customer Metrics
- **Daily active customers:** Target 150–200 in Year 1
- **Repeat rate:** Target 40% of customers visit 2+ times per month
- **Average customer lifetime value:** Target €300–500 in Year 1
- **Customer acquisition cost:** Via word-of-mouth, social media, events
- **Community size:** Email list, social followers, regular attendees

### Operational Metrics
- **Margin on café:** Target 60–70% gross margin
- **Margin on retail:** Target 40–50% gross margin (wholesale + markup)
- **Foot traffic during peak hours:** Measure capacity utilization
- **Event attendance:** Track participation, satisfaction, repeat bookings
- **Social media engagement:** Follows, reach, mentions, tags

### Experience Metrics
- **Customer satisfaction:** NPS (Net Promoter Score), target 50+
- **Community sentiment:** Track what people say about the space
- **Product quality feedback:** Rating products and experiences
- **Staff retention:** Measure if team feels ownership of the concept

### Growth Metrics
- **Break-even timeline:** Target 18–24 months
- **Path to replication:** Can the model work in other cities/neighborhoods?
- **Scalability markers:** Can you license the concept to partners?

## 4. UNIQUE VALUE PROPOSITION (Why this, not Starbucks or a regular boutique?)

**Single sentence:**
A thoughtfully designed space where community and commerce coexist—where what you drink matters as much as the people you meet there.

**The three-part promise:**

1. **Beautiful by design** — Not accidentally aesthetic. Every element (from coffee cup placement to product lighting to music) is intentional. Customers notice details and feel the care.

2. **Rooted in relationships** — Not just transactions. You know the barista's name. Products are made by people you might meet. The space feels like "yours," not corporate.

3. **Honest economics** — Not hidden margins or unsustainable idealism. Transparent about how the business works. Profitable structure that proves beauty and business can coexist.

**Why customers choose you:**
- They want their morning coffee ritual to feel meaningful
- They appreciate discovering new products from local makers
- They want a space where they belong and can connect with like-minded people
- They value the story behind what they buy
- They want to support a business that reflects their values

**Why this model works (vs. alternatives):**

| Alternative | Why It Falls Short | How Simlas's Model Wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Regular café (Starbucks model)** | Transactional, no community, no uniqueness | Community first; becomes a destination |
| **Pure retail boutique** | No gathering space, pure commerce, lonely | Café creates natural gathering + ritual |
| **Subsidized art café** | Relies on grants, not sustainable, lacks business discipline | Profitable by design, not dependent on aid |
| **Chain concept stores** | Lacks authenticity, no local roots, high overhead | Deeply local, curated, lower operational bloat |

### Retention Channels
- **Loyalty (non-gamified):** Relationship-based regularity, not points systems
- **Email:** Weekly updates, new product features, event invitations

## 6. CUSTOMER SEGMENTS (Who are you serving?)

### Primary Segment: Creative Professionals (Age 25–45)
- **Profile:** Designers, architects, artists, makers, startup employees, writers
- **Location:** Paris (Le Marais, Belleville, 11th arr. initially)
- **Behavior:** Value authenticity, discovery, community; willing to pay premium
- **Needs:** Space for focus work, inspiration, connection, curated products
- **Size:** ~2,000–5,000 in target neighborhood; 400–600 monthly active users target

### Secondary Segment: Culture Enthusiasts (Age 20–60)
- **Profile:** Students, teachers, museum visitors, people seeking meaningful experiences
- **Behavior:** Follow cultural trends, attend events, support local makers
- **Needs:** Gathering space, educational events, stories behind products
- **Size:** Overlaps with creative professionals; events can draw new customers

### Emerging Segment: Tourists Seeking Authenticity (Age 25–65)
- **Profile:** Visitors seeking "real Paris," not tourist traps
- **Behavior:** Use social media to find authentic spots, willing to take time
- **Needs:** Instagram-worthy space, memorable experience, gifts with meaning
- **Size:** 10–15% of customers; seasonal strength

### Collaborators (not customers, but key segment):
- Local makers, artists, designers (product/content creators)
- Corporate clients (team events, product launches)
- Media/influencers (coverage, partnerships)

## 7. UNFAIR ADVANTAGE (What makes you hard to copy?)

1. **Founder authenticity:** Simlas's genuine vision and taste curates the space. Hard to replicate without the person.

2. **Community moat:** First mover advantage in a specific neighborhood creates network effects. People feel invested in the space's success.

3. **Taste + discipline combination:** Most creative spaces lack business rigor; most profitable cafés lack soul. Doing both well is rare.

4. **Local maker relationships:** Strong, personal connections with product suppliers and collaborators take time to build. Defensible.

5. **Detailed operational playbook:** Not just "beautiful café," but documented systems (supply chains, staff training, product curation, financial model) that make it scalable.

6. **Cultural positioning:** Becoming known as THE space where a specific creative community gathers. Defensible because it's about belonging, not just product.

7. **Sustainable economics from Day 1:** Profitable model means you don't panic under pressure and compromise the vision.

## 8. REVENUE STREAMS (How you make money)

### 1. **Café Operations** (Primary revenue, ~70%)
- Espresso-based drinks: €3.50–5.50
- Tea and alternative drinks: €2.50–4.50
- Pastries (sourced or made in-house): €2–5
- Lunch items (soups, salads, sandwiches): €8–12
- Target: €45–55 per customer visit average

**Unit economics:**
- Average order: €12–14
- Coffee gross margin: 65–70%
- Food gross margin: 55–65%
- Daily target: 180 customers × €12 avg = €2,160/day
- Monthly revenue: ~€65,000 (from café alone)

### 2. **Retail Sales** (Secondary revenue, ~25%)
- Curated products from local makers (apparel, books, home goods, stationery)
- Branded merchandise (café cups, bags, limited editions)
- Gift sets and seasonal collections
- Average order: €25–50
- Markup: 40–50% above wholesale
- Target: 30–50 retail transactions per day
- Monthly revenue: ~€23,000 (from retail)

### 3. **Events & Experiences** (~5%)
- Workshop/artist talk ticketing: €10–25 per person
- Private café bookings for teams/groups: €500–1,500
- Product launch events (commission from makers): Variable
- Corporate coffee catering: €300–800 per event
- Monthly revenue: ~€8,000 (from events)

### Pricing Strategy:
- **Coffee:** Premium (Paris 6–8, positioned above Starbucks but below high-end)
- **Retail:** Fair trade to makers (40–50% of retail price goes to producer)
- **Events:** Affordable but profitable (€12–25 workshop tickets, €800+ team bookings)

## 9. COST STRUCTURE (What it costs to run)

### Fixed Costs (Monthly, ~€12,000–15,000)
- **Rent:** €3,000–5,000 (prime location, ~100m², Marais/Belleville)
- **Utilities (electric, water, gas):** €400–600
- **Insurance + permits:** €300–400
- **POS system + tech:** €200–400
- **Accounting + legal:** €300–500
- **Marketing + social media:** €500–1,000
- **Equipment maintenance:** €200–300
- **Staff salaries (2 FTE barista + 1 FTE retail/events manager):** €6,000–8,000

### Variable Costs (per unit sold)
- **Café COGS:** 25–35% of revenue (coffee, milk, pastries, cups)
- **Retail COGS:** 50–60% of revenue (wholesale cost to makers)
- **Payment processing:** 2–3% of transactions

### One-Time Startup Costs
- **Build-out & design:** €25,000–40,000
- **Equipment (espresso machine, grinder, fridge, etc.):** €15,000–25,000
- **POS + tech setup:** €3,000–5,000
- **Initial inventory (coffee, products, supplies):** €10,000–15,000
- **Permits + licensing:** €2,000–4,000
- **Marketing launch:** €3,000–5,000
- ****Total startup:** €58,000–94,000 (target: €75,000 with 50/50 founder savings + angel investment)

### Financial Timeline
- **Month 1–6:** Ramp-up phase, negative cash flow
- **Month 7–12:** Break even month to profitability
- **Month 18–24:** Stable operations, ~€10,000/month profit
- **Year 3+:** Path to location 2 or licensing model

## 10. PROBLEM / SOLUTION FIT

**How do you know there's real demand?**

1. **Founder validation:** Simlas has personally researched Paris cafés and feels the gap
2. **Market research:** Paris has strong creative economy + high design consciousness + existing cultural café culture (as seen at Le19M, POUSH)
3. **Precedent:** Successful similar models exist (Merci in Paris, Kinfolk cafés, Café Kitsuné)
4. **Customer interviews:** Validate with target segment before launch

**Validation plan:**
- Pre-launch: 30 interviews with creative professionals
- Soft launch: Pop-up or limited hours to test model
- Early adopter program: Invite 100 "charter members" to co-create concept
- Measure: Daily foot traffic, repeat rates, retail attachment rate after first month

## 11. GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY

### Phase 1: Pre-Launch (Months 1–2)
- **Build:** Finalize location, design, systems, team
- **Community:** Host pop-ups, build email list, social media following
- **PR:** Features in design blogs, interviews about the concept
- **Target:** 1,000 email subscribers, 500 Instagram followers before opening

### Phase 2: Soft Launch (Month 3)
- Limited hours, friends + early adopters
- Gather feedback, refine operations, build word-of-mouth
- Document story (social media, behind-the-scenes, founder narrative)
- Target: 100 daily customers, 60% positive feedback

### Phase 3: Grand Launch (Month 4)
- Full operations, events, press coverage
- Community celebration, founder story prominence
- Target: 200+ daily customers by end of month

### Phase 4: Growth (Months 5–12)
- Build loyalty through community
- Expand product offerings based on customer feedback
- Plan events and collaborations (artist talks, product launches, workshops)
- Test licensing / franchising model with 1–2 potential partners
