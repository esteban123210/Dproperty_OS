## Preparation Tips

### Before Each Visit

1. **Read the guide** — Understand the organization's role and what to look for
2. **Prepare questions** — What do you want to know?
3. **Charge your phone** — You'll be filming and taking notes
4. **Bring your notebook** — Capture real-time observations
5. **Dress professionally** — Business casual for formal visits

### During Each Visit

1. **Listen actively** — Don't just record, engage
2. **Ask follow-up questions** — In Q&A sessions, ask *why*, not just *what*
3. **Observe the space** — How is the place designed? What does it signal?
4. **Watch people** — How do people interact? What's the culture?
5. **Take photos/video** — Document the visual evidence
6. **Jot notes** — Capture direct quotes and key observations

### After Each Visit

1. **Debrief immediately** — Discuss with your team while fresh
2. **Capture reflections** — Film short 1-minute team reactions
3. **Compare to Amsterdam** — How does this differ from what you know?
4. **Connect to concepts** — Which entrepreneurship concepts did you see?
5. **Update your notebook** — Add interpretation and significance

## Key Questions for Each Site

### Universal Questions

**For founders/staff:**
- What problem does your organization solve?
- What kinds of startups do you work with?
- What makes Paris special for your work?
- How is this different from what you see in other countries (e.g., Amsterdam)?
- What's the most common mistake founders make?
- What does success look like to you?

**For the physical space:**
- What does the architecture/design signal about the culture?
- Who are you trying to attract by being here?
- How does the physical space shape behavior?
- What surprises visitors about this place?

**For the ecosystem:**
- How do you connect to other organizations in the Paris ecosystem?
- What role does government/policy play here?
- How do informal relationships shape your work?
- What's missing from the ecosystem?

## Paris Context

**Current ecosystem narrative:**
Paris is positioning itself as a major European startup hub, rivaling Berlin and Amsterdam. The strategy includes:
- Large-scale infrastructure (Station F)
- Deep tech focus (Hello Tomorrow, École des Mines)
- Cultural and creative emphasis (Le19M, POUSH)
- Accelerator support (WILCO)
- Integration of formal and informal learning

**Paris vs. Amsterdam comparison:**
- Paris: More institutional, infrastructure-focused, cultural emphasis
- Amsterdam: More open, pragmatic, internationally accessible
