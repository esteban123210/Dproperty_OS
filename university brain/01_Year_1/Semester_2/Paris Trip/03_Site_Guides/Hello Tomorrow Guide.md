## Why This Organization Matters

### The Core Question
**What kind of entrepreneurship is NOT fast?**

Many startup narratives focus on speed:
- "Move fast and break things"
- "Iterate quickly"
- "Fail fast, learn fast"

But deep tech is different. It answers:
- **What if your startup needs 5–10 years to validate?**
- **What if your first customer is not a consumer?**
- **What if you need to prove scientific principles first?**

### Examples of Deep Tech
- Advanced materials
- Quantum computing
- Biotechnology
- Energy technology
- Space technology
- Advanced robotics
- Semiconductor innovation

### Why Paris Needs This
Deep tech requires:
- University connections for research
- Long-term patient capital
- Industrial partnerships
- Technical credibility
- Government support

Paris has these assets, so Hello Tomorrow helps unlock them.

## Key Concepts You'll Encounter

### 1. **Deep Tech**
The core concept.

**Definition:** Science-based startups that develop fundamentally new technologies over long time horizons.

**Characteristics:**
- Rooted in scientific research
- Long validation cycles (5–10+ years)
- Capital-intensive
- Technical risk (will it work?)
- Market risk (will anyone buy it?)
- Regulatory complexity

**How it differs from traditional startups:**
- Traditional startups: problem → solution → market → revenue (1–3 years)
- Deep tech: fundamental research → prototype → industrial partnership → market (5–15 years)

### 2. **Patient Capital**
The funding model.

**Definition:** Investors willing to fund startups for 10+ years with long validation cycles.

**Examples of patient capital:**
- University funds
- Government research grants
- Strategic corporate investment
- Family offices with long horizons
- Some venture capital firms specializing in deep tech

**Not patient capital:**
- Typical venture capital (expects exit in 5–7 years)
- Angel investors looking for quick returns
- Bank loans (need revenue)

### 3. **Research Commercialization**
The bridge from lab to market.

**What it means:** Taking scientific research and turning it into commercial products.

**Steps:**
1. Publish research (university)
2. Patent key findings
3. Spin out a company
4. Build a prototype
5. Test with industry partners
6. Commercialize

**Why it's important in Paris:**
- École des Mines produces significant research
- French government supports R&D
- Hello Tomorrow helps connect researchers to funding

### 4. **Long Validation Cycles**
The time it takes to prove something works.

**Traditional startups:**
- Validate idea with MVP (3–6 months)
- Get feedback from customers (weeks)
- Iterate (ongoing)

**Deep tech:**
- Validate scientific principle (months to years)
- Build industrial prototype (years)
- Test with partners (years)
- Achieve market readiness (years)

**Why this matters:**
- Founders need to stay committed for the long haul
- Investors need patience and understanding
- Success requires different metrics than traditional startups

### 5. **Ecosystem Support for Deep Tech**
What makes an ecosystem good for deep tech?

**Key factors:**
- Strong research institutions (universities)
- Patient capital (strategic investors, government)
- Industrial partnerships (large companies willing to invest)
- Infrastructure for manufacturing prototypes
- Regulatory expertise
- Technical talent
- International connections

## What to Look For During the Visit

### During the Presentation

**Listen for:**
1. **The founder journey** — How did this organization start?
2. **The problem** — What gap did they see in the Paris ecosystem?
3. **The solution** — How do they help deep tech founders?
4. **The portfolio** — What kinds of companies do they work with?
5. **The success stories** — How long did companies take? What are they doing now?
6. **The challenges** — What's hard about supporting deep tech?
7. **The future** — Where is deep tech going?

### Questions to Ask in Q&A

**Strategic:**
- "What percentage of your companies have university founders?"
- "What's the average time to market for your portfolio companies?"
- "How do you balance patient capital with traditional venture capital?"
- "What percentage fail, and why?"

**Comparative:**
- "How does the Paris deep tech ecosystem compare to other countries?"
- "What makes Paris special for deep tech?"
- "How does the Dutch approach deep tech differently?"
- "Are there cross-border opportunities between Paris and Amsterdam?"

**Practical:**
- "What's the biggest mistake deep tech founders make?"
- "How do you evaluate scientific credibility?"
- "What does success look like in your portfolio?"
- "How do you connect founders to industry partners?"

### Observe the Space

**Physical environment:**
- Is this a startup office or more of a research facility?
- What does the design communicate about the mission?
- Are there visible projects or prototypes?

**Culture signals:**
- How do people interact?
- Is it formal or casual?
- What's on the walls? (Science? Process? Companies?)
- What's the energy?

**Network:**
- Who's in the room with you? (Other founders? Investors? Journalists?)
- How do people introduce themselves?
- What conversations are happening in breaks?

## Key Takeaway

### The Central Insight

**Entrepreneurship is not one thing. It's adapted to the problem.**

If your problem requires:
- Fast iteration → lean startup methodology
- Patient capital → deep tech support
- Market testing → growth hacking
- Community building → cultural entrepreneurship

Hello Tomorrow is designed for founders whose innovation *cannot* be fast. And that's where a strong ecosystem becomes crucial.
