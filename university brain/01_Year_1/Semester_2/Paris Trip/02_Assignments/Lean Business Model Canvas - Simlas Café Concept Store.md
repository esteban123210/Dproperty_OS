# Lean Business Model Canvas
## Simlas's Multi-Space Concept Store: Slow Living Hub

**Core Mission:** Help people slow down through intentional, artsy, bohemian spaces that foster wellbeing, creativity, and connection.

---

## 1. PROBLEM

**Who has the problem?**
- Urban professionals (25–50 years old) experiencing burnout, overstimulation, and disconnection
- Creative individuals seeking authentic community and meaningful spaces
- Mindfulness practitioners and wellness professionals lacking accessible teaching spaces
- Conscious consumers wanting curated products that align with their values
- People caught in fast-paced city life who crave rituals of slowing down

**What is the problem?**

In cities like Paris:
- **Overstimulation culture:** Constant rushing, digital overwhelm, productivity obsession
- **Lack of intentional spaces:** Most commercial spaces are transactional and sterile
- **Fragmented wellness:** Yoga studios, cafés, retail shops exist separately—no integrated experience
- **Shallow community:** People consume but don't connect; spaces exist but don't belong to anyone
- **Wellness access:** Yoga teachers, mindfulness coaches, therapists lack affordable teaching spaces
- **Disconnection paradox:** More "connected" digitally, but isolated and anxious

**The core insight:**
> **People don't know how to slow down because there's nowhere intentional to do it.** Modern city life offers abundance of everything except space for pause, reflection, and genuine connection.

**Why does it matter?**
- Mental health crisis in urban areas (anxiety, burnout, loneliness)
- Growing wellness movement seeking authentic alternatives to corporate gyms/studios
- Conscious consumers want to support businesses with purpose, not just profit
- Creative professionals need hubs where they can teach, share, collaborate
- Cities need cultural anchors that heal, not just entertain or sell
- **Entrepreneurship can solve this:** by building systems that serve human wellbeing, not just extraction

---

## 2. SOLUTION

**What are you building?**

A **multi-space, slow-living concept store** (working title: "The Slowness Hub" or similar) that:

### Three Interconnected Spaces

**SPACE 1: Curated Retail ("The Store")**
- Intentionally selected products for slow living: ceramics, natural textiles, books on wellness/philosophy, plants, candles, natural skincare, journals, mindfulness tools
- Focus: quality over quantity, local makers, ethical/sustainable sourcing
- Aesthetic: artsy, bohemian, warm, inviting (not sterile boutique)
- Purpose: Conscious consumption that aligns with slowing down values
- Experience: Browsing is part of the ritual, not rushed checkout

**SPACE 2: Café ("The Pause")**
- Specialty coffee, herbal teas, slow-crafted drinks
- Light food: pastries, salads, nourishing bowls (made fresh)
- Seating designed for lingering: comfortable chairs, good lighting, quiet corners
- Aesthetic: artsy, bohemian, warm (mirrors the store)
- Purpose: Ritual space for morning coffee meditation, afternoon breaks, social connection
- Experience: Coffee as ceremony, not transaction

**SPACE 3: Rental Spaces ("The Studio")**
- 2–3 flexible rooms available for hourly/weekly rental
- For: Yoga classes, meditation circles, sound baths, tarot readings, therapy sessions, creative workshops, author talks, small gatherings
- Aesthetic: bohemian, sacred, intentional (different vibe from retail/café, but same philosophy)
- Purpose: Income stream + community gathering + service to wellness professionals
- Experience: Teachers + practitioners have affordable, beautiful space; community has access to wellness offerings

### The Holistic Experience

```
SLOW LIVING ECOSYSTEM

RETAIL STORE (curated products)
    ↓
CAFÉ (ritual, pause, community)
    ↓
RENTAL STUDIOS (teachings, practices, connection)
    ↓
INTEGRATED EXPERIENCE
(One place where people come to slow down,
discover, practice, and belong)
```

**The Bohemian Aesthetic (throughout all spaces):**
- Natural materials: wood, stone, clay, linen, plants
- Warm lighting: candles, soft lamps, daylight
- Artistic touches: local artwork, installations, rotating exhibitions
- Intentional curation: nothing random or corporate
- Lived-in warmth: feels like a friend's home, not a brand
- Sense of ritual: music, timing, attention to detail

**Core Philosophy:**
> This isn't a café with retail. It's not a yoga studio with a gift shop. **It's a sanctuary for slowing down, where retail, café, and practices all support the same mission: helping people reclaim their time and attention.**

**The Business Model Twist:**
- Traditional café/retail: extract money from transactions
- This model: create conditions where people naturally slow down, and they spend more time (and thoughtfully choose to support) because they feel held by the space
- Retail products, café revenue, and studio rentals are all secondary to the real value: being a place where slowness is possible

### 1. **CAFÉ OPERATIONS** (Target: 40% of revenue, ~€30k/month Year 1)

**Pricing (Premium, justified by experience):**
- Espresso-based drinks: €4.50–6.50 (vs. €3 at Starbucks)
- Specialty drinks (matcha lattes, herbal lattes): €5.50–7
- Loose-leaf tea: €3.50–5.50
- Pastries (local makers, ethical sourcing): €3–6
- Breakfast bowls, salads, seasonal specials: €9–14
- Lunch items (soups, sandwiches): €8–13

**Unit Economics:**
- Average order value: €15–18 (higher than chain cafés because people linger + upsell products)
- Gross margin: 65–70% (higher margin possible because focus is experience/community, not volume)
- Daily target: 100–120 café customers × €16 avg = €1,600–1,920/day
- Monthly revenue: €48,000–57,600 (conservative: €48k)

**Why customers pay premium:**
- Experience premium: Beautiful space, slow ritual, presence
- Quality premium: Ethical sourcing, real ingredients, care in preparation
- Community premium: They're supporting a mission, not just buying coffee
- Aesthetic premium: Visual beauty of product, packaging, presentation

**Cross-sell opportunities:**
- Coffee customer discovers tea at retail (€5 coffee → €25 tea purchase)
- Café customer sees yoga class flyer, takes class (€15 coffee → €15 class → regular practice)
- Regular café customer brings friend to evening event (€16 avg → €20 avg + event ticket)

### 3. **STUDIO RENTAL SPACES** (Target: 20% of revenue, ~€15k/month Year 1)

**Space Configuration:**
- **Room 1:** Yoga/movement studio (80 sq m, capacity 25 people)
  - Hourly rate: €40–60/hour
- **Room 2:** Therapy/consultation room (40 sq m, capacity 6–10 people)
  - Hourly rate: €30–40/hour
- **Room 3 (optional):** Event/workshop room (60 sq m, capacity 20 people)
  - Hourly rate: €35–50/hour

**Target Booking Model (Year 1):**
- Room 1: 5 classes/week × 60€ = €300/week + 2 private bookings/week × 50€ = €100/week = €1,600/month
- Room 2: 3 therapy sessions/day × 5 days × €35 = €2,625/month + 2 workshops/month × €100 = €2,825/month
- Room 3: 2 events/week × €50 = €400/week + 1 workshop/month = €900/month
- **Total monthly: €5,325 → €5,625 from 3 rooms**

**Wait, that's low. Let me recalculate with better occupancy:**
- Room 1: 8 hours/day booked avg × 5 days/week × €50/hour × 4.3 weeks = €8,600/month
- Room 2: 6 hours/day booked avg × 5 days/week × €35/hour × 4.3 weeks = €4,515/month
- Room 3: 4 hours/day booked avg × 5 days/week × €45/hour × 4.3 weeks = €3,870/month
- **Total monthly: ~€16,985 (round to €17k)**

**Actually, more realistic Year 1 (ramp-up):**
- Month 1–3: 30% booking capacity = ~€5k/month
- Month 4–9: 50% booking capacity = ~€8–10k/month
- Month 10–12: 70% booking capacity = ~€12–15k/month
- **Year 1 average: €10k/month**

**Teacher Pricing & Incentives:**
- Teachers set their own class price (€15–25 per student typical)
- Teachers keep 100% of ticket revenue
- Space rental: €30–50/hour depending on room
- Volume discount: Book 4+ classes/week → get €35/hour instead of €50
- This incentivizes regularity and builds community

**Revenue model features:**
- **High margin:** Once built out (€3–5k build cost), operational cost ~€500/month utilities/cleaning
- **Gross margin on studio rental: 75%+**
- **Community building:** Teachers promote space, bring students (free marketing)
- **Cross-sell:** Yoga students become café customers; studio generates foot traffic

**Studio + Café synergy:**
- Yoga class ends → students stay for post-class smoothie/tea (€15+ per person × 25 people = €375 revenue)
- Studio creates predictable foot traffic at set times
- Students discover products during transition between rooms
- Evening classes → dinner crowd at café
