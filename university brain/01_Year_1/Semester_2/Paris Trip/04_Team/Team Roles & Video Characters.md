# 🎬 Team Roles & Video Characters

## Team Formation Requirements

**Ideal team size:** 5 members  
**Composition:** Mix of profiles (not all one type)

### The Three Profiles

The professor's team formation criteria (Hacker / Hipster / Hustler) maps onto entrepreneurship archetypes.

| Profile | In Startups | In Your Video | Character Role |
|---------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Hacker** | Technical, analytical, problem-solving, systems thinking | The Serious Academic Entrepreneur | Explains structure, concepts, frameworks |
| **Hipster** | Design, culture, storytelling, creativity, user sensitivity | The Main Character / Artistic Vision | Asks questions, experiences Paris visually |
| **Hustler** | Networking, sales, people, opportunity, energy | The Messy Networking Entrepreneur | Connects people, shows informal side |
| **Seasoned Founder** | Balance of all above | The Seasoned Entrepreneur | Final synthesis, wisdom |
| **Connector** | Reflection, listening, synthesis | The Narrator | Ties the story together |

## The Five Character Archetypes

### 1. **The Narrator** 👁️
**Role:** Guide, reflection, synthesis  
**Purpose in video:** Ties the journey together, explains the learning

**Who this is:**
- Usually a team member comfortable speaking to camera
- Good listener and synthesizer
- Can articulate connections
- Reflects on what the team is learning

**What they do:**
- Opens the video
- Provides context between scenes
- Delivers learning hooks ("This moment taught us X")
- Closes the video with final wisdom
- May appear on camera or as voice-over

**Lines they might say:**
- "The professor gave us five profiles. We didn't realize it was our plot."
- "After deep tech, we understood: entrepreneurship isn't one thing."
- "Culture wasn't what we expected. It was more important than we thought."
- "By the end, we understood that entrepreneurship is an ecosystem."

**Speaking style:** Thoughtful, clear, accessible, occasionally surprised

### 2. **The Main Character** 🤔
**Role:** The learner, the evolving perspective  
**Purpose in video:** Guides the viewer through transformation

**Who this is:**
- Often the "hipster" — creative, sensitive to aesthetics
- Asks good questions
- Not afraid to show confusion or learning
- Visually interesting on camera

**What they do:**
- Arrives with a question: "What is entrepreneurship?"
- Visits each site with curiosity
- Visually explores Paris
- Evolves understanding through the trip
- Transforms from confused to integrated

**Visual style:**
- Carries a notebook (motif throughout)
- Takes notes in cafés
- Asks questions in Q&As
- Walks Paris streets
- Sits in conversations

**Lines they might say:**
- "I thought I knew what entrepreneurship was."
- "This is entrepreneurship?"
- "Wait, how does that connect?"
- "So it's not just the official visits..."

**Arc:** Beginner → Confused → Integrating → Transformed

### 3. **The Serious Academic Entrepreneur** 🎓
**Role:** The analyst, the framework thinker  
**Purpose in video:** Explains the formal ecosystem structure

**Who this is:**
- Often the "hacker" — analytical, systematic
- Likes concepts and frameworks
- Asks why things are designed as they are
- Comfortable with definitions

**What they do:**
- Explains what each visit means
- Defines concepts (Deep Tech, Startup Hub, Accelerator)
- Analyzes ecosystems critically
- Takes structured notes
- Sometimes frustrated by chaos or lack of framework

**Lines they might say:**
- "Deep tech means long-term innovation. Here's why that matters..."
- "Station F is a startup hub. Density creates access. Let me show you."
- "An accelerator is a structured support program. It works like this..."
- "The Paris ecosystem is institutional. Amsterdam is more pragmatic."

**Speaking style:** Clear, analytical, sometimes a bit dry but insightful

**Interaction with Messy Character:** The two often playfully debate. "That's not rigorous!" vs. "But that's how things actually work!"

### 4. **The Messy Networking Entrepreneur** 🤝
**Role:** The connector, the relationship builder  
**Purpose in video:** Shows the informal ecosystem

**Who this is:**
- Often the "hustler" — people person, connector
- Loves networking and relationships
- Skeptical of pure analysis
- Finds energy in human connection

**What they do:**
- Initiates conversations with strangers
- Shows networking moments (bars, dinners, coffee)
- Challenges the Serious character
- Shows trust and relationships matter
- Captures informal ecosystem

**Lines they might say:**
- "You can define all you want, but this is about people."
- "The real ecosystem happens after the official meeting ends."
- "Coffee here tastes better than the perfect framework."
- "Did you see the conversation at dinner? That's where the deal happens."

**Speaking style:** Enthusiastic, intuitive, people-focused

**Interaction with Serious Character:** "Yes, that's the theory. Now let me show you how it actually works."

### 5. **The Seasoned Entrepreneur** 🎯
**Role:** The synthesizer, the voice of experience  
**Purpose in video:** Final wisdom, coexistence of both sides

**Who this is:**
- Most experienced team member, or someone who takes this role
- May appear less in formal sessions
- Shows up increasingly as the trip progresses
- Speaks with authority and nuance

**What they do:**
- Appears in background during visits (observing)
- Shows up at informal moments (dinners, drinks)
- Delivers the final synthesis at the closing dinner
- Explains why both perspectives matter
- Brings resolution to the narrative

**The Final Scene (Closing Dinner or Final Breakfast):**
Main character sits with Seasoned Entrepreneur.

**Seasoned character says:**
- "I've seen startups built on structure alone. They're efficient but brittle."
- "I've seen startups built on relationships alone. They're energetic but chaotic."
- "The strongest startups I've known have both."
- "The serious part builds the company. The human part keeps it alive."
- "Entrepreneurship is an ecosystem. Not just a business model."

**Speaking style:** Wise, measured, integrative, slightly weary and experienced

## Psychological Safety & Team Culture

**Why this matters:**
Both assignments (brief and video) require honest reflection and vulnerability.

A weak team culture produces:
- Superficial observations
- No one willing to be "the confused character"
- Political disagreements about what to film
- Video that feels fake or forced

A strong team culture produces:
- Honest, real reflections
- Everyone comfortable appearing on camera
- Genuine learning journey
- Video that feels authentic

### How to Build Psychological Safety

✅ **Before the trip:**
- Discuss why you're each here
- Share what you're hoping to learn
- Talk about fears or uncertainties
- Agree on team values (honesty, curiosity, fun)

✅ **During the trip:**
- Laugh together (especially at awkward moments)
- Debrief honestly after each visit
- Ask each other real questions, not surface questions
- Give feedback with kindness
- It's okay to disagree

✅ **During filming:**
- Retake scenes if someone feels uncomfortable
- No one has to do anything that feels wrong
- Celebrate imperfect moments (often the best ones)
- Give compliments on performance

✅ **For your video:**
- Show real conversation, not just polished takes
- Include moments where someone says "I don't know"
- Show the Serious and Messy characters respectfully disagreeing
- Let people laugh and be imperfect

## Casting Your Team

**Hacker (Serious Academic Entrepreneur):**
- Who on your team is analytical?
- Who likes frameworks?
- Who asks "why is this structured this way?"

**Hipster (Main Character):**
- Who is creative and observant?
- Who sees details others miss?
- Who would be comfortable on camera?

**Hustler (Messy Networker):**
- Who loves talking to people?
- Who finds energy in chaos?
- Who bridges groups?

**Seasoned (Synthesizer):**
- Who has experience or maturity?
- Who can see both sides?
- Who delivers wisdom well?

**Connector (Narrator):**
- Who can articulate learning?
- Who's comfortable speaking to camera?
- Who ties things together?

**Note:** People can play overlapping roles. One person might be both Main Character and part of Narrator role.

## Role Responsibilities

### The Narrator
- Creates the opening narration (1–2 min of total video)
- Records bridge narration between scenes
- Delivers learning hooks ("This taught us X")
- Creates the closing narration

### The Main Character
- Appears on camera regularly throughout
- Asks questions in Q&A sessions
- Walks through Paris, takes photos
- Has visible note-taking moments
- Shows transformation throughout

### The Serious Character
- Explains concepts clearly and confidently
- Analyzes sites after visits
- Delivers learning definitions
- Appears in debrief moments
- Interacts with Messy character (friendly debate)

### The Messy Character
- Initiates informal moments
- Shows relationship-building
- Captures bar/restaurant/dinner scenes
- Challenges Serious character (playfully)
- Represents the human side

### The Seasoned Character
- Appears sparingly but meaningfully
- Shows up for final dinner/breakfast
- Delivers final synthesis
- May interview founders or mentors
- Represents integrated wisdom

## Shooting Guidelines by Character

### For The Main Character
**What to film:**
- Arriving at each site (first impression)
- Listening in sessions (thoughtful face)
- Asking questions
- Writing in notebook
- Walking through Paris
- Conversations with others
- Reactions to new information

### For The Serious Character
**What to film:**
- Taking structured notes
- Asking analytical questions
- Explaining concepts after visits
- Looking at buildings/infrastructure analytically
- Direct-to-camera explanation
- Interacting with speaker/presenter

### For The Messy Character
**What to film:**
- Chatting with strangers
- Coffee moments
- Opening drinks on Seine
- Dinner conversations
- Connecting different people
- Laughing moments
- Energy and movement

### For The Seasoned Character
**What to film:**
- Observing in background during visits
- One-on-one conversations
- Wisdom moments (fewer, more impactful)
- Final dinner scene (most important)
- Mentoring other characters

## Character Development Arc

### Day 1 (Tuesday) — Deep Tech
- **Main Character:** Confused by technical complexity
- **Serious Char:** Excited about frameworks
- **Messy Char:** Confused but engaged with people
- **Narrator:** "We arrived in Paris to learn about entrepreneurship"

### Day 2 (Wednesday) — Culture
- **Main Character:** Awakening to cultural value
- **Serious Char:** Trying to categorize craft-based innovation
- **Messy Char:** In their element (creative, relational)
- **Narrator:** "Then we learned innovation has many faces"

### Day 3 (Thursday) — Scale
- **Main Character:** Processing ecosystem concepts
- **Serious Char:** Analyzing infrastructure
- **Messy Char:** Seeing the network in action
- **Narrator:** "Station F showed us scale and density"

### Day 4 (Friday) — Acceleration
- **Main Character:** Starting to integrate
- **Serious Char:** Connecting frameworks
- **Messy Char:** Thriving in informal dinner
- **Narrator:** "The informal ecosystem became as important as the formal"

### Day 5 (Saturday) — Synthesis
- **Main Character:** Transformed, integrated
- **Serious Char:** Presenting integrated framework
- **Messy Char:** Celebrating the human journey
- **Seasoned Char:** Delivering final wisdom
- **Narrator:** "We learned that entrepreneurship is an ecosystem"

## Technical Notes for Filming

### Character-Specific Audio
- **Narrator:** Record separate voice-over
- **Main Character:** Capture on-camera dialogue and questions
- **Serious Character:** Record explanations separately if possible
- **Messy Character:** Capture ambient conversation + direct audio
- **Seasoned Character:** Record final speech separately for quality

### Subtitles
- All characters should have subtitles
- Makes the video more accessible
- Helps viewers follow the narrative

### Visual Continuity
- **Main Character:** Notebook as recurring visual motif
- **Serious Character:** Note-taking, analytical framing
- **Messy Character:** Movement, interaction, energy
- **Seasoned Character:** Reflection, background presence → forefront
- **Narrator:** Consistent location or framing device
